> **⚠ THE FRAMING RULE.**
> **This framework governs what happens when the company's governance stops working** — when the board or
> the shareholders cannot decide, when a shareholder wants out, when a shareholder dies or becomes
> incapacitated, or when the group needs to be reconstructed.
> **It cannot govern the Trust, and it cannot take away anyone's legal rights.** Where the Trust is a
> shareholder, **the Trustees' own duties and the trust deed govern how the Trust acts** (`D04`, `D09`).
> Where a family dispute is involved, `D17` applies. **And no clause in this document, in the Articles or
> in the shareholders' agreement can prevent a person from applying to a court.**
> **There is no deadlock now. Nothing in this document has been exercised.**

## 1. Purpose and the three cases this framework must handle

| Case | Situation | Why it must be planned **now** |
|---|---|---|
| **Case 1 — Governance deadlock** | The board or the shareholders cannot pass a decision (a class veto exercised; a 50/50 split; a director refusing to act; an unreachable shareholder) | Deadlock is the single most destructive event in a family company: it stops decisions, invites litigation, and can halt the business |
| **Case 2 — Exit and transfer** | A shareholder wants to realise value, or the family wants to restructure, or a shareholder must leave | Without a pre-agreed mechanism, an exit becomes litigation or a stalemate — usually at the worst time |
| **Case 3 — Succession, incapacity and control continuity** | A shareholder (or a key person) dies, becomes incapacitated, divorces, becomes insolvent, or exits | Control and value can move unintentionally; the enterprise can be paralysed at the worst moment (`D57`, `D58`, `D59`) |

**1.1 The design principle.** **Every mechanism in this framework must be: pre-agreed, objective, time-boxed,
evidenced, and workable in a downturn.** A mechanism that depends on goodwill, on the company having cash, or
on a person signing something they have refused to sign, **is not a mechanism** — it is a hope.

## 2. Case 1 — Deadlock

### 2.1 The escalation ladder (each step time-boxed; total `[USER TO SUPPLY]`)

| Step | Action | Who | Time-box | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Record the deadlock**: the matter, each position, the power in issue, the cost of delay | Company Secretary | Immediately | A written deadlock notice |
| 2 | **Board-level re-consideration**: the matter returns to the board with the information that was missing; the board records its reasons | Chair | `[NUMBER]` days | Board minute |
| 3 | **Shareholder-level consideration**: the matter goes to the members (or, where a member approval is the route, is put to them) | Chair / Secretary | `[NUMBER]` days | Meeting record |
| 4 | **Independent advice**: the parties jointly instruct `[counsel / an independent expert]` on the question in dispute, and **share the advice** | The parties | `[NUMBER]` days | The advice, circulated |
| 5 | **Structured mediation**: a mediator independent of the family and the company, under a written mediation agreement (`D17` §5) | Mediator | `[NUMBER]` days | Mediation agreement; settlement if any |
| 6 | **Ultimate resolution**: the agreed mechanism (§2.3) **or** the court/arbitration forum (§4) | Per the instrument | `[NUMBER]` days | Award / judgment / executed agreement |

**2.2 Hard rules during a deadlock.**

1. **The company keeps trading and keeps complying.** Filings, taxes, accounts, payroll, insurance and
   licences **continue**. **A deadlock is never a lawful reason to miss a filing or a payment.**
2. **No irreversible step** outside the ordinary course may be taken unilaterally (a disposal, a financing, a
   related-party contract, a share issue, a director appointment) except where a failure to act would cause
   serious or irreversible harm — **and then only under the emergency procedure in `D62`, with immediate
   reporting and ratification.**
3. **No "acting for" the company** beyond one's actual authority.
4. **No pressure on employees, customers, suppliers or the Foundation** (`D17` §7.4).
5. **Records are preserved** under legal hold (`D08` §8.1).
6. **Nobody may declare the company's governance "suspended."** Only a court can do anything resembling that.

### 2.3 The ultimate-resolution mechanisms (choose in advance — `[USER TO SUPPLY]`)

| Option | Mechanism | Pros | Risks / requirements |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Court** | The parties apply to the court for directions or a remedy | Certainty of a decision; the court protects minorities | Cost; delay; publicity; a relationship may not survive |
| **B — Arbitration** | Under an arbitration agreement | Privacy; a chosen expert tribunal | **`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` what is arbitrable** — construction of the Articles, class rights and any matter affecting a person who cannot consent are commonly **reserved to the court** |
| **C — Expert determination** | A narrow technical question (a valuation; an accounting treatment) is determined by an expert and (if agreed) **binding** | Fast; cheap; no publicity | Only for a **narrow technical** question; the parties must agree in writing that it binds them |
| **D — Buy-sell (buy-outs)** | One party buys the other out; a structured mechanism decides who buys and at what price (see §3.4) | Ends the deadlock definitively | **Requires cash or finance**; can produce an unfair outcome; the Trustees' participation raises fiduciary issues (`Q-H-04(d)`) |
| **E — Mandatory sale of the whole company** | If the deadlock persists beyond `[PERIOD]`, the whole business is sold and the proceeds distributed | Prevents indefinite paralysis | Destroys the family enterprise; must be a **last resort** and must be drafted as such |
| **F — "Deadlock put/tag"** | A party may require the company to be sold or its shares to be bought on a formula | Objective; avoids negotiation | The formula must be agreed and must be workable |

**2.4 The clause that must appear in the Articles and the agreement.** A statement that: **the deadlock
mechanism does not remove any party's statutory rights, and does not permit the company to breach any
obligation; and that the parties will continue to act in good faith and to preserve the business pending
resolution.**

## 3. Case 2 — Exit, transfer and buy-out

**3.1 The right questions to answer now.**

1. **Who may buy?** (the company, the other shareholders, an employee trust, an external buyer, the
   Foundation — **no**: the Foundation's funds are charitable and cannot be used to buy family shares,
   `D51`/`D52`).
2. **Who must sell?** (a shareholder whose employment ends; a shareholder who becomes insolvent; a
   shareholder who transfers outside the family; a shareholder who has breached the agreement).
3. **At what price, and determined how?** (a formula; an expert; a market test; a pre-agreed multiple).
4. **Paid how?** (cash; instalments with security; a note; an earn-out — **each has a tax and enforcement
   consequence**).
5. **What happens if the buyer has no money?** (a sale to a third party; a staged buy-out; a drag-along).
6. **What happens to the Class B votes on the exit?** (`D22` §4: the conversion triggers apply).

**3.2 Leaver provisions (if adopted).** A "leaver" is a shareholder who ceases to hold a defined
relationship (employment, family membership, a directorship). **Leaver provisions must be drafted with**
(a) a clear definition of the trigger event; (b) a price mechanism distinguishing "good leavers" (retirement,
ill health) from "bad leavers" (misconduct, breach), **and counsel's confirmation that a discounted "bad
leaver" price is enforceable and does not constitute an unlawful penalty**; (c) a payment period; (d) what
happens on death or incapacity (a distinct case — `D57`); and (e) the conversion of Class B shares.

**3.3 Drag-along, tag-along.** As `D23` clauses 5.7–5.8. **The family must confirm the drag-along threshold
and must reconcile the drag with the control objective the dual-class arrangement is meant to serve**
(otherwise the family has built a lock and handed out the key).

**3.4 Buy-sell mechanisms.** The three common structures, with the honest assessment:

| Structure | Mechanism | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Russian roulette** | One party names a price per share at which **either** may buy the other out; the other must choose to buy at that price or sell at it | **Risk of unfair outcomes**: it favours the party with more cash, and can force a well-financed party to take the whole company at a price set by the weaker party (or vice versa). **Requires a liquidity balance that a family may not have.** |
| **Texas shoot-out** | Both parties submit sealed bids; the higher bidder buys at the higher price | Fairer than roulette; still requires cash; can be gamed by a party who knows the asset's value better |
| **Sealed-bid / put-and-call** | One party has a put (right to sell) and the other a call (right to buy), both at a mechanism-determined price | Predictable; but the price mechanism must be genuinely independent |

**3.5 The critical constraint the pack must state.** **Every buy-sell mechanism assumes that a buyer can
fund the purchase.** Where the buyer is the **company**, statutory solvency and creditor protections apply
(`D24` §6). Where the buyer is the **Trust**, the Trustees must have the power, the liquidity and the
lawful reason (`D04`, `D07`) — **and the Trustees may lawfully conclude that the purchase is not in the
Beneficiaries' interests.** **The family must therefore design an exit that does not depend solely on any
one buyer.**

**3.6 Exit and the class-rights trap.** An exit that transfers Class B shares triggers **conversion**
(`D22` §4.2 T-1) — which is the point — but a family that has not thought about it may find that **the
control it protected has converted away** at the moment of the first partial exit. **Draft the conversion
triggers and the exit mechanism together.**

## 4. Dispute resolution

`D17` governs **family** disputes; `D09` governs **Trust** disputes; this section governs **company**
disputes. The ladder is: **board reconsideration → shareholders → independent advice → mediation → expert
determination (if agreed) → arbitration (if agreed and arbitrable) → court.**

**4.1 Matters better suited to the court** (`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`): the construction of the Articles or an
instrument; the validity of a class-rights variation; an unfair-prejudice/oppression claim; the removal of a
director; any matter affecting a minor, an unborn person or a person lacking capacity; and any matter where a
third party's rights are engaged.

**4.2 Funding a company dispute.** A dispute **about the company** is funded by the **company** where the
company is a party, with: a board decision recording the reasons and the expected cost; the disinterested
directors deciding where a director or a shareholder is a party; and the costs tracked separately. **A
personal dispute between shareholders is funded personally.** **Using company money to fund one
shareholder's personal dispute is a breach of duty and must be prevented.**

**4.3 Interim protection.** A party may apply to court for interim relief; **the parties must not treat the
filing of proceedings as a reason to stop the company's compliance obligations.**

## 5. Case 3 — Succession, incapacity and control continuity

**5.1 The events and the required response.**

| Event | What must happen | Where governed |
|---|---|---|
| **Death of a shareholder** | The shares devolve as the law and any will provide; the personal representatives are registered; the **Class B votes are suspended or converted** per the Articles (`D22` §6.4); the board is **not** paralysed (the surviving directors act; a vacancy is filled per the Articles) | `D57`, `D59`; `Q-S-05` |
| **Death or incapacity of the Founder** | The emergency and continuity provisions operate: `D56` (founder incapacity), `D58` (role succession), `D62` (crisis authority) | `D56`, `D58`, `D62` |
| **Incapacity of a director** | Ceases to act; the vacancy is filled per the Articles; **no software determination of incapacity** | `D03` §6.4 by analogy |
| **Incapacity of a Trustee** | `D03` §6 applies — evidence, declaration, replacement, notification | `D03` §6 |
| **Insolvency of a shareholder** | The shares may vest in a trustee-in-bankruptcy or be realised; **the Articles should address the consequence for Class B rights and pre-emption** | `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` |
| **Divorce of a shareholder** | Matrimonial property law may reach shares or their value; **take advice immediately**; the Articles' transfer restrictions and any pre-emption are the protections | `Q-S-04`; `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` |
| **A key person departs** | Business continuity per `D60`; role succession per `D58`; **the board must not be required to wait for a family decision** | `D58`, `D60` |
| **A shareholder becomes unreachable / refuses to act** | The deadlock mechanism (§2) and, for a required signature, the Articles' mechanism (or the court) | This document; `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` |

**5.2 The continuity requirements that must exist before any of these events.**

| # | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 1 | **A current will** for each shareholder, and the deposit/registration position confirmed (`Q-S-01`) |
| 2 | A **power of attorney** (and, if available in the applicable law, an instrument that survives incapacity) for each key person — **`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` whether an enduring/continuing form exists** (`Q-S-03`) |
| 3 | **Named financial and operational top-up verification** — the single most common operational failure in a family enterprise |
| 4 | An **emergency successor** named for every key role (`D58`) |
| 5 | **Bank mandates** that do not collapse on one person's absence (`D35`) |
| 6 | **Insurance** in force for key persons and for the business (`D71`) |
| 7 | A **written continuity plan** (`D60`) with a named owner and a test date |
| 8 | **Critical records** accessible to the successor (a documented vault and access protocol — `D74`) |
| 9 | A current **authority matrix** (`D27`) so that a successor knows what they may decide |
| 10 | Confirmation of what happens to **Class B** on each event (`D22` §4.2) |

**5.3 The rule that prevents the worst outcome.** **No company, Trust or group should ever be unable to act
because one person is unavailable.** If the answer to "who can sign this today?" is a single name, the
structure has a fatal weakness, and it must be fixed before anything else.
