> **⚠ THE BOARD'S DUTIES ARE NOT A MATTER OF THE FAMILY'S PREFERENCE.**
> Directors owe duties **to the company** — not to the founder, not to the family, not to the Trust, not to
> the group. **A charter cannot dilute those duties, and this one does not try.** Where the family wants an
> outcome, the route is the **shareholder** power (the Articles, a reserved matter, an appointment), **not a
> direction to a director to breach a duty.**
> **And the corollary that the family must accept: a director who follows a family instruction that harms
> the company may be personally liable — and the instruction is not a defence.**

## 1. Purpose and status

| # | Provision |
|---|---|
| 1.1 | This charter states how the board of each company in the structure will operate, what it decides, what it must not, and how it manages conflicts |
| 1.2 | **The charter binds the board only once adopted**; and it is always subject to the **Articles**, the **shareholders' agreement**, the **applicable company law** and **the directors' statutory duties** |
| 1.3 | Where the charter conflicts with the Articles or the law, **the law and the Articles prevail**, and the charter is corrected |
| 1.4 | **No AI** may be a director, an alternate, an attendee counted for quorum, or a decision-maker; AI may prepare papers, analyse and minute-draft for human adoption (`D02` cl. 7.4) |
| 1.5 | The board is **not** a family body. The family's governance runs through `D11`–`D19`; the company's runs here. **Where a person holds roles in both, they must separate them (`D18` §5.2 hat rule)** |

## 2. Composition

| # | Provision |
|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Number:** `[MIN]` to `[MAX]` directors `[USER TO SUPPLY]`; at least `[N]` must be **independent** where the board exceeds `[N]` members. **Eligibility and disqualification** are checked before appointment (`Q-H-03`) |
| 2.2 | **Independence** means: no employment by the group in the last `[N]` years; no material business relationship; not a family member of the founder; no cross-directorship that compromises judgement. **The board records the basis of each independence determination** — it is a recorded judgement, not a label |
| 2.3 | **Appointment** is a shareholder decision (H-01, `D27`) on the Nomination Committee's recommendation; the **registry filing follows immediately** (`D66`, `D68`) |
| 2.4 | **Recommendation:** at least one director who is **not a family member and not an employee**, whatever the size — the single cheapest protection a family board can buy |
| 2.5 | **Chair:** appointed by the board (or the shareholders, per the Articles); the chair sets the agenda, ensures the papers are adequate, and manages conflicts. **The chair's role is procedural and leadership-based, not an extra vote** unless the Articles say so |
| 2.6 | **Company Secretary:** attends all meetings; responsible for notice, papers, minutes, the register of decisions (`D73`) and the statutory filings. **The secretary has a standing duty to warn the board of an unlawful or irregular proposal** |
| 2.7 | **Term and rotation:** `[N]` years, with retirement by rotation `[USER TO SUPPLY]`. **A director's continuation is never automatic** |
| 2.8 | **Vacancies:** a vacancy that leaves the board below `[QUORUM]` must be filled within `[N]` days |
| 2.9 | **Removal and resignation:** per the Articles and the law; **resignation does not discharge liability for acts already done**; the registry filing is immediate |

## 3. Duties of a director (stated in plain words)

| # | Duty | What it means here | Where it bites hardest |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-01 | **Act within the constitution and the law** | Follow the Articles; do not exceed the powers; do not act on an invalid decision | Payments without authority |
| D-02 | **Promote the success of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole** | The **company's** interest, not the group's, not the family's | Any group-subsidy or cost-shifting question |
| D-03 | **Exercise independent judgement** | Not to be directed by the founder, the family, the Trust, a fellow director or a shareholder instruction | Where a family instruction conflicts with the company's interest |
| D-04 | **Exercise reasonable care, skill and diligence** | Read the papers; ask; get advice where needed; record dissent | Approving accounts or a solvency statement without understanding it |
| D-05 | **Avoid conflicts of interest** | Declare; recuse; do not use the position for personal benefit | Any related-party transaction (`D72`) |
| D-06 | **Do not accept benefits from third parties** | Includes commissions, rebates, hospitality above the policy limit | Procurement, advisers (`D36`) |
| D-07 | **Declare an interest in a proposed or existing transaction** | Into the register; before the discussion; and the disinterested decision | Family companies, the Foundation |
| D-08 | **Do not misuse company property, information or opportunity** | Includes corporate opportunities, customer lists, data, IP, and **the company's money** | A director's own business overlapping the company's |
| D-09 | **Consider the interests of employees, creditors, the environment and the community where the law requires** | Practically: **creditors' interests become paramount as insolvency approaches** | Trading while insolvent |
| D-10 | **Act honestly and in good faith** | Cannot be delegated, cannot be excused by advice, cannot be overridden by an instruction | — |
| D-11 | **Do not fetter discretion** by a blanket agreement (a "directed" director is a breach waiting to happen) | Relevant to a shareholders' agreement that purports to dictate votes | `D23` |
| D-12 | **Keep proper records and disclose** what the law requires | Registers; filings; the `D73` record | Late filings |

**3.1 The company's interest and the group's interest.** A subsidiary's board must act in **that company's**
interest. Group-wide benefit may be considered **only** where it is capable of benefiting the company itself
(a real business judgement the board must be able to articulate). **The charter forbids treating a group
instruction as a substitute for the board's own judgement.**
**3.2 The Trust's interest.** Where the Trust is the shareholder, the Trustees' interest is **the
beneficiaries'** — which is not the same as a particular director's preference or a family member's wish.
**A director who is also a Trustee wears one hat at a time** (`D14` §5.4).

## 4. Meetings, quorum and decisions

| # | Provision |
|---|---|
| 4.1 | **Regular meetings:** at least `[N]` per year (recommended: at least quarterly), plus ad hoc meetings on `[N]` directors' request or the CEO's request |
| 4.2 | **Notice and papers:** `[N]` days' notice with the agenda and the papers; **papers go to every director**, including any director representing a minority or the Trustees' perspective |
| 4.3 | **Quorum:** `[QUORUM]` directors, of whom at least `[INDEPENDENT QUORUM]` must be independent for `[MANDATORY MATTERS]`. **A conflicted director's presence does not count towards the quorum for that item** |
| 4.4 | **Decisions:** by majority; the chair has `[A CASTING VOTE / NO CASTING VOTE — USER TO DECIDE]`. **A decision requiring a shareholder approval under `D27` is not a decision until that approval exists** |
| 4.5 | **Written resolutions** are permitted per the Articles; the signature requirement is stated; **the same conflict rules apply** |
| 4.6 | **Delegation** is only under `D27` and `D28` AD-01; a delegation is recorded, time-limited and revocable |
| 4.7 | **Minutes** record: attendance; conflicts declared and the recusal; the papers before the meeting; the substance of the discussion where material; the decision; the dissent (on request); the resulting actions with owners and dates |
| 4.8 | **Circulation** of the minutes for approval at the next meeting; **approval does not permit retroactive alteration of the substance** — a correction is a new minute |
| 4.9 | **Where a director disagrees**, the dissent is minuted on request. **Dissent is protection for the director and information for the shareholders** — it must never be discouraged or penalised |
| 4.10 | **Notice of significant matters** (insolvency risk, litigation, a breach of the limits, a regulatory issue) is given to the board, the Trustees where the Trust is exposed, and the Audit and Risk Committee — **without waiting for the next meeting** |

## 5. The board's standing agenda and information rights

**5.1 Standing agenda items (every regular meeting).**

| # | Item |
|---|---|
| 1 | Conflicts and additions to the register of interests; **quorum confirmation** |
| 2 | Minutes of the previous meeting and the action list status |
| 3 | **Safety, compliance and regulatory matters** (no exceptions) |
| 4 | Financial performance vs budget; **cash, liquidity and solvency** |
| 5 | **Related-party transactions** in the period (`D72`) and the arm's-length position |
| 6 | Obligation and filing status (`D68`) — overdue items named |
| 7 | Risk register movements (`D61`), including any new critical risk |
| 8 | People: senior appointments, exits, disputes |
| 9 | Matters reserved for the board or requiring shareholder approval (`D27`) |
| 10 | Any director's request for a matter to be tabled |

**5.2 Information rights and the "no surprises" rule.** A director is entitled to: access to management; the
books and records; the advisers (with the board's consent, not unreasonably withheld); and the company's
information. **A director may not use that right for an improper purpose** — and where the purpose is
questionable, the matter goes to the board, and **the director's right is not extinguished merely because the
question is awkward.**

## 6. Conflicts of interest framework

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 6.1 | **Declare early and in writing** — the default is declaration at the start of every meeting, plus an immediate declaration when an interest arises mid-discussion |
| 6.2 | **The register of interests** (`D66`/`D73`) is maintained by the Company Secretary and reviewed at least annually |
| 6.3 | **Recusal:** the conflicted director must leave the discussion and not vote, **and not be counted for quorum** on that item — unless the Articles permit otherwise and the **disinterested** directors resolve otherwise, with reasons recorded |
| 6.4 | **No self-approval:** no director may approve their own remuneration, expenses, related-party transaction or a benefit to a connected person (`D15` §4.7, `D35` §4) |
| 6.5 | **The connected-persons test** is applied deliberately wide: family, spouse, dependants, entities they control or are a director of, and the Foundation/Trust where their role makes them interested |
| 6.6 | **Standing conflicts** (e.g. a director who is also a Trustee, or whose family are beneficiaries) are declared once, recorded, and handled under `D14` §5.4 — **but the specific transaction still requires the specific process** |
| 6.7 | **Where the majority is conflicted** on a matter, it is escalated to the **shareholders** (and where the Trust is the shareholder, the Trustees) — the board cannot decide it |
| 6.8 | **Where a conflict is unmanageable** (the director is on both sides of a transaction), **the transaction must be approved by the disinterested organ alone, or not proceed** |
| 6.9 | **A conflict that is concealed** is a disciplinary and, potentially, a legal matter — and it is handled as such, whatever the seniority |

## 7. Conflicts with the Trustee role (the recurring case)

**7.1** Where an individual is both a director of the holding company and a Trustee, the **orders of
priority** are: act in the relevant capacity; declare the interest in the other capacity; and, where the
transaction is between the Trust and the company, **the person must not decide on either side**.

**7.2** **The Trust–company double decision** is a structural feature, not a defect: the Trustees decide for
the beneficiaries; the directors decide for the company. **The pack requires both to be recorded for the same
transaction** (`D27` §8.3).

**7.3** Where the same person must give effect to both decisions, the minutes of each meeting must show the
capacity in which they acted.

## 8. Evaluation, removal and succession

| # | Provision |
|---|---|
| 8.1 | **Annual board evaluation**: the board assesses its own composition, skills, information quality, decision quality and conflicts; the outcome is minuted and the actions tracked |
| 8.2 | **Skills matrix** maintained; gaps identified and addressed by appointment (or by advice) |
| 8.3 | **Succession:** every key role (chair, CEO, CFO, secretary) has a documented successor or an interim plan (`D58`) |
| 8.4 | **Removal/retirement** is implemented immediately on the register, the mandate, the bank and the access (`D08` §8.3, `D66`) |
| 8.5 | **No indemnity** for dishonesty, fraud, wilful misconduct or a breach of duty; **insurance** is a board matter (`D71`) and does not cover what the law forbids covering |

## 9. The board's own controls

| # | Control |
|---|---|
| C-01 | **Two-signature rule** for payments and bank movements (`D35` §3) — **the board is not exempt** |
| C-02 | **No member of the board may authorise a payment to themselves** |
| C-03 | Related-party transactions are registered (`D72`) **within `[N]` days** of approval |
| C-04 | All reserved matters are recorded in `D73` with the instrument |
| C-05 | The board's papers are retained as records (`D74` for the instruments; the corporate record for the papers) |
| C-06 | **Confidential board material is not circulated to the family at large** — a director who leaks it breaches their duties |
| C-07 | **AI summarisation of board papers is permitted only on an approved service, with personal and privileged material excluded, and never as the record of the meeting** (`D70`, `D36` §4 S-04) |
