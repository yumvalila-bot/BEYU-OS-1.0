## 1. How this schedule is used

**1.1** It is designed to be inserted into **the articles of association** of the holding company (because
class rights that must bind the company and all shareholders must be **constitutive**), with a
**mirroring** clause-set in any shareholders' agreement (`D23`).

**1.2 The consistency rule.** Where a right appears in both documents, **the wording must be identical in
substance and the articles prevail in case of conflict.** Any difference is a defect and must be logged in
`CROSS_DOCUMENT_CONSISTENCY_REPORT.md`. **Drafting half the right in the articles and half in the agreement
is the single most common structural defect in family company documents.**

**1.3 The tagging convention.** `[M]` = a clause intended to reflect a mandatory legal requirement (**every
instance `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`**). `[P]` = a private contractual proposal. `[O]` = optional governance
practice with no legal force.

### 2.2 Equal economic rights `[P]`

**2.2.1** Subject to clause 2.2.2, each Class A share and each Class B share shall rank **pari passu** as
regards dividends and as regards any distribution of surplus on a winding up, **in each case per share**.

**2.2.2** Nothing in clause 2.2.1 shall be read to: (a) create a right to a dividend; (b) prevent the
company from lawfully applying its profits in accordance with the applicable law and the articles; or
(c) override any rule of law on the payment of dividends or on the reduction of capital.
**`[M]` `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`**

**2.2.3 The unequal-economics warning.** If the family ever decides that the classes should have **different
economic rights** (for example, a preference or a higher dividend for one class), that is a **different and
more complex arrangement**: it is a **transfer of value**, it raises valuation and tax questions, and it
must be separately advised. **This schedule assumes equal economics.**

## 3. Voting rights `[P]` `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`

**3.1 General meeting.** On a poll at a general meeting:

(a) each **Class A** share confers **one (1) vote**;
(b) each **Class B** share confers **`[1,000]`** votes;
(c) subject to clause 3.2 (protected matters) and to any separate class-consent right in §5.

**3.2 Protected matters — where votes may be counted equally `[P]` `[O]`.** The family **may** decide that
on certain matters the classes vote **without** the enhanced ratio (i.e. one vote per share), because
applying a 1,000:1 ratio to a matter that requires a broad consensus may be counter-productive. The family
must choose. Candidates for equal voting: `[amendment of the class rights themselves; a related-party
transaction above a threshold; the adoption or amendment of the reserved-matters list; the removal of an
auditor]`. **The family must decide whether to use this mechanism at all, and counsel must confirm it is
lawful.** `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`

**3.3 Show of hands / poll.** The articles should provide that a **poll may be demanded by any member
holding a stated proportion of the shares or of the votes**, so that the ratio actually operates rather
than being defeated by a show of hands. `[P]`

**3.4 Written resolutions `[P]` `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`.** The articles should state whether members may act
by written resolution, and whether the ratio applies to it. **A written-resolution mechanism that ignores
the classes is a defect.**

**3.5 The ratio is a right of the *class*, not of a person.** If a Class B share is transferred, converted
or cancelled, the votes travel (or cease) with the share, **not** with the holder — **unless** the articles
provide otherwise. **This must be stated expressly; ambiguity here is the most common source of disputes
about dual-class structures.** `[P]`

## 4. Conversion of Class B shares `[P]` `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`

**4.1 Voluntary conversion.** A holder of Class B shares may convert them into Class A **at any time**, on
a **one-for-one** basis (`[CONVERSION RATIO — TO BE CONFIRMED]`), by written notice to the company, with
the certificates surrendered and the register updated. **`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` the formalities, any capital
consequence, and any stamp or filing step.**

**4.2 Automatic conversion on a trigger `[P]` — the list must be chosen by the family and confirmed by
counsel.** The proposal is that Class B shares convert automatically into Class A shares on any of the
following, **each of which must be legally effective to do what it says** (`Q-H-01(g)`):

| # | Trigger | Notes and risks |
|---|---|---|
| T-1 | **Transfer** (other than to a permitted transferee such as the Trust or a wholly-owned family vehicle) | Requires a clear definition of "permitted transferee"; an ambiguous definition defeats the mechanism |
| T-2 | **Death** of a Class B holder | **The critical trigger.** Whether it operates depends on how the shares devolve; the articles must override any inconsistent transmission rule lawfully (`Q-S-05`) |
| T-3 | **Incapacity** of a Class B holder | Requires a workable definition of incapacity — **and a person must not be able to be "declared" incapacitated by a software flag or by a family vote** (`D03` §6.4 by analogy) |
| T-4 | **Insolvency** (adjudication, a scheme, an arrangement, an enforcement by a creditor against the shares) | Must be drafted to survive insolvency law; **`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` that the conversion is effective and does not prejudice creditors** |
| T-5 | **Ceasing to hold a defined family relationship** (e.g. on divorce) | Highly sensitive; requires the family's definition (`FIR-003`) and counsel's confirmation that it is lawful and not void |
| T-6 | **Ceasing to hold a defined office or role** | Must be objective and evidenced |
| T-7 | **Sunset date** | A fixed date, or the occurrence of a defined event |
| T-8 | **A specified proportion of the classes resolving to convert** | Requires class-meeting drafting |
| T-9 | **A stated proportion of Class B held by persons other than the permitted holders** | Prevents control passing to third parties incrementally |

**4.3 The conversion must be automatic and self-executing where possible.** A conversion that depends on a
person "agreeing" to convert, or on a board resolution that a conflicted board might decline, **does not
deliver the protection the family is buying.** `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`

**4.4 Effect of conversion.** On conversion: the shares become Class A; the register, the certificates and
any depositary or nominee record are updated; the company (or its secretary) must give effect to it
**without requiring the holder's cooperation**; and the position must be filed/recorded where the law
requires. `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM the filing position.]`

**4.5 No compensation claim on conversion.** The articles should state that conversion under clause 4.2 does
**not** entitle the holder to compensation, and that the holder is treated as having accepted the
conversion on becoming a Class B holder. **Whether such a clause is enforceable, and whether it could be
attacked as an unlawful deprivation, is a counsel question.** `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`

## 5. Class consent rights (vetoes) `[P]` `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`

**5.1 Proposed classes of consent.** The family may choose to give a class (or the Trust) a **consent right**
over defined matters. **A consent right is a different right from a voting right**, and it must be drafted
with precision. Proposed (family to choose):

| # | Matter requiring the consent of `[CLASS / THE TRUST]` | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| C-1 | Any amendment of the class rights attaching to that class | `[ ]` |
| C-2 | The creation or issue of any new class ranking ahead in voting or in economics | `[ ]` |
| C-3 | A **sale or transfer of control** of the company, or of substantially all its assets | `[ ]` |
| C-4 | A **winding up**, a scheme of arrangement, a merger, a demerger, or a reconstruction | `[ ]` |
| C-5 | A change to the **reserved matters** list (`D27`) | `[ ]` |
| C-6 | The appointment or removal of a **class-appointed director** | `[ ]` |
| C-7 | Any **related-party transaction** above `[THRESHOLD]` (see `D72`) | `[ ]` |
| C-8 | A change of the company's **accounting reference date**, auditor, or governing law | `[ ]` |
| C-9 | Any transaction that would result in the company ceasing to hold a **material subsidiary** | `[ ]` |
| C-10 | Any increase in the **authorised share capital** or change in the **nominal value** | `[ ]` |

**5.2 The shadow-director warning `[M]`.** A consent right that, in substance, gives a holder or the Trust
the ability to **direct** the company's management may cause that holder (or the Trust, or its trustees) to
be treated as a **shadow director**, with the duties and liabilities of a director. **This is a real and
frequently overlooked consequence.** Counsel must advise on how to structure consent rights so that they
operate as **shareholder protections**, not as management direction (`Q-H-03(b)`).

**5.3 Consent mechanics.** The articles must state: who gives the consent; in what form; the time period
within which it must be given or refused; what happens if it is not given within the period (does the
absence of consent **block** the matter — the safer default — or does it permit it?); and that a refusal
must be **reasoned** where the holder is the Trust (because the Trustees must record their reasons under
`D04`/`D08`).

**5.4 Consent and Trustee duties.** Where the Trust holds the consent right, **the Trustees must decide how
to exercise it in accordance with the Trust instrument and their duties** (`D02`, `D04` §3). The company's
documents must **not** attempt to bind the Trustees' decision, and a consent clause purporting to require
the Trustees to consent is **void as to that requirement** and must be re-drafted.

## 6. Transfer restrictions and pre-emption `[P]` `[M — the private-company restriction on transfer is a statutory characteristic as read]`

**6.1 Private-company restriction.** The articles shall restrict the right to transfer shares, as a private
company's articles are required to do (as read, in the private-company test). **The form and the permitted
formulation are a counsel question.** `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`

**6.2 Pre-emption on transfer.** A member wishing to transfer shares must first offer them to the other
members (or to the company, if the articles so provide) on the **same terms** as the proposed transfer,
with a stated acceptance period, and with a mechanism for determining the price if the terms are not
arm's length. **Every step must be time-boxed** so that a deadlock cannot block a legitimate exit for
years.

**6.3 Permitted transfers (no pre-emption).** Proposed: a transfer to the Trust; to a wholly-owned family
vehicle approved by the board; on death to the holder's personal representatives (see 6.4); and any
transfer required by law or a court order. **Each permitted category must be defined precisely**, because
"permitted transfers" are the standard route by which a dual-class structure is circumvented.

**6.4 Transmission on death.** The articles should state how shares devolve on the death of a member, how
the personal representatives are registered, and what happens to the **Class B** votes in the interim.
**The default proposal: Class B votes are suspended from death until the shares are registered in the name
of a permitted holder, and then convert unless the articles provide otherwise.** `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`
(`Q-S-05`)

**6.5 Pre-emption on issue.** Any new issue of shares must be offered to existing members **pro rata**, with
a stated acceptance period, **unless** the members resolve otherwise (by the required majority and class
consents) for a specific issue (for example, an ESOP pool — `D24`).

**6.6 Anti-dilution.** The proposal is that the classes are protected against **control dilution** by
pre-emption (6.5) plus a class consent for any issue that would materially alter the voting balance
(`C-2`). **A "full ratchet" or weighted-average anti-dilution formula is not proposed here** — it is a
private-equity mechanism, it is complex, and it requires separate advice.

## 8. Board, appointment and removal rights `[P]`

**8.1** The family may choose: Class B appoints `[NUMBER]` directors; the Trust appoints `[NUMBER]`
directors; Class A appoints `[NUMBER]`; and `[NUMBER]` are independent. **Or no class appointment rights at
all.** The family must decide (`DC-01` D-7).

**8.2** If adopted: the appointment right must be exercised by a **written notice** to the company, and the
appointed person must still satisfy any statutory eligibility requirement and any requirement of the
articles. **The right appoints; it does not decide anything else.**

**8.3** A class-appointed director **owes duties to the company**, not to the appointing class. **The
articles and the shareholders' agreement must say so expressly**, to prevent a class from treating "its"
director as a delegate. `[M]` `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`

**8.4** Removal: how a class-appointed director may be removed, and whether the class may remove its own
appointee. **The statutory removal right (if any) cannot be contracted away** — `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`.

## 9. What this schedule does NOT do `[M]`

**No clause in this schedule shall be read, construed or applied so as to:**

1. **Defeat mandatory law**, a licence condition or a regulator's requirement.
2. **Defeat a creditor** or a court order, or operate to prejudice creditors on insolvency.
3. **Remove, reduce or override** any **statutory minority protection**, including the statutory remedy for
   unfair prejudice/oppression, the statutory right (if any) of a dissentient minority to apply to court on
   a variation of class rights (as read: a dissentient group may petition within a short period, with the
   variation requiring court confirmation), any statutory pre-emption rule, and any statutory right to
   information, to requisition a meeting, or to require an audit.
4. **Exculpate** a director from a breach of duty, or protect a person who acts on an unlawful instruction.
5. **Direct the Trustees** of the Trust in the exercise of any right the Trust holds.
6. **Bind a person who is not a party** to the document in which the clause appears, except where the
   constitution of a company binds members by law.
7. **Create, or be read as creating**, any share, class, right, office or holding.
8. **Represent the arrangement as lawful, adopted or in force.**
