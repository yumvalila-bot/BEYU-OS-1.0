Document ID: `BEYU-LF-EMP-04`
Title: Bilingual Glossary and Language Control for Employment and Related Documents (English / Kiswahili)
Version: 1.0 (draft) · Date 2026-10-15
Status: **`PRELIMINARY_FRAMEWORK`** — **a working glossary. NOT counsel-reviewed, NOT certified, and NOT a
translation of any executed instrument.**
Owner (proposed): HR function with the Company Secretary function · Approving authority (proposed): the board,
with counsel confirming the language rules
Next review date: **Every six months**, and on any new term used in a document
Classification: `CONFIDENTIAL`
Related: `EMP-01`, `EMP-02`, `EMP-03`, `D15`, `DEFINED_TERMS`, `PRIV-01`, `D74`
Depends on: **counsel on the governing-language question and on whether any translation must be certified**

> **⚠ THE LANGUAGE CONTROL — WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS.**
> **1. A translation is a legal document.** If a worker signs a Kiswahili contract and the group relies on the
> English version, or wishes it had, the divergence becomes the dispute. **The glossary's purpose is to make
> the two texts say the same thing — and to make the divergence visible where it does not.**
> **2. This glossary is NOT certified and NOT reviewed.** Every entry is a working term for drafting and
> training. **A term used in an instrument must be settled by counsel and, where required, by a certified
> translator.** The glossary holds the **consistency**; it does not hold the authority.
> **3. The governing language must be decided, and stated in the document.** That is a legal question
> (`§5`), not a preference.
> **4. Where a Kiswahili term does not exist for a legal concept (a trust, a protector, a shareholder's
> agreement, an option), do NOT invent one.** Use the English term, define it in Kiswahili, and mark it as a
> **borrowed term** — which is what this glossary does.

## 2. Rules for using the glossary

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 2.1 | **One concept, one term.** If a document introduces a new rendering of a term already in this glossary, the glossary is updated **first**, or the document is wrong |
| 2.2 | **Borrowed terms are marked as borrowed** and are defined at first use in the document |
| 2.3 | **Never translate a statutory term loosely.** Where the law has a term, the **statutory term is used** and its source is recorded |
| 2.4 | **Numbers, dates, amounts and names are not translated**; they are reproduced identically in both texts |
| 2.5 | **Currency and amount format** is written identically (e.g. `TZS 1,000,000`) in both texts |
| 2.6 | **Both texts have the same clause numbering.** If a clause exists in one, it exists in the other, in the same position |
| 2.7 | **A divergence is not resolved by preference; it is resolved by correction** and recorded in the version log (§4) |
| 2.8 | **The glossary is maintained**: each addition is dated, and the reason for the addition recorded |
| 2.9 | **The glossary is not evidence of a legal meaning.** It records drafting consistency |

## 5. The governing-language question (the decision counsel must make)

| # | Option | Consequence | `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **English governs**, with a Kiswahili courtesy translation | The worker may not fully understand the binding text — a fairness and enforceability risk, and in some systems a defect | Is a translation required by law? Is a mis-stated term enforceable? |
| 5.2 | **Kiswahili governs**, with an English translation | The group's own understanding of its documents is now a translation | Same |
| 5.3 | **Both texts govern**, with a conflict rule | Requires the two texts to be reconciled **before** signature; a conflict clause that resolves in the group's favour may be unfair and, in some systems, ineffective | Is a conflict clause valid? |
| 5.4 | **The language the worker understands governs**, with the other as a reference | Strongest fairness position; requires a real language assessment | How is the assessment recorded? |
| 5.5 | **English governs, and the key terms are explained orally in Kiswahili and recorded** | A practical middle route — but the **record** is what makes it useful | Would an oral explanation need to be witnessed? |

**5.6 The pack's default recommendation (subject to counsel).** **Prepare both texts; reconcile them clause by
clause; state the governing language expressly in the document; give the worker both texts; and record the
acknowledgment.** **Never ask a worker to sign a text they cannot read**, and never rely on a translation the
group has not reconciled.

## 6. Decisions required from the user / family / governing bodies

1. Whether the group will issue **bilingual documents as standard** (recommended for employment documents).
2. **Which language governs**, per document type (`§5`).
3. Whether to engage a **certified translator**, and who maintains the glossary.
4. Whether the **handbook** is issued bilingually in full or with a bilingual summary of the key policies.
5. The **review cycle** for the glossary.
