> **⚠ THE HANDBOOK RULES.**
> **1. A policy the group cannot operate is worse than no policy.** It creates an expectation, a standard and,
> in some systems, a contractual obligation — and then the group breaches it. **Every policy in this handbook
> names who operates it, how, and where the record goes.**
> **2. A policy is not law.** Where a policy conflicts with the law or with a contract, **the law and the
> contract prevail**; the policy is corrected.
> **3. Policies are `[PROPOSED]` until adopted.** Nothing here is adopted. Adoption requires a board decision
> and communication to workers, **and the acknowledgment records are the evidence** (`EMP-01` §4.20).
> **4. `D15` is not repeated.** The family-employment policy and its 15-item contract checklist are
> **incorporated by reference** so that the two documents cannot diverge. **Where this handbook and `D15`
> conflict, the conflict is corrected in one place and recorded in `CROSS_DOCUMENT_CONSISTENCY_REPORT.md`.**
> **5. Nothing in a policy may move a non-transferable right** (`EMP-01` control 4).

## 1. How to use this handbook

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1.1 | **One handbook per employing entity per jurisdiction**, with a **core** (this document's Part 1) and **entity schedules** (Part 3) |
| 1.2 | Policies are **numbered** so that they can be cited in a disciplinary or grievance file |
| 1.3 | **Communication is evidenced**: a signed acknowledgment per worker, an accessible copy, and a record of each revision issued (`D74`) |
| 1.4 | **Revisions are versioned** with a date, and §6 tracks them. A policy silently edited is a policy nobody can rely on |
| 1.5 | **The handbook is not the contract.** Where a term is contractual, it belongs in `EMP-01`; where it is a policy, it belongs here. **The distinction must be stated in the handbook itself** |
| 1.6 | **Language**: each policy is prepared in English and Kiswahili (`EMP-04` §4), with the governing-language question settled by counsel |

## 4. The policies that must never be adopted as drafted (hazard list)

| # | Hazard | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | A **monitoring** policy without transparency, or one that permits reading private communications | Unlawful monitoring creates data-protection and employment exposure `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` |
| 4.2 | A **"no discussion of pay"** policy | In some systems such a clause is unlawful; and it obstructs equality enforcement |
| 4.3 | A **blanket "no outside work"** policy | May breach a lawful secondary occupation right; use the conflict/moonlighting standard instead |
| 4.4 | A **retaliation** rule that is silent on protected reports | `P-08`, `P-09` and `NDA-01` §3.18 must all preserve the right to report |
| 4.5 | A **"company owns everything you create"** clause in a policy | An IP clause must be in the **contract/assignment** (`D41`), narrowly drafted, and lawful — not in a handbook |
| 4.6 | A **deduction-from-wages** policy without the law's authority | Unlawful deductions are a serious exposure `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` |
| 4.7 | A **zero-tolerance** policy the group has no capacity to operate | Creates unachievable standards and inconsistent enforcement |
| 4.8 | A policy that **treats family members differently** — either in their favour or against them | Both are defects: preferential treatment is a conflict; adverse treatment is a dispute |
| 4.9 | An **AI-use policy** that is silent | Workers will use AI tools; the policy must address **confidential and personal data, and the accuracy of outputs** (`D45` §7; `PRIV-01` §9) |
| 4.10 | A policy that **promises absolute security, absolute confidentiality or absolute compliance** | The group cannot deliver absolutes, and the promise becomes the liability |

## 5. Volunteers, interns and Foundation workers (the boundary)

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 5.1 | **A volunteer is not a way to get free labour for a commercial entity.** Where a person does a job's work under the group's direction, they are likely a worker, **whatever the document says** — and a nonprofit must not be used to staff a commercial group |
| 5.2 | Internships must be **a real programme** with learning objectives, a supervisor, a defined duration, and no substitution for a funded post |
| 5.3 | **Foundation-funded roles** are employed by the Foundation (or by an entity the Foundation contracts with), on terms consistent with its nonprofit obligations (`D48`–`D55`) |
| 5.4 | **Any arrangement where a person works without pay** requires counsel's advice **before it starts**, and the reason is recorded |
