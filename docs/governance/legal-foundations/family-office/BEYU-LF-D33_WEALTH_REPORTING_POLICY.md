> **⚠ THE THREE RULES THAT GOVERN EVERY NUMBER IN THIS PACK.**
> **1. Never mix owners.** Trust assets, company assets, Foundation assets, Family Office operating funds and
> a family member's personal assets are **different owners' money**. **A single "family wealth" total that
> adds them together is prohibited** (`D07` §7.2) — it misstates every one of them and creates the false
> belief that money is available where it legally is not.
> **2. Never mix realised and contingent.** Insurance cover, an expected inheritance, an unrealised gain, a
> contingent liability and a proposed transaction are **not** assets or liabilities. They are disclosed
> **separately**, or not at all.
> **3. Reconcile or label.** Any figure that has not been reconciled to the accounting authority is marked
> **`UNRECONCILED`** and excluded from decision totals.

## 1. Purpose and scope

**1.1** To define how the family's and the group's financial information is consolidated, reported, budgeted
and controlled: who owns each report, what it may contain, what it must never contain, who receives it, and
how it is protected.

**1.2 Scope.** Applies to the Trust (`D08` governs the Trust's own accounts — this policy governs the
**reporting layer**), each group company, the Foundation (its own regime, `D55`), the Family Office's own
costs, and the family's own budget.

## 3. Consolidation rules

**3.1 Owner separation (absolute).** Every figure is attributed to an owner and shown separately.
Consolidation is permitted **only** within a single owner's perimeter, and where the group's accounting
framework requires it.

**3.2 Three permitted presentations.**

| Presentation | Permitted when | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Single-owner report** | Always | The owner is identified on every page |
| **Group consolidation** | The group has a legitimate consolidated accounting requirement | The perimeter is stated; the Foundation is **excluded**; the Trust is **excluded** unless it holds group assets directly |
| **Family overview (narrative)** | For family communication | **No summing.** Presented as separately-labelled blocks per owner, each with its own basis, and with the beneficial ownership of each block stated. **A total across owners is prohibited** |

**3.3 The "whose money is it?" statement.** Every report that will be read by a family member must contain a
plain statement of: **whose money is being reported; on what legal basis that owner holds it; who can
lawfully decide what happens to it; and what the family member's own position is** (which is, in the Trust's
case, **nothing** beyond the Trustees' discretion — `D11` §5.2).

**3.4 Prohibited consolidation practices.** (a) Adding Trust, company, Foundation and personal assets into
one figure; (b) netting a contingent benefit against a real liability; (c) presenting an unrealised
valuation gain as available money; (d) presenting projected income as assets; (e) presenting a group total
that includes a subsidiary the group does not own; (f) including an entity that does not exist (including
any seeded or demonstration entity).

## 4. Budgeting and forecasting

**4.1 Budget owner.** Each owner produces its own budget: the Trust's budget is the **Trustees'** (a spending
plan for the Trust); each company's is its **board's**; the Foundation's is its **governing body's**; the
family's own is the **Assembly's** (administered by the Family Office).

**4.2 Budget discipline.** (a) A budget requires an approving decision with reasons; (b) any assumption that
materially drives the budget must be stated; (c) a variance above `[USER TO SUPPLY]` must be reported with an
explanation and a corrective action; (d) **a budget is not an authority to spend** — the authority comes from
`D27`'s limits and the delegations; (e) an overspend must be escalated, not absorbed.

**4.3 Forecast honesty rule.** Every forecast must state: the basis of each major line; the range of
plausible outcomes (a **single-point** forecast presented as likely is not acceptable for a material
decision); and the cash effect. **A forecast must never be described as a commitment or a certainty.**

**4.4 The "faithful pessimism" requirement.** For liquidity purposes, the Family Office must prepare a
**downside** case, not only a central case: what happens if the main income source falls by
`[PERCENTAGE]`, if a receivable is not collected, and if a facility is withdrawn. **For a family structure
whose main asset is illiquid equity, this is the single most valuable analysis the Family Office produces.**

## 5. Liquidity and solvency monitoring

**5.1 Liquidity floor.** The floor (L-03, `D07` §3) is `[USER TO SUPPLY]`. **A breach must be reported
immediately, in writing, to the owner's governing body and (for the Trust) the Protector, with a remediation
plan.**

**5.2 Solvency monitoring (company level).** Each board must receive a monthly solvency indicator: cash and
committed facilities against the next `[NUMBER]` months' obligations; the ageing of receivables; the
covenant position; and any contingent liability that could crystallise. **A board that does not know its
solvency position cannot discharge its duty to creditors.**

**5.3 The "cannot pay" trigger.** Where a board or a Trustee concludes that the entity may be unable to pay
its debts as they fall due, the correct sequence is: (a) record the conclusion immediately; (b) obtain
professional advice at once (**this may be required by law, and a failure to act may expose the directors
personally**); (c) convene the governing body; (d) take lawful steps to preserve value for creditors; and
(e) **do not trade on, do not pay a related party, and do not make a distribution.** `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`
the statutory duties of a director and of a trustee in that position.

**5.4 Trust liquidity and the distribution interaction.** A Trustee decision to make a Distribution must
consider the Trust's remaining liquidity (`D05` §4.1 step 7). **A distribution that leaves the Trust unable
to meet its own liabilities is not permitted** (`D02` cl. 10.4(a)).

## 6. Investment governance interface

This policy governs **reporting on** investments. **The mandates, limits and approvals are in `D07` (Trust)
and `D34` (group and family investment framework).** The reporting obligations are: performance **against the
mandate**; fee disclosure; limit compliance; valuation basis and date; and the disclosure of any departure
from the mandate.

## 7. Controls, access and retention

| Control | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Reconciliation | Every report reconciles to the accounting authority; unreconciled figures are labelled and excluded from decision totals |
| Preparation vs approval | The preparer and the approver of a material report are different people |
| Source | Every material figure in a report has a source document or a system reference |
| Version control | Reports are versioned; a superseded report is marked and retained (never quietly replaced) |
| Access | `HIGHLY_RESTRICTED`; per-owner separation of access; a family member does not see another beneficiary's detail (`D19` §5.1) |
| Distribution | Only to the owner and to those the owner authorises; **never to a group chat** (`D19` §2.1) |
| Retention | Per `D08` §8 and, where unconfirmed, retain |
| Legal hold | On any dispute, investigation or audit, no report is deleted (`D08` §8.1) |

**7.1 The correction rule.** An error in a report is corrected by issuing a **corrected version** that states
what was wrong — **never** by deleting the original, silently editing it, or "re-issuing" without a note.
**A silent correction of a financial report is a serious breach** (`D08` §8).
