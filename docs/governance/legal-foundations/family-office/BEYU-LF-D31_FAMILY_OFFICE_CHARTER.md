> **⚠ THE FIVE BOUNDARIES (the whole charter in one box).**
> **1. The Family Office is not a legal authority.** It is a **shared capability** — a function. It may
> exist inside an existing company, as its own service company, or as an unincorporated team. **Its legal
> form is a separate decision (`DAA-FO-001`) and this charter presumes none.**
> **2. It owns nothing.** Not Trust assets, not company assets, not Foundation assets, not family members'
> personal assets. Where it holds records, it holds them as **agent/administrator for the body that owns
> them**. **Custody is not ownership.** (`DAA-FO-002`.)
> **3. It decides nothing that belongs to a fiduciary body.** It does not decide a distribution, a company
> resolution, a share issue, a grant, an investment outside a mandate, or a related-party transaction.
> **4. It acts only under a written mandate**, with scope, limits, reporting and revocation (`D35`).
> **5. No software or AI output from it has authority.** NOELIA_AI and any agent may prepare and analyse;
> **a human decides, and the decision is recorded by the body that owns it** (`FIR-017`).

---

## 1. Purpose

**1.1** To provide one professional, controlled capability that (a) administers the family's wealth and
governance **on instruction**; (b) prepares the analysis and reporting that the family's decision-makers
need; (c) coordinates advisers with disciplined management of fees, conflicts and confidentiality; and
(d) implements decisions **after** they are lawfully taken — through the correct accounting authority.

**1.2 What the Family Office exists to prevent.** The failure mode it addresses is a family in which
information is scattered, advisers are engaged without control, money moves without record, conflicts are
unmanaged, and no one can answer "what do we actually own, owe and owe to whom?" with evidence.

**1.3 What it must not become.** A shadow governing body; a place where decisions are taken informally and
then "recorded" elsewhere; a custodian of assets; an entity that holds a family's personal information
without a lawful basis; or a vehicle for a family member's private dealings.

**a licence may be required, and acting without one may be an offence.** **`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` before any
such activity begins** (`Q-LEG-03`). **The control plane's payment/lending models are software, not a
licence.** (`DAA-FO-001`.)

## 3. Mandate, scope and the four limits

**3.1 The mandate rule.** The Family Office acts **only** under a written mandate from a competent body
(the Trustees; a board; the Family Assembly for family-activity work). **No mandate = no authority. An oral
instruction is not a mandate** — it may be an instruction to *prepare* a mandate. Every mandate states:
the client (the body); the scope; the limits; the reporting; the fee or cost basis; the conflicts rule; and
the revocation right (`D35` §Mandates).

**3.2 The four limits.**

| # | Limit |
|---|---|
| **L1** | **It decides nothing fiduciary.** No distribution; no grant; no company resolution; no share issue; no related-party approval; no investment outside the mandate limits; no compromise of a claim |
| **L2** | **It holds no legal title.** Assets are registered in the name of the owning body (`D06` §6, `D35`) |
| **L3** | **It moves no money except through the accounting authority**, on an instruction traceable to a recorded decision (`D35` §Payments) |
| **L4** | **It creates no records of its own authority.** Its reports and dashboards are **information**, never evidence of a decision, a holding, or a right |

**3.3 What the Family Office *may* do.**

| # | Permitted |
|---|---|
| P-1 | Maintain the family's and the Trust's **registers** under instruction, from source documents (`D63`–`D74`) |
| P-2 | Prepare **reports, models, valuations requests, budget packs, and due diligence** (`D33`, `D34`) |
| P-3 | **Coordinate advisers**, run fee comparisons, check conflicts, hold the engagement register (`D36`) |
| P-4 | Administer the **family-activity budget** approved by the Family Assembly, and the `D16` programme |
| P-5 | Prepare **agenda packs and drafts** for the family bodies and the boards (`D12`, `D25`) |
| P-6 | **Execute** decisions within a written execution mandate: instruct a bank transfer per a recorded distribution decision; file a document the Company Secretary is authorised to file; place an investment within a mandate |
| P-7 | Maintain the **document vault and index** (`D74`) |
| P-8 | Run the **control checks** (reconciliation, two-signature verification, access reviews) and **escalate exceptions** |

**3.4 What the Family Office must never do.**

| # | Prohibited |
|---|---|
| X-1 | Decide, or present itself as deciding, anything in L1 |
| X-2 | Hold assets, or hold them "for convenience" in a staff name, a nominee arrangement, or an unregistered account |
| X-3 | Pay a family member, an employee, an adviser or a related party without a recorded decision and the correct authority (`D35`, `D72`) |
| X-4 | Keep a **shadow ledger, a private spreadsheet of balances, or any off-system account of money** (`FIR-018`) |
| X-5 | Approve its own fees, expenses or scope; or pay itself outside the approved arrangement |
| X-6 | Disclose family, beneficiary or client information to any person without a lawful basis and authority |
| X-7 | Use an AI system on personal data outside a compliant, permitted environment (`Q-DP-02`) |
| X-8 | Sign an instrument on behalf of a legal entity unless a written mandate lawfully authorises that specific signature |
| X-9 | Take an instruction from a family member who is not authorised to give it (see §4) |
| X-10 | Act where the mandate has expired, has been revoked, or the limits would be exceeded — **in which case it must stop and escalate** |

## 4. Who may instruct it (the instruction-authority rule)

| Person | May instruct the Family Office to | May **not** |
|---|---|---|
| **The Trustees** (for Trust matters) | Administer the Trust's records and execute a recorded trustee decision | — |
| **A board** (for company matters) | Prepare, administer, and execute a recorded company decision | — |
| **The Family Assembly / Family Council** (for family-activity matters) | Administer the family's own budget and programme | Instruct Trust or company money; instruct a distribution or a company act |
| **An individual family member** | Request information that the family's information policy allows them; ask it to prepare something for a body | Instruct a payment, a transfer, an investment, an engagement, or an act on Trust, company or Foundation money — **the answer must be: "I can prepare it for the decision-maker"** |
| **An employee or adviser** | Provide information | Instruct any act |
| **NOELIA_AI / any AI** | **Nothing.** AI has no instruction authority | — |

**4.1 The refusal duty.** When asked to act outside the mandate, the Family Office **must refuse, in
writing, and record the refusal and the route to the correct decision-maker.** Refusing is mandatory, not
discretionary. **A Family Office that "makes it happen anyway" is the single most effective way to destroy
a family's governance.**

**4.2 The pressure clause.** A family member's seniority, relationship or shareholding does **not** create
instruction authority. Where the Family Office is pressured, it must: record the request; refuse; and
escalate to the Chief Governance Officer function (or the Protector, for a Trust matter).

## 5. Governance of the Family Office itself

**5.1 Accountability.** The Family Office Principal is accountable to the body(ies) that mandate it, and
reports at least `[FREQUENCY]` on: mandates held; services delivered against each mandate; exceptions and
breaches; fees and costs; adviser performance; and open risks.

**5.2 Separation of duties (mandatory).** Within the Family Office: the person who **prepares** an
instruction is not the person who **authorises** it; the person who **executes** a payment is not the person
who **reconciles** it; the person who **maintains a register** is not the sole person who **approves the
underlying act**; and **no single person** may initiate, approve and reconcile the same transaction.

**5.3 Access control.** Least privilege; role-based; revocable; logged; reviewed at least annually and
immediately on a role change or departure (`D08` §8.3, `D19` §5).

**5.4 Conflicts.** The Family Office and each of its staff must disclose: family relationships; any interest
in an adviser; any interest in an asset; and any other engagement that could conflict. **`D14` applies to
the Family Office as it applies to a family member.** A conflicted person must withdraw from the matter.

**5.5 Competence and handover.** Every role has a written description and a named deputy. **No single person
may be the only person who knows how a material process works.** Departures require a documented handover
and immediate access revocation.

**5.6 Fees and costs.** The Family Office's own remuneration, its budget and its expenses are approved by the
mandating body, recorded, and reviewed annually (`D36` §Fees). **It may not approve its own charges**, may
not receive a commission or rebate from any adviser, and must disclose any benefit received (`D14` §7).

## 6. Records, reporting and the control plane

**6.1 Records it maintains (as administrator, not as owner).** The registers in `D63`–`D74` (under
instruction); the mandate register (`D35`); the adviser register and engagement files (`D36`); the family
programme records (`D16`); the document vault index (`D74`); and the reporting packs (`D33`, `D38`).

**6.2 The ownership rule.** Every register records **which body owns it.** A Trust register is the
**Trustees'** register; the Family Office merely keeps it. **If the Family Office ceases to act, the
registers return to their owners with a complete handover and a reconciliation.**

**6.3 Reporting principles.** (a) Reconcile to the accounting authority or state clearly that a figure is
unreconciled; (b) never mix Trust, company, Foundation, Family Office and personal assets in one "family
wealth" total (`D07` §7.2, `D33` §Boundaries); (c) label every projection as a projection; (d) report bad
news as prominently as good news; (e) explain, in plain language, what the numbers mean.

**6.4 The control-plane position.** The Family Office's software views, dashboards and permissions convey
**no legal authority**. Specifically: a dashboard is not the accounts; a register view is not the register's
owner; a workflow state is not a decision; and a role grant is not a mandate.

**6.5 AI within the Family Office.** (`FIR-017`.) AI may: draft, summarise, model, reconcile-flag, and
prepare analyses. AI may **not**: decide; approve; authorise a payment; hold a mandate; be recorded as an
actor in a legal or financial decision; or have its output presented as legal, tax or investment advice. **An
AI-generated report must be attributed to the human who approved it.**

## 7. Termination and transition

**7.1** The Family Office's mandates may be revoked by the mandating body at any time, in writing.
**7.2 On termination:** all registers, records, keys, credentials and access are handed back or
re-designated within `[NUMBER]` days; a **final reconciliation** is produced and signed; the mandate
register is closed; and **no asset may be transferred by the Family Office as part of the transition** — the
owning bodies deal with their own assets (`D06`, `D35`).
**7.3** Records relating to the family's personal data must be dealt with per `D70` (retention, deletion,
lawful basis).

## 8. Decisions required from the user / family / governing bodies

1. **The Family Office's legal form** (§2 — A/B/C/D), and whether a **licence** is required (`Q-LEG-03`).
2. The **mandates** it will hold, and from which bodies (the Trustees? each board? the family?).
3. The **authority limits** inside the mandates (e.g. a maximum single instruction value).
4. Who performs the **Company Secretary** function, and whether it sits inside or outside the Family Office.
5. The **fees/cost basis**, the budget, and how the group's shared costs are allocated (`D53`).
6. The **reporting frequency** and the reports' audience.
7. The **separation of duties** design and the deputy arrangement.
8. What the Family Office may tell a **family member who asks** about Trust or company money (the default:
   nothing not already disclosed under `D08` §6.3).

## 9. Counsel review note — `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`

1. Whether the intended activities require **registration or licensing** (investment advice, financial
   services, trust services, payments/lending, debt collection) and the consequences of acting without it.
2. Whether a **service company** arrangement creates a deemed employment, a permanent establishment, or a
   transfer-pricing exposure (`Q-TAX-04`).
3. How **intra-group service charges** must be documented and priced (`D53`).
4. Whether the Family Office's **custody of records** (and of any key or credential) creates a legal duty,
   and how to document the agency relationship.
5. Whether the Family Office's **processing of family and beneficiary personal data** requires registration
   and a lawful basis, and whether any transfer requires a permit (`Q-DP-01`, `Q-DP-02`).
6. Whether a **Family Office employee who is a family member** creates a conflict requiring a specific
   control.
7. Whether the Family Office may lawfully **execute payments** on behalf of a body, and the authorisation
   formalities for a bank mandate (`D35`).
8. Whether any **fiduciary duty** could arise from the Family Office's role (for example, an assumption of
   responsibility, or the giving of advice on which a person relies).

**The Family Office does not exist as a legal entity, holds no mandate, owns nothing and has done nothing.
This charter confers no authority and is not adopted.**
