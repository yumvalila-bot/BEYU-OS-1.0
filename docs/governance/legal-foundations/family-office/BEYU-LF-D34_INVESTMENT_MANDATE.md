> **⚠ FOUR WARNINGS.**
> **1. A mandate is a permission to act within stated limits — never a permission to decide what the limits
> are.** Continuing to act after a limit is breached is not permitted; the mandate is suspended.
> **2. A mandate never covers a prohibited instrument.** `D07` §2's prohibited list (speculative and
> unregulated instruments, foreign assets before advice, any asset class not cleared by counsel) applies
> regardless of what a mandate says.
> **3. No mandate may cover a related-party transaction.** Those are decided by the disinterested body
> (`D27` F-07, `D72`).
> **4. The Family Office's own investment decisions are prohibited.** It prepares, recommends, executes
> within limits, and reports.

## 1. Purpose and structure

**1.1** To provide the **single mandate template** for any investment authority granted to a manager, the
Family Office, or an officer — and the due diligence and approval framework that must be completed **before**
any investment is made.

**1.2 The three instruments.**

| Instrument | Who signs | What it authorises |
|---|---|---|
| **The investment policy** (`D07` for the Trust; the company's own policy for a company) | The governing body | What classes may be held, and the limits |
| **This mandate** | The governing body → the mandatary | Who may act, within what limits, for how long |
| **The approval record** | The governing body (or the delegate within limits) | The specific investment, on the specific terms |

**1.3 The rule.** **A mandate does not authorise an investment that the policy prohibits; a policy does not
authorise an investment that the law prohibits; and an approval record does not cure a missing mandate.**

**2.1 The written-confirmation requirement.** The governing body must **write down** each element and
**approve it** — not "note" it. **A risk appetite that has not been written down cannot be applied, and a
mandate issued without one is a mandate without limits.**

**2.2 No return promises.** Neither the mandate, nor any report, nor any AI-generated document may state or
imply a guaranteed, expected or projected return as a certainty. **A projection must be labelled as a
projection, with its assumptions.**

## 4. Due diligence and approval checklist (before **any** investment or acquisition)

| # | Step | Evidence required | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Policy check**: is the class permitted, and is the instrument permitted? | The policy clause cited | Mandatary |
| 2 | **Limit check** (post-transaction basis) on every limit | The calculation, recorded | Mandatary |
| 3 | **Purpose check**: does this serve the stated purpose of the fund (A-1)? | A written rationale | Mandatary |
| 4 | **Liquidity check**: does the transaction respect the floor? | The cash-flow effect, in figures | Mandatary |
| 5 | **Legal**: title, contract, security, regulatory and licensing position | Counsel's or counsel-confirmed analysis; **`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` for any new class or jurisdiction** | Counsel |
| 6 | **Tax**: the treatment of income, gain and the structure | Tax advice reference | Adviser |
| 7 | **Financial diligence**: the counterparty's financial position; for an unlisted business, its accounts and liabilities | The documents, reviewed | Mandatary |
| 8 | **Governance diligence**: for an unlisted business, who decides; the minority position; the exit route; the deadlock risk | The analysis | Mandatary |
| 9 | **Insurance**: is the asset insurable, and is it insured? | The policy or a written confirmation that cover is not available | Mandatary |
| 10 | **Conflicts**: any interest of the Mandatary, a Trustee, a director, a family member or an adviser | The `D72`-style disclosure, recorded | Mandatary |
| 11 | **Valuation**: the price and the basis | A valuation or a documented internal basis with assumptions | Mandatary |
| 12 | **Approval**: the correct body decides, with the power cited (`D07`/`D27`), and the decision is recorded | The decision (`D08` §2 or `D28`/`D73`) | Governing body |
| 13 | **Execution**: the correct instrument and consideration mechanics | Per `D06` V1–V5 | Per mandate |
| 14 | **Register**: recorded in `D67` (asset), `D64`/`D65` (if a shareholding), `D72` (if related), `D74` (the instrument) | The register entries | Company Secretary/Family Office |

**4.1 The fail-closed rule.** Where a step cannot be completed, the investment must not proceed. **"We will
complete the paperwork afterwards" is prohibited** — and it is how families acquire assets they cannot prove
they own.

**4.2 The unlisted-investment warning.** An investment in a private business is the highest-governance-risk
class: illiquid, uncontrolled, and capable of consuming unlimited family attention. **Before any such
investment, the family must answer: who runs it, who decides, how the family exits, and what happens if the
founder dies.** (`D07` §2 A7, `D27` C-10.)

## 5. Ongoing monitoring and the exit

**5.1 Monitoring (each reporting cycle):** valuation with its basis; the case against the original
investment thesis; the liquidity and concentration position; any change in the counterparty or management;
any legal or regulatory development; and the exit path.

**5.2 Impairment discipline.** Where value has fallen materially or recoverability is doubtful, the
governing body must **decide and record** whether to impair, hold or exit. **Carrying a dead asset at cost
to avoid an awkward decision is a breach of the duty to account** (`D07` §5.5).

**5.3 Exit.** Every illiquid investment must have a **written exit plan** at the time of acquisition: the
route, the timing, the likely buyer, the tax consequence and the fallback. **An investment with no exit plan
is a trap, and the family should not make it.**
