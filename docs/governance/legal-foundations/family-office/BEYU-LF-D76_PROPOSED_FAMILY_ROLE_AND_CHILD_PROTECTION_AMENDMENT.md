## 2. The eight roles are separate, and this document keeps them separate

The central proposal: these are **eight distinct things**, and no one of them implies any other.

| # | Role | Created by | What it does **not** confer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Family relationship** | biology, adoption, marriage or law — a fact, not a grant | nothing: a relationship is not a right, a power or an entitlement |
| 2 | **Family Office membership** | the family's own decision, recorded | **no** trustee status, **no** beneficiary right, **no** directorship, **no** signing authority, **no** bank mandate, **no** investment authority, **no** distribution approval, **no** instruction authority |
| 3 | **Settlor status** | the trust instrument, when executed | no trusteeship, no reserved power unless the instrument reserves it, no sole authority over another settlor |
| 4 | **Beneficiary status** | the instrument, through a defined Beneficiary Class | no decision-making authority, no access to trust records beyond what the instrument and law allow, no management control |
| 5 | **Trustee status** | appointment under the instrument, with the trustee's duties | never inferred from relationship, membership, settlor status or age |
| 6 | **Representative / delegate status** | a recorded delegation, with scope, limits and expiry | no authority beyond the recorded scope, and it expires |
| 7 | **Operational access** (who may use the platform, and for what) | a grant, scoped to capabilities | no legal authority. Platform access is an operational fact, **never** evidence of a legal power |
| 8 | **Legal signing powers** (who may bind an entity or the trust) | resolution, mandate or instrument, with evidence | never granted by membership, relationship, or a platform permission change |

**The correspondence with the code is deliberate and already true today.** In
`src/lib/family/office/authority.ts`, authority derives **only** from a `RESOLUTION` or `DELEGATION`
reference; there is no `role` field at all. In `src/lib/admin/registry-service.ts`,
`registerFamilyMember` creates **no** users row, **no** password, **no** session and **no** role, and it
refuses a duplicate membership, requires a governed parent for descent, and refuses
`directDescendant: true` without verification. **Membership does not create access, and access does not
create authority.** This proposal asks the family to adopt what the software already enforces.

### 2.1 A worked contrast

| Act | Family Office members may | Nobody holds today |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare information, draft, schedule, coordinate | ✅ | — |
| Record a decision that an authorized body already made | ✅ | — |
| **Approve** a distribution, an investment, a payment or a company act | ❌ by membership alone | the authority to do so is not evidenced by anyone, for any sum |
| **Sign** for the trust or an entity | ❌ | no signing power is created |
| **Instruct** a transfer of trust, company or Foundation money | ❌ (**`D31` §4.1 refusal duty applies**) | — |

## 3. Permissions are granted on evidence, and every grant is recorded

Proposed for every role assignment and every operational access grant:

| Field | Required | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Who granted it | ✅ | authority must be attributable |
| Who verified the identity and the authority | ✅ | separated from the grantor where possible |
| Evidence relied on | ✅ | a grant without evidence is a rumour with permissions |
| Effective date | ✅ | — |
| Scope (which entity, which property, which capability) | ✅ | no blanket grants |
| **Expiry** | ✅ | access ends; it does not persist by inertia |
| Revocation route and who may revoke | ✅ | must be knowable *before* it is needed |
| Review date | ✅ | — |

**No self-elevation.** A person may not acquire authority over themselves by editing a profile, asserting a
relationship label, changing client-side state, uploading a document, or holding a seniority position.
`D31` §4.2 already states the principle for instruction authority; this proposal extends it to permission
grants.

**Current state, honestly:** `family_members` rows enter with `verificationStatus = UNVERIFIED` and no
verifier is evidenced (`BEYU-SCG-16` `BLK-15`). The schema is right to default to `UNVERIFIED`. Nothing
below should be read as having been verified.

## 4. Child protection — the protective position for `FAM-3`

A minor cannot consent, cannot ratify a mistake later, and cannot be made whole by an appeal. The default
is therefore **minimum data, minimum access, no capability**.

### 4.1 Proposed prohibitions (no login, no capability, no control)

1. **No login credentials, authentication factor or session** for the minor — not now, and not on any
   transition to majority without a fresh, evidenced decision.
2. **No signature capability and no cryptographic key of any kind**, ever, from membership alone.
3. **No payment, investment, redemption, transfer or custody authority**, direct or delegated.
4. **No administrative permissions**, and no direct control of trust assets, from membership alone.
5. **No instruction authority**: `D31` §4.1 applies to a minor exactly as it applies to any other family
   member — *"I can prepare it for the decision-maker."*

### 4.2 Proposed data-protection position

6. **Minimisation**: the minimum data necessary, and no more. A reported age does not justify a recorded
   date of birth.
7. **Never in logs, analytics, public blockchain data, or unnecessary user interface.** No reference to a
   child belongs on any chain, in any telemetry stream, or in any screen that does not need it.
8. **Access-controlled**: `HIGHLY_RESTRICTED` classification, child records private by default, and access
   on a need-to-know basis with an auditable trail.
9. **Synthetic data only** in tests, fixtures, demonstrations and any environment outside production.
10. **Sealed records**: any register of the minor's interest is stored so that a third party — including a
    commercial counterparty or a service provider — cannot enumerate the family's children.

### 4.3 What is missing, and why this is not fixed in code today

**Minority is not modelled anywhere**: there is no date of birth, no age, no guardian, no incapacity and no
age-transition event in the schema, the services or the tests (`BLK-06`). A system with no concept of
minority cannot protect a minor *by system*; the protection today rests entirely on classification,
access control and the prohibitions above — which is real and partial, not sufficient.

Modelling minority is **recommended and not performed**, because it is a **privacy decision first**:

> It requires a decision on what child data is collected, where it is stored, who may read it, how long it
> is retained, and what happens on transition to majority. Writing a schema before that decision would
> harden an unconsidered answer into a migration.
