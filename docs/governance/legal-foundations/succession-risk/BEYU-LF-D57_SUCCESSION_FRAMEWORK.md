> **⚠ SUCCESSION IS TWO QUESTIONS, AND THEY ARE OFTEN CONFUSED.**
> **1. The ASSETS (who receives what).** Governed by wills, the trust, matrimonial property and the succession
> regime that applies — **and constrained by whatever the law protects for dependants, which the pack does not
> override and does not assume away** (`Q-S-02`).
> **2. The CONTROL (who decides what, from the day after).** Governed by the company's articles, the
> shareholders' agreement, the trust instrument, and the directors' appointments — **not by the will.**
> **A will that says "my son runs the business" does not make the son a director, a trustee, or a shareholder
> with control. Only the instrument governing the role does that.**

## 2. The four succession streams

| # | Stream | Governed by | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Personal assets** | The will, the applicable succession regime, matrimonial property | Who receives what |
| 2.2 | **The Trust and its assets** | The trust instrument, the Trustees' powers, the protector | Who benefits; whether the structure continues |
| 2.3 | **The company (shares and control)** | Articles, shareholders' agreement, `D22`/`D23`/`D24`, `D59` | Who holds shares; who controls the board; what happens on death |
| 2.4 | **The roles (offices)** | The appointment instruments (`D66`) | Who is trustee, director, officer, signatory — **and from when** |

**2.5 The role-transmission rule.** **No office passes automatically.** Each office has its own replacement
mechanism, it must be exercised, and the register updated (`D03` §6, `D25` §2, `D66`).

## 3. Risks to be mitigated (and the mechanism for each)

| # | Risk | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **The shares pass to multiple heirs and control fragments** | A **single holder** or a **trust** holding the control shares; a pre-emption regime in the articles; a **shareholders' agreement** with permitted-transferee and transfer mechanics (`D23`) |
| 3.2 | **A transmission-on-death event freezes the company** | `D59` decision tree: interim voting arrangements, a **registered** mechanism in the articles, and a documented protocol for the registry |
| 3.3 | **The executors are the wrong people** (or there are too few) | Executors named in the will; the **maximum number is limited (four observed for the relevant regime — `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`, `Q-S-05`)**; a trust corporation alternative where the law allows |
| 3.4 | **The successors have no capacity or interest** | Next-generation preparation (`D16`); an independent director; a professional manager; a **buy-out/settlement route** (`D29`) |
| 3.5 | **A dependant is left unprotected and challenges the plan** | The law's protection is respected in the plan (`Q-S-02`) — **plan within it, not against it** |
| 3.6 | **The Trust's trustee body is depleted** | Minimum trustee numbers and a replacement mechanism (`D03`) |
| 3.7 | **Nobody knows where the documents are** | `D74` custody register; the executor and family know the location |
| 3.8 | **The founder's key relationships and knowledge are lost** | Documented deputy; written know-how; `D60` critical records |
| 3.9 | **Tax on transmission is ignored** | `[TAX TO CONFIRM]`: inheritance/estate/capital gains/stamp duty, and the CGT 30-day payment rule observed in the Income Tax Act materials (`LEGAL_SOURCES_REGISTER.md` `TAX-2`) |
| 3.10 | **A beneficiary's own circumstances** (divorce, insolvency, incapacity) expose the asset | `Q-S-04`; the trust's protective provisions (`D05`) |

4. Whether a **family employment** route is intended for successors (`D15`).
5. The **timing and disclosure**: when the family is told, and what they are told.
6. The **liquidity** available to meet any tax, buy-out or settlement obligation.
