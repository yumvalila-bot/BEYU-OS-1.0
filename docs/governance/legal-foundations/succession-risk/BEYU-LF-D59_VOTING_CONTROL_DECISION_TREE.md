> **⚠ THE CENTRAL QUESTION OF THE WHOLE STRUCTURE.**
> **On the day a controlling shareholder dies or loses capacity, does the control architecture survive — or
> does it freeze?** The answer is determined **today**, by what is written in the Articles, the shareholders'
> agreement and the trust instrument. **It cannot be fixed afterwards.**
> **And the corollary: a shareholder cannot control the company from their grave by a will alone.** The
> mechanics must be **registered** — in the company's own constitutional documents — or they will not operate.

## 1. The four control mechanisms (and what each requires)

| # | Mechanism | What it does | Where it must live | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **The Trust holds the shares** | Continuity — the trustee survives the individual | The trust instrument + the company's articles recognising trustees | **The most robust** — but it must be valid, and the Trustees must be able to act |
| B | **Articles-based transmission provision** | Defines what happens on death/incapacity (e.g. the shares vest in identified persons, or a **pre-emption/buy-out** applies) | **The Articles** (binding on everyone, including an estate) | Must respect the law on transmission and the registry's practice (`Q-S-05`) |
| C | **Shareholders' agreement** | Contractual obligations among the current holders (transfer, buy-sell, funding) | The agreement + adherence deeds | **Binds signatories, not the company or an unwilling heir** unless the articles support it |
| D | **Dual-class structure** (`DC-01`) | Separates economic interest from voting control | The Articles | **A proposal only** — `DAA-ARCH-005` open, `Q-H-01` unanswered |
| E | **Nominee/bare-trust holding** | Legal title with a third party | The arrangement + the law | **`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` — high risk of being disregarded** |

## 2. The decision tree

```
Q1. Does a single holder (or a trust) hold a CONTROL position today?
   ├─ NO  → Control is already shared. Go to Q4 (deadlock and exit: D29). Adopt the transmission rules anyway.
   └─ YES → Q2.
Q2. Is the holder the TRUST (or another body that survives the individual)?
   ├─ YES → Q3.
   └─ NO (an individual) → Q3B.
Q3. Trust holds control:
   (a) Confirm the trust is validly constituted and the Trustees can act.           [COUNSEL]
   (b) Confirm the trusteeship has a replacement mechanism and a minimum number.    [D03]
   (c) Confirm the company's articles RECOGNISE a trustee holder and define how the
       vote is exercised (by the Trustees' decision, not opinion, and not by a
       beneficiary personally).                                                     [D22/D23]
   (d) Confirm a trustee change does not require a new share issue.                 [D24]
   (e) Confirm the trust's own governing law permits the holding and the duration.  [D01/D02]
   → Outcome: continuity. Record it, and test it annually.
Q3B. An individual holds control:
   (a) What happens on death?  → the estate; probate; the registry steps; timing.    [Q-S-05]
   (b) What happens on incapacity? → D56; is there an instrument that operates?      [Q-S-03]
   (c) Choose a mechanism (A, B or C above) and IMPLEMENT it in the instruments.
   (d) If none is implemented, record the risk as CRITICAL and tell the founder.     [D61]
   → Outcome: an unmitigated single point of failure. Do not describe the structure
     as "succession-ready".
Q4. Multiple holders:
   (a) Deadlock mechanics: D29 §2 (escalation ladder, time-boxes, options A–F).
   (b) Exit: D29 §3 (leaver categories, drag/tag, price determination, liquidity).
   (c) Transmission: the articles' mechanism; pre-emption; permitted transferees; the
       treatment of an estate, a minor, a spouse, an incapacitated person.            [D22/D23]
   (d) Buy-sell on death: who buys, at what price, with what funding (insurance?).
Q5. Family/next generation in the frame:
   (a) Is the intended successor competent and willing?    [D16]
   (b) Is the family definition settled?                    [FIR-003 — currently NO]
   (c) Are the family's own governance rules consistent with the company's? [D11–D19]
```

## 3. Transmission-on-death: the practical mechanics

| # | Step | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Death is notified**; the register is marked and the date recorded | `D64`, `D66` |
| 3.2 | The **personal representatives/executors** establish their authority (grant of probate, or the applicable process) | `[COUNSEL: the registry's practice, `Q-S-05`]` |
| 3.3 | The **articles' mechanism** determines whether the estate becomes a member, whether the shares must be offered to the others, and at what price | Must be in the articles to bind the estate |
| 3.4 | **Voting during the estate period**: who may vote, and on whose instruction — **this must be answered in advance** | The most common freeze |
| 3.5 | **Directors' appointments** continue independently of the shareholding; check whether a shareholder-appointed director's position lapses | `D25`, `D27` H-01 |
| 3.6 | **Pre-emption/offer mechanics** are followed strictly; a failed procedure is a defect | `D23` §5.6 |
| 3.7 | **Registry filings** made; certificates reissued; the register updated | `D68` |
| 3.8 | **Tax** on transmission considered **before** the transfer is completed | `[TAX TO CONFIRM]`, `Q-S-05` |

## 4. The recommended (not required) shape

**The pack does not choose the family's control model.** Where the family asks for a default, the sequence
that carries the fewest structural risks is: **(1) control held by the Trust; (2) the Articles containing an
explicit transmission and pre-emption mechanism; (3) a shareholders' agreement binding the current holders,
with adherence deeds for future ones; (4) the dual-class arrangement only if counsel confirms it and the
family accepts the trade-offs (`DC-01` §4).** **And in every case: an implemented mechanism beats an intended
one.**

## 5. Decisions required from the user / family / governing bodies

1. **Who is intended to hold voting control**, now and after the founder.
2. **Which mechanism** (§1) is adopted, and who implements it.
3. Whether the **Trust** holds the control shares, and on what terms.
4. The **transmission and pre-emption** rules, and the **price mechanism** (`D29` §3.4).
5. Who may **vote during an estate period**.
6. Whether **insurance funding** is arranged for any buy-out obligation.
7. Whether a **minor, a spouse or an incapacitated person** may become a shareholder, and on what conditions.
