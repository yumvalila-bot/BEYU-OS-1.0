> **⚠ THE TWO CONTROLS THAT MAKE EMERGENCY AUTHORITY SAFE.**
> **1. It is NARROW** — only to preserve life, safety, a critical system, or an asset from immediate loss.
> **It is never available for a transaction the family wants to do.**
> **2. It is TIME-BOXED and REPORTED** — it expires unless renewed (`D27` E-02), and every action is recorded
> and reviewed. **Emergency authority used once and not reviewed becomes a habit, and then a governance
> failure.**

---

## 1. What qualifies (and what never does)

| # | Qualifies | Never qualifies |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Immediate risk to **life, health or safety** | A payment or contract that could wait a day |
| 2 | Immediate risk to a **critical system** or of **major data loss** | A transaction requiring a reserved matter |
| 3 | Immediate risk of **irreversible loss of an asset** (a domain about to lapse, a lockout of an account) | A distribution, an issue or a transfer of shares |
| 4 | A **legal deadline** that cannot be met otherwise (a court step, a filing whose lapse causes serious harm) | Anything that benefits a director, a family member or a connected person |
| 5 | Immediate risk of a **regulatory breach with severe consequence** | A decision the family simply wants quickly |
| 6 | **Fraud or misappropriation in progress** (freeze, preserve, report) | Anything outside the scope of the plan |

| Ref | Situation | Who may act | What they may do | Limit | Reporting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E-01 | Life/health/safety emergency | `[NAMED ROLES]` + the person on site | Take the necessary protective action; commit up to `[AMOUNT]` | `[AMOUNT]`, `[HOURS]` | Within `[N]` hours |

## 3. Mandatory features of any emergency decision

| # | Feature |
|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Contemporaneous record**: what was known, what was decided, who decided, on what authority, and when (use the `D28` E-01 template) |
| 3.2 | **Two-person rule preserved wherever humanly possible**; where one person acts alone, it is recorded as a single-signature action and reviewed |
| 3.3 | **Immediate post-action reporting** to the board, the Audit and Risk Committee, and the Trustees where Trust assets are exposed |
| 3.4 | **Ratification or unwinding**: if the action exceeded the authority, the board decides whether to ratify or unwind it — and records the decision |
| 3.5 | **Review of the cause**, with the lesson fed into `D61` |
| 3.6 | **No emergency power over**: instruments, shares, trust assets, distributions, related-party transactions, or anything the law reserves to a specific organ |
| 3.7 | **No AI may act** under this matrix; AI may prepare, log and notify |

## 4. Prohibited uses (the abuse list)

| # | Prohibited |
|---|---|
| 1 | Using emergency authority to do something that was refused through the normal process |
| 2 | Using it to pay or benefit a director, officer, family member or connected person |
| 3 | Using it to change a mandate, a signatory, an ownership record or an instrument |
| 4 | Using it to avoid a reserved matter or a shareholder approval |
| 5 | **Retro-fitting** a justification after acting ("we decided it was an emergency afterwards") |
| 6 | Acting alone when two persons were available |
| 7 | Failing to report, or reporting late and blaming the crisis |
| 8 | Allowing a time-boxed authority to continue without renewal and record |

## 5. Decisions required

1. **Who is named** in each row of the matrix, and their deputies.
2. The **amount limits** and **time limits**.
3. The **reporting times** and recipients.
4. The **ratification process** and who reviews the emergency record.
5. The **notification lines** (insurer, bank, regulator, family).
6. Whether the group runs a **crisis simulation** at least annually (`D60` §3.10).
