> **⚠ THE THREE THINGS A LICENCE MUST NEVER DO.**
> **1. Never transfer title by accident.** If the intention is to transfer ownership, use an assignment
> (`D41`) — a document called a "licence" that grants "all rights, permanently" may be construed as one.
> **2. Never grant more than the licensor owns.** A licensor can only grant what it holds — **and where
> ownership is `OWNERSHIP UNESTABLISHED` (`D40`), the licence is a promise that may be incapable of
> performance. Do not licence an asset whose title has not been established.**
> **3. Never move the Foundation's or the Trust's property for a private benefit.** A licence that favours a
> family member or a group company at the Foundation's expense is a private-benefit and tax defect
> (`D51`, `D52`).

## 1. Licence types and when each is used

| # | Type | Use | Key constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| L-1 | **Intra-group licence** | One entity owns, another uses | Must be documented, at a defensible value, and registered in `D72` |
| L-2 | **Group → Foundation licence** | The Foundation uses the group's mark/software | Purpose-serving; no private benefit; arm's-length testable (`D54`) |
| L-3 | **Foundation → group licence** | Rare — only where the Foundation genuinely owns an asset | Must be on terms **no less favourable to the Foundatization**; independent decision (`D51`) |
| L-4 | **Group → third-party licence** | Customers, partners, resellers, integrators | Scope-controlled; supports the brand rules (`D47`) |
| L-5 | **Third-party → group licence** | Inbound software, content, data, models | `D45` for software; a licence review for anything else |
| L-6 | **Trade-mark co-existence / consent** | Where another party has a similar mark | Counsel only — a co-existence agreement is a risk instrument |
| L-7 | **Open-source licence** | Free of the group's drafting, but binding on the group | `D45` |

## 4. Intra-group licence rules (special conditions)

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| I-1 | **Documented, always** — "it's all the same family" is not a licence, and in a dispute, a tax enquiry or a due diligence, it is indefensible |
| I-2 | **Consideration stated** — including where the value is zero; record **why** zero is defensible |
| I-3 | **Arm's-length test** — would the same terms be offered to an unrelated party? If not, document the reason and register it (`D72`) |
| I-4 | **Cost and benefit allocation** — cross-referring to `D53`; a licence fee is a real cost in the licensee and a real income in the licensor |
| I-5 | **Termination on separation** — what happens if the entity leaves the family structure (`D29`), or is sold? **The licence must not silently travel with a sold entity.** |
| I-6 | **Tax and transfer-pricing documentation** — `[TAX TO CONFIRM]` |
| I-7 | **No implied authority** — the licence is not authority to bind the licensor |

## 5. Foundation and Trust licences (higher requirements)

**5.1 Group → Foundation.** Mandatory: a statement that the licence **serves the Foundation's purposes**; the
Foundation governing body's **independent** decision (with the conflict managed, `D51`); a **value
comparison** demonstrating the terms are no less favourable to the Foundation than alternatives (including
free/open alternatives); a termination right for the Foundation; and no obligation on the Foundation to
purchase anything.

**5.2 Foundation → group — the critical scrutiny point.** Any licence from the Foundation to a group company
or a family member must be: **purpose-serving**; approved by a fully **independent** quorum with the
interested parties excluded; on terms **more favourable to the Foundation** than, or at least equal to, the
market; documented with the comparison; and registered (`D72`). **A licence that lets the group use a
Foundation asset for nothing, or for less than it is worth, is the classic private-benefit problem.** Where
the Foundation's asset was received **under a restricted or donor-imposed condition**, it may not be licensed
at all (`D52` on restricted funds).

**5.3 Trust-held IP.** Where the Trust owns IP, the licence requires the Trustees' decision, any Reserved
Matter consent (`D04` §7), a conflict check, and counsel's view on the Trustee's duty to the beneficiaries
when granting a licence to a family-controlled company (`Q-T-04`).

## 7. Decisions required

1. Whether intra-group use is licensed in writing at all (the pack's position: **yes**).
2. The **fee policy** for intra-group and Foundation licences (market, cost, or nil — with reasons).
3. Who may grant a licence, and who may receive one (`D27`).
4. Whether exclusive licences will ever be granted (they are hard to unwind).
5. Whether the group will permit sublicensing by partners.
