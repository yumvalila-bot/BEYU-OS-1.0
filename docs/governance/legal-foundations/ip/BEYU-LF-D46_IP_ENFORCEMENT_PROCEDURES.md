> **⚠ THE ENFORCEMENT PRINCIPLE.**
> **The group's default posture is to document, preserve and escalate — not to threaten.** Every enforcement
> step carries the risk of a **counterclaim** (groundless threats, defamation, abuse of process, an
> invalidity attack that destroys the registration), and it consumes money the family may prefer to spend
> elsewhere. **Enforcement is a commercial decision taken by the board with advice — never by a frustrated
> employee, a brand ambassador, a chatbot or an AI-drafted letter.**
> **And the first question is always: does the group actually own the right, and is the registration
> valid? A demand letter sent on a right the group cannot prove is worse than sending nothing.**

## 1. Renewal and maintenance

| # | Step | Owner | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every dated obligation is entered in `D44` and creates a `D68` entry with **two reminders** | Company Secretary | On registration/filing |
| 2 | The **renewal owner is named**, and the reminder goes to a role **and** a person | Company Secretary | Annually reviewed |
| 3 | Confirm the **registry's own** position before paying (amount, deadline, form) | Agent/counsel | ≥ 60 days before |
| 4 | Instruct the payment through the proper chain (`D35` §3) | Finance authority | ≥ 30 days before |
| 5 | Obtain and file the **registry acknowledgement** | Company Secretary | On receipt |
| 6 | **Test the diary** annually (a reminder that never arrives is not a control) | Chief Governance Officer | Annually |
| 7 | If a renewal is **deliberately abandoned**, record the decision and the reason | Board | Before lapse |
| 8 | If a renewal is **missed**, treat it as an incident (`D71`): cause, remedy attempt, exposure, and report | CTO → board | Immediately |

**1.1 The instruction rule.** **No renewal may be paid on an unverified amount or an unverified deadline,
and no renewal may be made from a "reminder" that does not state its source.** (This is the practical
consequence of the unresolved trade-mark term: `D40` §5.2.)

## 2. Protection and preservation

| # | Measure | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Evidence of creation and use** | Dated artefacts are preserved (commit history, design files, dated publications, invoices showing the mark in use); **evidence is collected in the ordinary course** |
| 2.2 | **Secrecy programmes** | Trade secrets have access lists, markings, and confidentiality instruments (`D44` §2.5) |
| 2.3 | **Access control** | Repository, cloud, registrar, DNS and app-store accounts are entity-owned with two-person control (`D35` §2) |
| 2.4 | **Contractual protection** | Every contributor covered (`D43`); every third-party use licensed (`D42`) |
| 2.5 | **Publication discipline** | Nothing is published that **discloses a trade secret** or **pre-empts a registration** (a design or invention is often unregistrable once public) (`D47`) |
| 2.6 | **Employee/contractor exits** | The exit checklist covers return, revocation and confirmation of no retention (`D15`) |
| 2.7 | **Incident route** | Leakage, a lost device with source code, an exposed repository, a departing contributor with materials — all are **incidents**, escalated immediately |

## 3. Access control

**3.1** Access to IP assets is **role-based, least-privilege, named and reviewed** (`D08` §8).
**3.2** **Admin access to critical accounts** (repository, cloud, registrar/DNS, app stores, payment,
production databases) is held by **at least two people**, never one, and never an external party alone.
**3.3** **Access is not ownership and not authority** — a person with admin access may not license, transfer
or publish without a decision (`D39` §7).
**3.4** Every change of personnel triggers an access review; **departure is a controlled event**.
**3.5** A **third party** (agency, developer, host) never holds the **only** copy or the **only** admin
credential. **If it does today, that is a priority remediation item.**

## 4. Licensing and transfer

| # | Step | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | **Confirm title** in `D40` — **if title is unestablished, stop** | The register |
| 4.2 | Choose the instrument: licence (`D42`) or assignment (`D41`) | — |
| 4.3 | Identify the **authority** (`D27`): board; and shareholder/Reserved-Matter consent where the transfer is outbound or material | `D27`, `D04` §7 |
| 4.4 | Complete the schedules, the consideration and the formalities | Counsel |
| 4.5 | Execute; file in `D74`; **record in `D40`/`D44`**; register in `D72` if related-party | The registers |
| 4.6 | **File any recordal** with the registry and diarise the acknowledgement | `D68` |
| 4.7 | Update insurance, financing and any security documents affected by the change | Company Secretary |

**4.1 Exception — the Trust and the Foundation.** Any transfer **into** the Trust, **out of** a company **to**
a family member or another family entity, or **between** the group and the Foundation, requires the
additional process in `D04` §7, `D51`, `D53` and `D72`. **The pack's position: an IP transfer to a related
party is one of the highest-risk transactions in the whole structure, and it is never a formality.**

## 5. Valuation

**5.1 When a valuation is needed.** On a transfer to a related party; on a contribution to or from a trust;
on a financing where IP is security; on a sale or an exit; on a tax filing where a value must be stated; and
on a dispute.

**5.2 How a valuation is done here.**

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The valuer is independent of the decision** and is instructed by the body that needs the valuation (`D36`) |
| 2 | The **basis** is stated: cost / market / income (with assumptions) / replacement / liquidation |
| 3 | **The basis and the date are recorded in `D44`** — a valuation without a basis and a date is a rumour |
| 4 | **A valuation is an opinion, not a fact.** It is never presented as the price; the price is a decision |
| 5 | Where the transaction is with a related party, the valuation and the comparative tests are documented (`D72`) |
| 6 | **No unaudited internal "value" is used** for a related-party transfer |
| 7 | **No AI-generated valuation** is used, quoted or filed |

## 6. Enforcement: detection, preservation and escalation

### 6.1 Detection

| Source | Route |
|---|---|
| Watch service / registry monitoring | CTO |
| Marketplace, app-store and social monitoring | Brand owner (`D47`) |
| Customer, supplier, employee or family report | Any person → CTO; **a report is protected, never punished** |
| Automated similarity/scan tooling | CTO |
| A cease-and-desist or cease-and-desist-like letter received | **Immediately to the Company Secretary and counsel — never answered informally** |

### 6.2 Preservation (do this first, always)

**Preserve the evidence before doing anything else**: screenshots with dates and URLs; purchase or download
records; the infringing material itself; correspondence; and a written log of who saw what and when.
**Preserve, then decide — never have a conversation that puts the other side on notice before the evidence
is secured, and never "clean up" or delete the group's own records.**

### 6.3 Escalation ladder

| Step | Action | Authority | Counsel instructed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Internal assessment**: does the group own the right? Is the registration valid and in force? Is the mark actually being used? Is there a real commercial harm? | CTO | Not yet |
| 1 | **Evidence pack** assembled and preserved | CTO | Not yet |
| 2 | **Commercial assessment**: revenue at risk, brand damage, cost, and whether a conflict with a partner/customer exists | CTO + CFO | Not yet |
| 3 | **Counsel opinion** on the merits, the risks (including a **groundless-threats** or invalidity-counterclaim risk) and the options | Board | **Yes — mandatory before any approach** |
| 4 | **Options**: do nothing and monitor; a "please note our rights" courtesy letter; a take-down notice; a licence/co-existence approach; a formal demand; proceedings | Board (large value: shareholders if Reserved) | Yes |
| 5 | **Action** taken by counsel or with counsel's settled correspondence — **never in the group's own template words** | Board | Yes |
| 6 | **Litigation**: authority per `D27` (and `D71` for the register, the insurer notification, and any security for costs) | Board | Yes |
| 7 | **Settlement**: authority per `D27`; any settlement that affects the Trust or crosses a Reserved Matter goes through that process | Per `D27` | Yes |
| 8 | **Record** the outcome in `D71` and the lessons in `D44` | Company Secretary | — |

### 6.4 Absolute prohibitions

| # | Prohibited |
|---|---|
| 1 | Sending a demand without counsel's written view on ownership, validity and the merits |
| 2 | Threatening proceedings to obtain a commercial advantage the group has no legal basis to obtain |
| 3 | Contacting a counterparty's **customers** to allege infringement without advice (this is one of the fastest routes to a counterclaim) |
| 4 | Responding informally to a demand received by the group |
| 5 | Making public allegations of infringement or copying (including on social media, in a press release, or through an influencer) |
| 6 | Letting an AI tool draft or send legal correspondence |
| 7 | Destroying or altering evidence, logs or records once a dispute is foreseeable |
| 8 | Continuing to use a right the group knows (or an adviser has advised) is likely invalid |

### 6.5 Defensive posture

**6.5.1 Before adopting a new name, mark, logo or domain** (`Q-IP-05`: the prior-user/common-law position, and what evidence establishes use): run a **clearance search** (registry, marketplaces,
social, DNS, the company registry) and record the result — **a search is cheap; a rebrand after an
infringement claim is not.** Where a search reveals a likely conflict, escalate before committing to the
brand.
**6.5.2 The "we'll deal with it later" rule does not exist here.** A mark adopted without a search is a
decision to accept the risk — **and if the family accepts it, that acceptance must be recorded, not
implied.**

## 7. Records, reporting and the control plane

| # | Requirement |

## 8. Decisions required from the user / family / governing bodies

1. The **enforcement appetite** and the annual budget for protection and enforcement.
2. Who may authorise a **cease-and-desist** and a **settlement**, and up to what value (`D27`).
3. Whether the group will **register** in additional markets, and which.
4. Whether the group will engage a **watch service**.
5. Whether to pursue **co-existence** rather than conflict where a similar mark is found.
6. Who is the **named brand and IP owner** for escalation purposes.
