> **⚠⚠ THE ONE RULE THAT MUST NOT BE BROKEN IN THIS DOCUMENT.**
> **It is not assumed that any restrictive covenant is enforceable — not the non-compete, not the non-solicit,
> not the non-hire, and not the non-dealing clause. In many legal systems a covenant that restrains a person's
> freedom to work is unenforceable unless it is strictly justified, reasonable in scope, duration and
> territory, and protective of a legitimate business interest; and in some it is unenforceable or unlawful
> in principle.** The law varies, has been reformed repeatedly, and is fact-specific. **This pack therefore
> drafts covenants as a request for counsel to test, and it designs the group's real protection to work
> WITHOUT them.**
> **The protections that do not depend on a non-compete: confidentiality (`NDA-01`); trade-secret protection;
> IP ownership (`D41`); access control; the employment contract's duty of fidelity during employment; notice
> periods; garden leave where the law permits; deferred remuneration; the treatment of equity on cessation
> (`CX-01`); and evidence.**
> **And a covenant may never be drafted, applied or enforced so as to prevent a person from: working in a
> lawful occupation they are entitled to pursue; reporting unlawful conduct; cooperating with a regulator;
> or exercising a statutory right.**

## 1. Which protection to use (the decision sequence — narrowest first)

| # | Protection | Enforceability posture | Cost / drawback | When to prefer it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **Confidentiality** (`NDA-01`) | The strongest and most portable protection; survives the engagement | Cannot prevent lawful competition based on general skill | **Always. The first line.** |
| 1.2 | **Trade-secret protection** (technical + contractual + access control) | Protected by law **while the information remains secret** | Requires real access control and evidence; lost by carelessness | **Always**, for the group's actual secrets |
| 1.3 | **IP ownership** (`D41`) | Ownership does not depend on a restraint | Does not stop a competitor from being founded | **Always** |
| 1.4 | **Duty of fidelity during employment/service** | Arises by law in most systems `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` | Ends with the engagement | During engagement |
| 1.5 | **Notice period + garden leave** (where lawful) | Often more effective than a covenant | The company pays the notice | Where the role is exposed |
| 1.6 | **Deferred remuneration / equity with a leaver condition** (`CX-01`, `ESOP-01`) | Economic rather than legal; enforceable if the forfeiture is lawful | **Must not be a penalty** | For long-term retention |
| 1.7 | **Non-solicitation of employees** | Usually (not always) more defensible than a full non-compete | Cannot stop a general recruitment campaign | Where the team is the asset |
| 1.8 | **Non-solicitation / non-dealing with customers and suppliers** | More defensible if **narrow** (named customers the person dealt with, a short period) | Hard to police | Where there is a real customer relationship |
| 1.9 | **Non-compete** | **The most likely to be unenforceable; the most likely to be attacked; the slowest to enforce** | Can be void in part or in whole; an over-broad covenant can **undermine the whole clause set** | **Last. Only with counsel's sign-off, and always as narrow as the interest requires.** |
| 1.10 | **Non-hire / no-poach** | `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM — in some systems a no-poach agreement between employers is unlawful]` | Regulatory risk | **Never as a general ban; only as part of a lawful transaction** |

## 2. The clause set — with the narrower alternative stated for each

| # | Covenant (as a request for counsel) | The narrower alternative the pack prefers | Drafting requirements |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Non-compete.** Restricted for `[PERIOD]` after cessation, within `[TERRITORY]`, in respect of `[SPECIFIC COMPETING ACTIVITY]` | **Confidentiality + trade secrets + a shorter, named-competitor list + a shorter period + a narrow activity definition** | Must be: **(a) reasonably necessary** to protect a **legitimate interest** (trade secrets, confidential information, customer connections, training investment); **(b) reasonable in duration** (shorter is more defensible); **(c) reasonable in territory** (narrow, not "worldwide"); **(d) precise in activity** (the actual activity, not "any business similar to the Company's"); **(e) supported by consideration** that the local law requires; and **(f) not contrary to the local regime** `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` |
| 2.2 | **Non-solicitation of employees.** Restricted for `[PERIOD]` in respect of people the individual **worked with** in the `[PERIOD]` before cessation | **Confidentiality + an announcement/non-disparagement + a shorter list** | Must exclude **general advertising** and **speculative applications**; must not name the whole workforce |
| 2.3 | **Non-solicitation / non-dealing with customers.** Restricted to **customers and suppliers the individual personally dealt with** in the `[PERIOD]` before cessation, for `[PERIOD]` | **A named-customer list** (the narrowest form), **not "all customers"** | Must define "customer" by an evidenced relationship; must exclude customers the individual did not deal with |
| 2.4 | **Non-dealing (no business with the customer even if the customer approaches).** To be used only where a real risk exists | **Non-solicitation only** | `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM whether a non-dealing clause is enforceable]` |
| 2.5 | **Non-disparagement** — no false or misleading statements | **A truthful-statement standard** | **Must** preserve truthful statements to a regulator, a court, an authority, and in a protected disclosure (`NDA-01` §3.18) |
| 2.6 | **Confidentiality after cessation** — indefinite for trade secrets; a period for other information | — | Coordinated with `NDA-01` §3.8 |
| 2.7 | **Intellectual property assignment** — perpetual | — | **A separate instrument** (`D41`); **not** a covenant, and not conditional on continued engagement |
| 2.8 | **Non-competition during the engagement** (exclusivity) | **Disclosure and a conflict-management covenant** rather than a blanket ban on all other activity | Must not prevent a lawful secondary occupation the law protects, or the person's own family/domestic affairs |
| 2.9 | **Notice and garden leave.** Written notice of `[PERIOD]`; the company may require the person to stay away from work on full pay where the law permits | **A longer notice period instead of a covenant** | `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM the lawfulness of garden leave, and of payment in lieu]` |
| 2.10 | **Return of property and information; a certificate of no retention** | — | Enforceable without a covenant (`D43` §C5) |
| 2.11 | **The "blue pencil" / severability expectation** | **Do not rely on severance to save an over-broad covenant** — draft it narrow enough to stand alone | Include a severability clause (`D02` §23.6 as the pack's model) **but treat an over-broad covenant as a defect, not a gamble** |
| 2.12 | **Reasonableness acknowledgement** by the individual | — | Useful but **not decisive**; a court is not bound by an acknowledgement `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` |

## 4. Application table (who, what, and why)

| Counterparty | Covenant set (proposed) | Why this set | Evidence to keep |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Founder** | Confidentiality + IP assignment + non-solicit of employees; **non-compete only if counsel advises** | Founders hold company information by nature; a broad covenant against a founder is the most litigated and the most fragile | `FA-01` execution; `D41` schedules; `D74` |
| **Employee** | Confidentiality + IP assignment + non-solicit; **non-compete only for defined roles and only on advice** | Employment regimes are protective; the risk of an unenforceable or unlawful covenant is highest here | `EMP-01` execution; role analysis |
| **Contractor** | Confidentiality + IP assignment + non-solicit | Workers classified as contractors may still be protected; classification itself must be correct (`EMP-04`) | The contract; the classification record |
| **Vendor / supplier** | Confidentiality + use restriction + no implied licence | A vendor has no "interest" to restrain — a non-compete is inappropriate | The contract; `NDA-01` variant 2.4 |
| **Adviser / professional** | **Professional duties + confidentiality**; a non-compete is normally inappropriate | Advisers serve multiple clients lawfully | The engagement letter; conflicts (`D14`) |
| **Counterparty (commercial)** | Confidentiality + IP position; **no restraint on business** | Restraining a counterparty's business may raise competition-law questions | The agreement |
| **Distributor / reseller / agent** | Territory and activity limits as a **commercial exclusivity term**, not a restraint covenant | Commercial limits are ordinary; restraints on the person are not | The agreement |

## 5. Enforcement and discipline

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 5.1 | **Enforcement is a decision**, taken by the board on counsel's advice, recorded with the assessment §3.12 — **never by an officer acting alone, and never by a letter sent in anger** |
| 5.2 | **Never threaten a covenant the group has not tested.** A threat based on an unenforceable clause exposes the group to a claim and undermines every other covenant |
| 5.3 | **Preserve evidence before acting**: access logs, download records, export records, email records (`D71` §3) |
| 5.4 | **The remedy ladder**: a written reminder citing the clause? → a request for undertakings? → a settlement negotiation? → **only then** an application to a court, with counsel |
| 5.5 | **Do not use a covenant to obstruct a lawful report, a regulator's enquiry or a court** (`NDA-01` §3.18) |
| 5.6 | **Record the outcome** in the litigation and claims register (`D71`) and, if the covenant was found unenforceable, **correct every other instrument using the same wording** (`CROSS_DOCUMENT_CONSISTENCY_REPORT.md`) |

## 6. Decisions required from the user / family / governing bodies

1. Whether the group will use **non-competes at all**, and for which roles (the pack's default is: **no, unless
   counsel advises and a real interest requires it**).
2. The default **period**, **territory** and **activity** definitions — set once, so that drafting is consistent.
3. Whether **garden leave / payment in lieu** is used instead.
4. Whether **notice periods** are lengthened for exposed roles.
5. Whether the group will adopt the **narrower alternatives** as the standard.
6. Who **authorises enforcement**.

## 7. Counsel review note — `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`

1. **Whether restraint-of-trade covenants are enforceable in the relevant jurisdiction at all, and on what
   tests** — this pack **does not** contain that law, and research has **not** been done.
2. Whether the employment regime requires **payment** during a restraint, or limits its duration.
3. Whether a covenant against a **founder who is also a shareholder** is assessed differently from one against
   an employee.
4. Whether **non-solicitation** of employees and customers is enforceable, and the narrowest form that is.
5. Whether a **no-poach** arrangement between the group and another employer raises competition-law risk.
6. Whether any **statutory right** (to report, to work, to a secondary occupation) is infringed by the drafted
   covenants.
7. Whether **severability** would save an over-broad clause, and whether to rely on it.
8. Whether enforcement should be by **injunction**, or whether a **damages claim** is the realistic route.
