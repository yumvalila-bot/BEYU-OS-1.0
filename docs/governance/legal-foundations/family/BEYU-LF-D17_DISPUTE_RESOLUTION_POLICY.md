> **⚠ THE BOUNDARY STATEMENT.**
> This policy governs **family** disputes — disputes between family members and disputes about family
> governance. It **cannot** govern: Trust administration disputes (**`D09` governs — the Trustees, the
> Protector and, ultimately, the court**); disputes under a contract (the contract governs); employment
> disputes (the employer's process and the labour law govern); company-law disputes (the Companies Act and
> the articles govern); or anything that a court has jurisdiction over, **because no family policy can
> take away a person's right to go to court.**
> **Nothing in this policy may be used, or described, as preventing any person from exercising a legal
> right. Any family member who tells another that they "cannot" take a matter to court is misrepresenting
> the position, and that is itself a breach of `D14`.**

## 1. Purpose and principles

**1.1 Purpose.** To prevent disputes where possible; to resolve them early and privately where prevention
fails; to protect the family's relationships, the enterprise, the Trust, the Foundation and — importantly —
the people who work for and depend on them; and to keep disputes from damaging assets, reputation and
health.

**1.2 Principles.**

1. **Early and direct.** Most disputes shrink if two people talk early, in good faith, with the facts.
2. **Facts before blame.** First establish what is actually true and what the documents actually say. Many
   "betrayals" are misunderstandings about what an instrument provides.
3. **Interests, not positions.** Ask what each person actually needs (security, respect, recognition,
   independence, an answer to a fear), not only what they demand.
4. **Proportionality.** The cost of a fight — money, health, relationships, the enterprise's stability —
   is part of the decision, not a footnote.
5. **Confidentiality.** A family dispute is not a public matter. See §7.
6. **Protect the institution and the innocent.** Employees, patients, customers, beneficiaries and the
   Foundation's work must not be collateral damage. Their interests take priority over family convenience.
7. **No person is a party to a dispute merely because they are related.** Only the people actually in
   dispute are parties; the rest of the family are not conscripts.
8. **Legal rights are preserved.** See the boundary statement above.
9. **The line that cannot be crossed:** a dispute may never be resolved by a means that is unlawful, that
   harms the enterprise or the Foundation, that pressures an employee or an officer, or that misleads a
   third party or a regulator.

## 2. Prevention (better than resolution)

**2.1 Instruments, not memory.** Every promise that matters must be written in the correct instrument
(`D11` §10). Most family disputes begin with "but you promised" about something that was never documented,
or was documented in the wrong place. **The Enforceability Verification Statement (`D11` §10.2) is the
family's main prevention tool.**

**2.2 Role clarity.** Every role has a written description, an authority limit, and a term (`D18`).
**Fights over "who decides this" are the most common corporate-family dispute, and they are preventable by
the single matrix in `D27`.**

**2.3 Expectations management.** The family is honest, early and repeatedly about: what family members are
entitled to (**nothing in `D11` §5.2**); what the Trust actually does (`D05`); what the enterprise can
offer (`D15`); and what the Foundation does with its money (`D52`). **Silence and vagueness manufacture
disputes.**

**2.4 Information flow.** Regular, honest reporting (`D08`, `D19`) means fewer disputes built on rumour.
**A rumour that circulates unanswered for a year becomes a grievance.**

**2.5 Conflict management.** `D14`'s conflict ladder resolves the disputes that arise from self-interest,
if it is actually followed. **Recording a conflict and then voting on the matter anyway is worse than not
recording it at all.**

**2.6 Early warning register.** The Family Council maintains a confidential list of **matters at risk of
becoming disputes** (e.g. an unresolved role question, an unclear promise, a business deal between
relatives, a perceived unfairness in support, a generational tension), reviewed at every Council meeting.
**The purpose is to act early, not to build a file against a person.**

**2.7 Structural safeguards.** Certain family decisions are structurally prone to dispute and should be
handled with extra process: money to a family member (documented decision); a business deal with a
relative (arm's length + register); a role given to a relative (merit + independent decision); a personal
loan (written); a promise about the future (in the correct instrument, or not made at all).

## 3. The escalation ladder

| Tier | Name | Forum | Who runs it | Time-box | Binding? | Recorded in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T0** | **Self-help** | The two (or few) people directly involved | Themselves | `[NUMBER]` days | Yes, if they agree a fix | Not recorded formally (see §4.5) |
| **T1** | **Facilitated discussion** | The parties plus a neutral family facilitator (a person the parties trust who is **not** a party and has no interest) | Facilitator | `[NUMBER]` days | No | Private note; recorded only if a settlement is reached |
| **T2** | **Council-assisted review** | The Family Council (excluding any member who is a party or conflicted) | Council Chair | `[NUMBER]` days | **No** — the Council may recommend, not decide | Council minutes (`D12` §4) |
| **T3** | **Mediation** | An accredited/experienced mediator, independent of the family and the enterprise | Mediator | `[NUMBER]` days | **Only if** the parties sign a settlement (see §5.5) | Mediation agreement + settlement |
| **T4** | **Expert determination** | An independent expert (valuation, accounting, technical) | Expert | `[NUMBER]` days | **Only if** the parties have agreed in writing that the determination is binding | Expert's written determination |
| **T5** | **Arbitration** | Per an arbitration agreement the parties have signed (or a clause in an instrument) | Arbitral tribunal | Per rules | **Yes**, if the arbitration agreement is valid and the matter is arbitrable | Award |
| **T6** | **Court** | The competent court | Judge | — | Yes | Court record |

**3.1 The ladder is a default, not a straitjacket.** A party may go straight to a higher tier where:
(a) there is an urgent risk (safety, financial exposure, destruction of records); (b) a limitation period
is about to expire; (c) the other party refuses to participate in good faith; or (d) the matter is not
appropriate for a lower tier (e.g. a serious breach of duty).

**3.2 Time-boxing is mandatory.** **A dispute that will not move is worse than a dispute that is
decided.** If a tier's time-box expires without resolution, the matter **must** escalate, and the failure
to escalate must be reported to the Council. **"We agreed not to talk about it" is not a resolution.**

**3.3 Trust and company disputes go to their own regimes.** A dispute about Trust administration runs
under `D09` (Trustees → Protector → mediation → court). A dispute under a shareholders' agreement runs
under that agreement. A dispute about a director's conduct runs under the Companies Act and the articles.
**This policy's ladder may supplement those regimes, never displace them.**

## 4. Tier 0–T2 in practice

**4.1 The Tier 0 conversation.** Before any escalation, the person raising the matter should put it in
writing (a short note: what happened, what they want, what they have tried) so that the other person can
respond to something concrete. **A verbal grievance repeated to third parties is not a Tier 0 attempt.**

**4.2 The neutral facilitator.** Chosen by the parties, or (if they cannot agree in `[NUMBER]` days) by
the Council from a list of `[NUMBER]` people the family has identified in advance. The facilitator must be
independent of the dispute, must not be a party or a party's spouse/parent/child/sibling, and must not be
a person who benefits from either outcome. **The facilitator's role: help them talk, clarify the facts,
and identify options. Not to decide, not to judge, not to report to the family.**

**4.3 Council-assisted review (T2).** The Council may, at a party's request or on its own initiative:
(a) establish the **facts** (which documents exist; what the instruments actually say — with counsel's
input where the law is involved); (b) clarify the **authority position** (who actually decides this);
(c) recommend a **process**; and (d) recommend a **resolution** the parties may accept. **The Council's
review must be recorded with reasons, and the Council must record what it could not determine.**

**4.4 What the Council may not do at T2.** Decide the dispute; impose an outcome; discipline a party
(that is `D18`); disclose a party's private information to the family; act as a mediator (a Council with a
preferred outcome is not a neutral); or use its position to pressure a party.

**4.5 Records at T0–T2.** Private, in the sense that the family is not told the content. **But:** (a) a
settlement reached must be recorded in writing and signed; (b) the **fact** that a dispute was raised and
resolved is logged (anonymised where possible) so the family learns from patterns; (c) anything requiring
a legal act must be referred to the competent body and recorded there.

## 5. Mediation (T3)

**5.1 When.** Whenever the parties agree, when a tier's time-box expires, and (for family-relationship
matters) as the default before any court proceeding **that the parties are free to mediate**.

**5.2 The mediator.** Independent; experienced in family enterprises or in the relevant subject matter;
**not** a family member, **not** an adviser to any party (unless all parties waive that in writing, which
is weak practice), and not a person with a financial interest in the outcome. Paid by `[WHO PAYS — TO BE
DECIDED: the parties in agreed shares / the family's own budget / the disputing parties]`. **A party must
not be prevented from mediating because they cannot pay** — the family's practice is that the Council
funds or advances the cost where necessary, without strings.

**5.3 The mediation agreement.** Signed before the mediation starts; states: the matter; the mediator's
role and fees; confidentiality; that the process is **without prejudice** (i.e. what is said cannot later
be used against the speaker); that a settlement is only binding once in writing and signed; that the
parties may leave at any time; and the position on cost.

**5.4 Conduct in mediation.** Every party attends (or is represented **with authority to settle** — a
person who cannot settle must not attend in place of the decision maker); discloses the material
documents; and does not misrepresent the law or the facts. **A party who uses mediation as a tactical
delay or to gather information in bad faith breaches this policy.**

**5.5 The settlement.** Must be **in writing, signed by the parties, and state**: what each party will do;
by when; what happens if not; and that each party has had the opportunity to take legal advice. **Where
the settlement requires a legal act** (a share transfer, a release, a variation of an instrument, a
payment from the Trust), the settlement **is not effective until that act is done by the competent body**,
and the settlement must say so. **A settlement that purports to bind the Trustees to a distribution, or a
board to a decision, is ineffective as to that part** and must be re-routed to the correct body.

**5.6 Review.** A settlement involving an ongoing role or an ongoing payment must include a **review
mechanism and an end date.** A settlement with no end date becomes the next dispute.

## 6. T4–T6: expert determination, arbitration and court

**6.1 Expert determination** is suited to a **narrow technical question** (a valuation; an accounting
treatment; an actuarial matter; a technical standard). The parties should agree **in writing, before** the
determination, whether it is binding, and what happens if the expert cannot decide. **It is not suited to
disputes about conduct, relationships, or rights under an instrument** — those need a mediator or a court.

**6.2 Arbitration** is available **only** where the parties have a valid arbitration agreement (or an
instrument contains an arbitration clause). **Do not assume it is available.** Also: **not everything is
arbitrable** — trust administration matters involving minors, unborn persons and incapacity, and the
construction of an instrument, are commonly reserved to the court. `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM — Q-S-06,
D09 §6.2]`

**6.3 Court.** Every person retains the right to go to court (subject to any valid, applicable arbitration
agreement). **The family's position: a court is the right forum where** (a) a person's legal rights need
protection; (b) a fiduciary duty has been breached; (c) an instrument needs construction; (d) a minor,
unborn person or person lacking capacity needs protection; or (e) mediation has failed and the matter
cannot otherwise be resolved.

**6.4 Conduct during litigation.** (a) Tell the family that a dispute has been filed (the **fact**, plus
what the family needs to know — not confidential legal advice); (b) do not use the enterprise's resources
or employees to fight a personal dispute; (c) do not conduct litigation through family meetings; (d)
preserve records (legal hold, `D08` §8.1); (e) do not make public statements about the dispute; (f) where
the dispute affects a company or the Trust, ensure the **body** is properly advised and that its interests
are represented separately from any individual's.

**6.5 Funding a dispute.** A family member's personal legal costs are their own. **Trust, company or
Foundation money may be used only where the body itself is a party, or where the body lawfully decides the
expense is properly incurred in its own interest — and then with a recorded decision, with the reasoning,
and with the costs tracked separately** (`D09` §6.3(2)). **Using the Foundation's charitable funds to
litigate a family dispute is prohibited** (`D51`).

## 7. Confidentiality and reputation

**7.1** Dispute details are confidential: the parties' communications; mediation content; the Council's
private review; legal advice.

**7.2 Permitted disclosures.** To a party's spouse/partner or professional adviser; to the mediator; to
the Council (to the extent necessary for a T2 review); to the body whose decision is required; where the
law requires; and in legal proceedings.

**7.3 Prohibited.** Posting about the dispute; briefing employees, customers, suppliers or the
Foundation's partners; briefing the press; using a family group chat as a forum; soliciting other family
members to take sides in public.

**7.4 The enterprise and the Foundation are not weapons.** Neither may be used to pressure a party: no
withdrawal of an employee's cooperation, no interference with the Foundation's grants, no employee briefed
to influence a family member, no change to a company's operations to harm a party's position.

**7.5 Correcting false statements.** Where a party has made a false public or family-wide statement about
the facts, the other party may ask the Council to record the correct position — **factually, and without
comment on the merits of the dispute.**

## 8. Special situations

| Situation | Required approach |
|---|---|
| **A dispute about a person's family membership** (`FIR-003` unresolved) | This is a **definition** problem, not a case-by-case problem: the family must adopt the definition (`D11` §4.2). Until it does, membership disputes cannot be fairly resolved and must be escalated to the Assembly. |
| **A dispute about a beneficiary's status** | **Trust administration** — `D09` applies. The Trustees determine class membership on the instrument and counsel's advice; the family does not decide it. |
| **A dispute about a distribution** | **Trust administration** — `D09`. The family Council may not decide, and may not promise an outcome. |
| **A dispute about a role or nomination** | `D18` process; removal by the competent body. |
| **A dispute involving a person who lacks capacity** | Involve the person's lawful representative; take legal advice on who may act and how; **never decide the person's rights without proper representation**. |
| **A dispute involving a minor** | Protect the child's interests first and separately from the adults'; take legal advice; keep the child out of the dispute's communications. |
| **A dispute involving a spouse or former spouse** | Take legal advice immediately — matrimonial-property and succession consequences may be involved (`Q-S-04`), and the Trust's position may be affected. |
| **A dispute where a person has a substance or mental-health issue** | Support plus a proper fitness assessment (`D18` §Fitness, `D58`); do not resolve it by gossip or by a secret decision. |
| **A dispute that is also a suspected offence** | Take legal advice; do not "handle it privately" (`D14` §10.3). |
| **A dispute affecting the Foundation's independence** | The Foundation's governors decide for the Foundation; the family may not direct them (`D51`). |
| **A dispute with a third party** | Not this policy's subject; the contract/instrument and its forum govern. Ensure the correct body (the company, the Trust) handles it with its own advice and its own decision. |

## 9. Outcomes, records and learning

**9.1 Every resolved dispute produces:** a written settlement or determination; a record of what instrument
(if any) implements it; the register updates; and — where a **process** failed — a recommended change to
the family's governance.

**9.2 The dispute log.** The Council keeps a confidential log: date opened; parties (or anonymised
category); subject matter; tier reached; outcome; duration; cost; and **the governance lesson**. Reviewed
annually to identify patterns. **Reported to the Assembly in anonymised aggregate only.**

**9.3 No "winning".** The family's stated measure of a good outcome is: the rights of each person are
respected; the facts are established; the enterprise and the Foundation are intact; employees and third
parties are unharmed; and the relationship has the best available chance. **A family member who "wins" a
dispute and loses the family has not won.**

**9.4 No punishment for raising a dispute in good faith.** A person who raises a genuine dispute through
this policy must not suffer: loss of a role; loss of a distribution; exclusion from family events;
withholding of information; or social pressure. **Consequences for breach of a role or a duty are handled
by their own process (`D18`, `D15`, `D03`) — not as retaliation.**

## 10. Decisions required from the user / family / governing bodies

1. Adopt this policy; decide the **T0–T3 time-boxes**.
2. Decide **who pays** for facilitation, mediation and expert determination, and the family's position on
   funding a party who cannot pay.
3. Identify a **panel** of `[NUMBER]` acceptable facilitators/mediators **in advance** (choosing a mediator
   mid-dispute is one of the hardest things a family does).
4. Decide whether the family will use **expert determination** for valuation/accounting disputes, and
   whether it will be binding.
5. Decide the family's position on **arbitration** in any instrument (with counsel).
6. Decide **who may log a dispute** and who may read the log.
7. Decide whether the **Council funds** the family's own dispute-related costs from a family budget.
8. Decide the family's position on **publishing** nothing (recommended) or a limited statement in a
   serious case.
