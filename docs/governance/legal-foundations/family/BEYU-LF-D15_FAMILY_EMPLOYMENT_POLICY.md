## 1. Purpose and the two-key rule for family employment

**1.1 Purpose.** To set out how family members may be considered for, hold, be paid for and exit work in
the enterprise, the Trust, the Foundation or the Family Office — **on terms comparable to those of
non-family employees**, with disclosure of the family relationship.

**1.2 The two-key rule.**

| Key | Holder | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Family key** | The family (Assembly/Council) | May state expectations, recommend, nominate, and set the family's own conditions for supporting a family member's candidacy |
| **Legal key** | The **employing body** (a company's board; the Trustees; the Foundation's governors; the Family Office's legal employer) | Decides whether to employ, on what terms, and whether to terminate — under labour law and the entity's own rules |

**Neither key alone hires anyone. A family decision without a corporate decision employs nobody; a
corporate decision made to accommodate a family wish, without merit, is a governance failure** (and may
be a breach of the directors' duty to the company).

**1.3 The rule in `D11` §9.3, restated.** A family member who works in the enterprise is an employee
like any other: subject to the same policies, the same performance standards, the same disciplinary
process, the same confidentiality, and the same accountability. **Family status is not a qualification,
a promotion criterion, a salary argument, or a protection from dismissal for cause.**

## 2. Recruitment of family members

**2.1 Principles.**

1. **Merit first, openly.** A family member must meet the same published requirement as any external
   candidate for the role.
2. **Disclosure.** In any recruitment process, the family relationship must be disclosed to the decision
   maker and recorded.
3. **No shadow hiring.** A role may not be created or graded to accommodate a person.
4. **No bypass.** The process (advertisement or a documented direct approach, screening, interview, a
   documented decision) must be followed and evidenced — including the reason the family member was
   selected over other candidates.
5. **Independence in the decision.** The decision maker must not be the candidate's parent, spouse,
   sibling, or a person in the candidate's reporting line who would be conflicted; where the recruiter is
   conflicted, an independent decision maker must decide and record the reason.
6. **Nepotism is prohibited** — the family's stated position is that hiring a relative without merit is a
   breach, and where the relative is a director's or officer's close family, the transaction is also a
   related-party matter (`D72`).

**2.2 Process (mandatory steps).**

| Step | Requirement | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Role defined, with a written job description, grade and salary band | Job description, band |
| 2 | Vacancy approved by the employing body's budget/authority | Approval record |
| 3 | Search conducted (advertised, or a documented reasons-recorded direct approach) | Search record |
| 4 | Shortlist includes external candidates **unless** the employing body records why not | Shortlist + reasons |
| 5 | Family relationship disclosed | Disclosure note |
| 6 | Interview panel includes at least one person not related to the candidate | Panel composition |
| 7 | Decision documented with reasons, against the published criteria | Selection note |
| 8 | Terms agreed in writing; **formal contract** executed under the labour law | Contract (`D37` templates; counsel to supply the form) |
| 9 | Background/reference checks in accordance with law | Check records |
| 10 | Conflict/related-party declaration | `D14`, `D72` |
| 11 | Onboarding: policies, confidentiality, IP/non-compete undertakings, data-protection duties | Signed onboarding pack |

**2.3 Where the family member proposes a role that does not exist.** The correct route is: propose the
role to the employing body as a business case, on its merits, with a budget and a market comparison; **not**
to create a job for a person. **If the business case is approved, the role is then filled competitively.**

**2.4 Next-generation placements.** Internships, apprenticeships and graduate placements are permitted
under `D16`, on these conditions: (a) a written programme with defined learning objectives; (b) a
supervisor who is not a family member; (c) a defined duration and a stipend/salary that complies with law;
(d) a written assessment; (e) **no automatic conversion into permanent employment** — that requires the
normal recruitment process.

**3.3 Family-specific clauses (the family's position, to be made lawful by counsel).**

1. **Family relationship disclosed in the contract** (so that there can be no later argument that it was
   hidden).
2. **Explicit acknowledgement that family status gives no special right** to promotion, salary,
   termination protection, or access to information about the family or other employees.
3. **Conflict and recusal duties** as in `D14`, with the person excluded from decisions about their own
   engagement.
4. **Confidentiality extended to family information** (the employee's access to family matters through
   the relationship, not through the job).
5. **IP assignment** covering all work product, including inventions made in the course of duty.
6. **No use of insider information** for personal or family dealings, and no dealing in any family
   security or asset on the basis of it.

**3.4 Consultants and contractors.** A family member engaged as a consultant rather than an employee must
still have: a written agreement; a defined scope and deliverables; a market-based fee with evidence; a
conflict declaration; an IP assignment; and a **classification check by counsel** — because
**mischaracterising an employment relationship as a consultancy can create tax, social-security and
labour exposure** (`Q-TAX-03`, `Q-LEG-04`).

## 4. Performance, promotion and remuneration

**4.1 Performance.** Every person, family or not, has: written objectives; at least `[FREQUENCY]`
documented reviews; a performance rating; and a development plan. **A family member must not be exempted
from review — exemption is the single most corrosive practice in family enterprises**, because it destroys
the credibility of the performance system for everyone.

**4.2 Ratings.** `[SCALE — USER TO SUPPLY]`. The rating for a family member must be **calibrated** with at
least one person outside the family's line of influence, and the calibration must be recorded.

**4.3 Promotion.** By the same criteria and process as for any employee, with the family relationship
disclosed and the decision maker independent where the person is promoted over others. **Where two
candidates are comparable, the family's stated preference is for the candidacy that strengthens the
institution** — and the reason must be recorded either way.

**4.4 Remuneration.** Paid within the published band for the grade, benchmarked to the market
`[BENCHMARK SOURCE — COUNSEL/ADVISER TO CONFIRM]`. **The family's stated position: no family premium and
no family discount** — the pay must reflect the role. Any deviation requires: an independent market
assessment; a written justification; the board's approval without the affected person present; and a
related-party register entry (`D72`).

**4.5 Bonuses and incentive pay.** (a) Paid against documented, pre-agreed metrics; (b) not paid as a
discretionary family allocation; (c) where an ESOP or equity award is involved, the corporate process in
`D24` applies (issue/allotment, resolutions, filings, tax) — **and a family member benefiting is a
related-party matter.** **`[TAX TO CONFIRM]`** the treatment of any equity award.

**4.6 Family loans, advances and guarantees to an employee.** A loan to a family-member employee is a
related-party transaction: written, on arm's-length terms, security considered, independently approved,
and registered (`D72`). **The Trust must not fund it as a distribution to disguise salary** (`D05`).

**4.7 Expenses and perquisites.** Expenses are reimbursed on receipts, per a published policy, with
approval by a person other than the claimant. **No family member may approve their own expenses, or the
expenses of their spouse, child or parent.**

## 5. Conduct, conflict and discipline

**5.1** `D14` applies to every family-member employee, in addition to the employer's own policies.

**5.2** The employer's disciplinary process applies **without exception** — including to a family member
who is also a shareholder's relative or a director's child. **Where a family member's conduct requires
discipline, the process must be run by a person independent of the family**, and the outcome recorded.

**5.3** Grounds for discipline (employment-law version to be supplied by counsel): misconduct; poor
performance; breach of the employer's policies; breach of confidence; conflict of interest; dishonesty;
harassment; health-and-safety breach; failure to follow a lawful instruction.

**5.4** Prohibited practices in family-member discipline: (a) informal "family" resolution of a serious
breach, including breach of law; (b) removal from a role without a documented process; (c) delaying an
investigation to avoid embarrassment; (d) letting a shareholder's or director's preference determine the
outcome; (e) protecting a family member from consequences that would apply to any other employee.

**5.5** Where a family member is disciplined, the **family** may not overrule or dilute the employer's
decision — it may only consider consequences for **family roles** under `D18`, which is a separate
question with a separate process.

## 6. Exit, resignation and dismissal

**6.1** Every exit — voluntary, mutual, retirement, redundancy, or dismissal — must be: documented;
compliant with the labour law; and processed through payroll with the correct settlement of salary,
accrued leave, statutory contributions, terminal benefits (as required) and tax withholding.

**6.2 Exit checklist `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM each]`.**

| # | Step | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Notice given/received per contract and law | Employer + employee |
| 2 | Final settlement calculated (salary, leave, benefits, notice or pay in lieu, gratuity if applicable) | Finance + HR |
| 3 | Tax and statutory contributions withheld and remitted | Finance |
| 4 | Company property, documents, devices, keys, cards returned — recorded | HR |
| 5 | **Access revoked** (systems, accounts, premises, banking if applicable) | IT + HR |
| 6 | Confidentiality and IP obligations restated in writing | Legal/HR |
| 7 | Non-compete/non-solicitation position confirmed as lawful and reasonable | Counsel |
| 8 | Handover document signed | Manager + employee |
| 9 | Certificate of service / reference (as the law requires) | HR |
| 10 | Records retained per the retention schedule | HR |
| 11 | **If family member: family role consequences considered separately under `D18`** | Family Council |
| 12 | Any dispute: grievance/appeal route offered | HR |

**6.3 Retirement and succession of a family-member employee.** The family's expectation is that a
family member in a **senior** role prepares a successor and a written handover **before** an anticipated
exit (see `D58`). **A role must not be left vacant because the holder is a family member and no one has
raised it.**

**6.4 "Family business" must not displace employment law.** Where the family and the employee agree a
mutually agreed separation, it must be documented as such, in writing, with the employee's informed
consent, and with counsel's confirmation that the agreement is lawful and that the payments are taxable
correctly. **A handshake is not a settlement.**

## 7. Non-family employees and fairness to them

**7.1** The family's stated commitment: **non-family employees must not be disadvantaged by the presence
of family employees.** Concretely: the same policies; the same performance standard; the same
opportunities to be promoted into senior roles; and **a credible route to raise a concern about a family
member's conduct** without fear of the family's displeasure.

**7.2** Every enterprise must have: a written grievance procedure; an anti-harassment policy; an
anti-discrimination position; and a confidential reporting channel that does **not** route a complaint
about a family member to that family member.

**7.3** Where a non-family employee raises a good-faith concern about a family member, **the concern must
be investigated by a person independent of the family**, and the outcome recorded. **Retaliation is
prohibited and is treated as a serious breach.**

**7.4** The family's own internal governance must never be used to handle an employment matter, because
doing so deprives the employee of their legal rights and remedies.
