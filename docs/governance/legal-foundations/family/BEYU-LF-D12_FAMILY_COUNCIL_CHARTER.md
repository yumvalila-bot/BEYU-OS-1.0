---

## 1. Purpose and character

**1.1 Purpose.** To constitute the family's standing governance body: the body that prepares family
decisions, maintains the family's own records, carries the family's voice to the bodies it does not
control, and develops the next generation.

**1.2 Character.** The Council is a **family body**. It is **not** a board, not a trustee, not a
committee of any company, not a governing body of the Foundation, and not a legal representative of any
person.

**1.3 The four limits (mandatory).**

| # | Limit | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **L-1** | **No authority over the Trust.** | The Council may not instruct a Trustee, request a distribution on the family's behalf as of right, or represent that it speaks for the Trust. It may **petition** the Trustees in writing, and it may receive reports the Trustees choose to give. |
| **L-2** | **No authority over any company.** | The Council may not direct a director, requisition a board decision, or speak for a company. Directors owe their duties to the company, not to the Council. |
| **L-3** | **No authority over the Foundation.** | The Council may not direct a grant, approve a grant, or represent the Foundation. The Foundation's governors are independent and must act for its purpose (`D51`). |
| **L-4** | **No authority over the Family Office or the family's assets.** | The Council sets the family's **governance agenda**; it does not manage assets, custody, accounts or payments. Those run on written mandates from the competent body (`D34`–`D37`). |

**1.4 AI has no seat.** NOELIA_AI or any other AI system may prepare agenda packs, drafts and analyses
**for** the Council. It may not vote, hold a seat, be counted for quorum, sign, or have its output treated
as a Council decision (`FIR-017`).

## 2. Powers (what the Council may actually do)

| # | Power | Nature | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|---|
| P-1 | Convene and prepare the Family Assembly; set its agenda | Procedural | Notice + agenda recorded (`D13`) |
| P-2 | Prepare and circulate draft family policies for Assembly decision | Recommendation | Draft + Council minute |
| P-3 | Recommend candidates for family roles (Council, representatives, family-enterprise liaison) | Recommendation | Candidate file + selection criteria + conflicts |
| P-4 | Recommend candidates and nominees to the Trustees, a board, or the Foundation **for their own consideration** — never as an appointment | Recommendation only | Written referral; the receiving body's own process and resolution |
| P-5 | Receive and consider reports the Trustees, a board, the Foundation or the Family Office **choose to give** | Receipt | Report + minute |
| P-6 | Make written representations on behalf of the family | Representation | Mandate from the Assembly where the matter is material |
| P-7 | Maintain the family's records, the family register of roles, and the family's versions of the Constitution and policies | Administration | Registers maintained (`D19`) |
| P-8 | Commission (within a budget the family approves) advice on family governance, education and mediation | Administrative | Budget approval + engagement record + conflict check |
| P-9 | Administer the family's dispute process up to mediation (`D17`) | Procedural | Record; neutrality confirmed |
| P-10 | Monitor the family's own compliance with `D11` and report to the Assembly | Oversight of the family's own conduct | Annual report |

**2.1 Powers the Council does NOT have.** To decide distributions; to appoint or remove a Trustee; to
appoint or remove a director; to instruct a board; to approve a Foundation grant; to direct the Family
Office to pay anything; to bind the family legally; to sign on behalf of the Trust or a company; to
approve any spending of Trust or company money; to amend this Charter alone; to suspend a family member's
legal rights; to enter litigation on the family's behalf without a specific Assembly mandate.

**2.2 The "recommend, don't instruct" phrasing rule.** Every Council communication to a Trustee, board,
Foundation governor or the Family Office must use the words **"the Council recommends"** or **"the
Council requests that the receiving body consider"**. **A Council communication that reads as an
instruction is defective and must be corrected on the record**, because it may mislead a third party about
who decides.

**3.1 Eligibility.** A member must be an Adult Family Member (definition per `FIR-003`) who: has signed
the `D14` conduct and conflict declaration; is not subject to an unresolved serious-breach finding under
`D11` §11; and is willing to comply with the limits in §1.3.

**3.2 Diversity and generational balance.** The Charter's intent is that each family branch be
represented, and that the next generation hold at least one seat. **The family must decide how to
implement this where branches or generations differ in size** — a rule that is not decided in advance is
the most common cause of family-governance deadlock.

**3.3 Conflicts and recusal.** `D14` applies. A Council member with a personal interest in a matter must
disclose and abstain from the vote, and (for a material interest) withdraw from the discussion. **A
conflicted member cannot count towards the quorum for that matter.**

**3.4 Removal of a member.** On the grounds in `D18` (absence; serious breach; loss of eligibility; a
written request by `[THRESHOLD]` of Adult Family Members). Removal is by the Family Assembly. The member
is entitled to notice, to a response, and to have their statement recorded. Removal from the Council
**does not** affect their rights as a Family Member (§1.3 does not extend to membership) and does not
affect any legal office they hold elsewhere, which is governed by the relevant instrument.

## 4. Meetings and decisions

**4.1 Frequency.** At least `[NUMBER]` times per year, and on the requisition of `[NUMBER]` members or the
Chair.

**4.2 Notice and papers.** Notice and the agenda at least `[NUMBER]` days before; papers at least
`[NUMBER]` days before. **A matter not on the agenda may be discussed but may not be decided** except by
unanimous consent of the members present, recorded.

**4.3 Quorum.** `[NUMBER]` members, none of whom is conflicted on the matter being decided. If quorum
fails, the matter is deferred; the deferral and its reason are recorded. **A deferred matter must not be
allowed to become urgent by neglect** — the Chair must escalate to the Assembly if a matter cannot obtain
quorum twice.

**4.4 Decisions.** By `[MAJORITY / CONSENSUS / SUPERMAJORITY — TO BE DECIDED]`. Decisions are
**recommendations** unless this Charter expressly says otherwise. **The Council has no decision that binds
a legal entity.**

**4.5 Minutes.** Every meeting produces minutes recording: date, attendees, apologies, conflicts declared,
matters discussed, decisions (with the wording), the votes, the reasons, actions with owners and dates,
and deferred matters. Minutes are circulated within `[NUMBER]` days and approved at the next meeting.
Adopted minutes are the record (`D19`).

**4.6 Written resolutions.** Permitted where each member signs; the resolution must record that it was
circulated in advance and whether any member objected.

**4.7 Openness.** An Adult Family Member may attend part of a Council meeting by invitation. **A closed
session is permitted only for the grounds in `D11` §7.4**, and the fact of the closed session and its
approved ground must be recorded (the content need not be).

**4.8 No shadow decisions.** Where members meet informally, the minute must record: **"informal
consultation of [names] — not a Council decision."** Any resulting proposal must be brought to the proper
forum. See `D11` §7.5.

## 5. The Council's working relationship with other bodies

### 5.1 With the Trustees

| The Council may | The Council may not |
|---|---|
| Write to the Trustees setting out the family's view, with reasons | Instruct the Trustees how to exercise a discretion |
| Ask the Trustees for information the Trustees are willing to give | Demand information the Trustees are entitled to withhold (`D08` §6.3) |
| Recommend a candidate as a Trustee **for the Trustees'/Protector's consideration** | Appoint or remove a Trustee |
| Ask to be consulted on a matter affecting the family | Veto a Trustee decision |
| Report a concern about the Trust's administration | Investigate the Trustees; or obtain the Trust's books |

**5.1.1** Where the Council believes a Trustee has acted improperly, the route is: (a) put the concern in
writing to the Trustees; (b) if unresolved, to the Protector; (c) if unresolved, take legal advice — and
only a person with standing may take the matter to court (`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` who has standing).
**The Council must not launch a public campaign, must not solicit a Beneficiary to sue, and must not
represent that the Council has decided the Trustees were at fault.**

### 5.2 With company boards

| The Council may | The Council may not |
|---|---|
| Route the family's shareholder-level views through the proper channel (the members, via the shareholder of record — the Trust — and through the Trustees as shareholder) | Contact a director to direct a decision |
| Ask a board (through the proper channel) to consider a matter | Requisition a board meeting |
| Recommend a candidate for a role **to the body that appoints** | Appoint a director or officer |

**5.2.1 Directors' duties notice.** A director who accepts a direction from the Council may breach their
duty to the company. **The Council must state in every such communication that the board's decision is the
board's alone**, and must not create pressure that a director could mistake for an instruction.

### 5.3 With the Foundation

| The Council may | The Council may not |
|---|---|
| Learn about the Foundation's purpose and priorities | Direct or veto a grant |
| Suggest a cause the family cares about for the Foundation's consideration | Represent that the Foundation will fund it |
| Nominate a family member **for the Foundation's own appointment process** | Appoint a Foundation governor |
| Support the Foundation's fundraising voluntarily | Commit family money |

**5.3.1 Private-benefit warning.** The Council must never propose, and the Foundation must never
consider, a grant whose **real** purpose is to benefit a family member, a family business, or the family's
lifestyle. That is a private benefit and is prohibited (`D51`, `D52`).

### 5.4 With the Family Office

| The Council may | The Council may not |
|---|---|
| Ask the Family Office to prepare reports and analyses for the family | Direct the Family Office to execute a transaction, pay money, or hold assets |
| Agree the family's own budget for family activities, and instruct the Family Office to administer **that** budget under a written mandate | Instruct the Family Office about Trust, company or Foundation money |
| Receive the family's own reports | Receive, on the family's behalf, information about another person's private affairs |

**5.4.1 Money boundary.** Any payment from Trust or company money requires the decision of the body
competent to make it, recorded under that body's process. **A Council resolution is never a payment
authority** (`D35`).

## 6. Council duties and standards

**6.1 Duties.**

1. **Represent the family's long-term interest, not your own.** Declare interests; abstain; do not use
   the seat for personal advantage.
2. **Prepare properly.** Read the papers; obtain the advice needed; do not decide blind.
3. **Record honestly.** Minutes must record what was actually decided, including the objections.
4. **Communicate.** Tell the family what was decided and why, within `D19`'s limits.
5. **Respect the boundaries** in §1.3 and §5.
6. **Protect confidentiality** — family and personal information, and the enterprise's confidential
   information, are not the Council's to share.
7. **Develop successors.** Each member is expected to help prepare the next generation (`D16`).
8. **Act within the family's competence** — refer legal, tax and technical matters to professionals.
9. **Do not misrepresent the family's position to any person.**
10. **Resign if you cannot act properly** — incapacity, conflict, or inability to give the time.

**6.2 Standards of conduct.** `D14` applies in full: confidentiality, conflicts, no use of position for
personal benefit, no harassment, no retaliation against a person who raises a concern, honesty in
communications, and a duty to correct a misleading statement promptly.

**6.3 Liability and protection.** Council members act in a **family** capacity and owe **no** fiduciary
duty to the Trust, the companies or the Foundation **by virtue of Council membership alone**. They may,
however, incur liability if they: hold another office (as a director, trustee or Foundation governor);
sign a contract personally; commit a wrong; or breach a contract. **Council membership does not confer
indemnity, and no Trust or company money may be used to defend a Council member personally** unless the
competent body lawfully decides otherwise and records the basis. `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`

## 7. Records, confidentiality and reporting

**7.1 Records.** The Council maintains: this Charter; the family register of roles and terms; minutes and
written resolutions; the family's policy library (its own drafts and adopted policies); the Enforceability
Verification Statement (`D11` §10.2); the family dispute log (`D17`); and the family's correspondence
with the Trustees, boards and the Foundation. Storage and access per `D19`.

**7.2 Annual report to the Family Assembly.** Content: what the Council did; what it recommended and what
happened to each recommendation; the family's compliance with `D11`; the dispute log's summary; the
Enforceability Verification Statement; the next-generation development report; the family budget's
outturn; and the risks the Council sees.

**7.3 Confidentiality.** Council deliberations are confidential to the family, **except**: (a) where a
member needs to consult their own professional adviser (who is bound by their own duty); (b) where the law
requires disclosure; (c) where a matter must be referred to the Trustees, a board, the Foundation or a
mediator; and (d) where a member must disclose information in legal proceedings. **A member must not
disclose another member's personal or health information to the family without that person's consent.**

## 8. Control-plane interaction

**8.1** The control plane contains governance models of bodies, memberships, charters, meetings and
resolutions. **They are records.** A `governance body` record named "Family Council" does not create the
Council; an appointment row does not elect a member; a resolution row does not decide anything.

**8.2 Mapping (record-keeping only).**

| Charter element | Suggested record | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Council composition | Body + membership records with terms | A role grant is access, not office |
| Meeting | Meeting record + attendance | Attendance is not a vote |
| Decision (recommendation) | Resolution/recommendation record with `type = RECOMMENDATION` | **Must be typed as a recommendation, never as a binding resolution** |
| Conflict | Declaration record attached to the item | Recording does not manage; recusal does |
| Referral to a Trustee/board | Correspondence record with `referral_ref` | The receiving body's own decision is the decision |

**8.3 Forbidden usages.** (a) Representing a control-plane "APPROVED" state as a Council decision;
(b) configuring a software role as "Family Council authority" over Trust or company data in a way that
implies legal authority; (c) auto-generating minutes and treating the generated text as adopted without
human approval; (d) using an AI system to cast, count or infer a vote.
