> **⚠ THE MOST IMPORTANT STATEMENT IN THIS DOCUMENT.**
> **This Constitution is not a statute, not a trust deed, not a company's constitutional document, and
> not a court order.** It binds nobody in law by itself. A clause of this Constitution has **legal**
> effect only if, and only to the extent that, it is also placed in an instrument that does have legal
> effect — a trust deed, a company's articles or memorandum, a shareholders' agreement, an employment
> contract, a lease, or another signed contract. **Every clause below is set out with its
> "Enforceability" status on the Enforceability Map in §10, and no clause may be quoted as binding
> without that status.**
> This Constitution also **cannot** override the Trust instrument, company law, any contract, any
> court order, or any mandatory rule.

## 1. Purpose

**1.1** The family intends to hold and steward its enterprise and assets across generations, in a manner
consistent with its values, with the law, and with the interests of the people who depend on those
assets.

**1.2** This Constitution records how the family will work together: how it decides, who is eligible to
participate, how it grows the next generation, how it resolves disagreement, how it communicates, and how
it will behave towards the enterprise, the Trust, the Foundation and the people who work for them.

**1.3** This Constitution exists **in addition to** the legal instruments, not instead of them. It
governs the family's **conduct and process**. It does not govern the Trust's administration (the Trustees
do, under the Trust instrument) and it does not govern the companies (the boards do, under company law
and the articles).

## 2. What this Constitution is and is not

| It **is** | It **is not** |
|---|---|
| A statement of the family's shared commitments and expectations | A legal instrument that transfers property |
| A governing procedure for family meetings and family bodies | A trust deed, articles of association, or shareholders' agreement |
| A source of mandates for the family's representatives | A power of attorney or an authority to act for the Trust or a company |
| A way to make decisions transparent and reviewable | A way to make decisions legally effective |
| A statement of ambition for the next generation | An enforceable promise of employment, office, money or a distribution |
| A living document, amended by family procedure | A contract that can be enforced in court (except where §10 says so) |

**2.1 The two-key rule.** Any family decision that requires a legal act (a signature, a resolution, a
filing, a payment, a transfer) needs **both**: (a) the family's proper process under this Constitution;
**and** (b) the legal act by the body competent to take it. **The family's process is not the legal act,
and the legal act is not the family's process.** Every procedure in this pack that has legal consequences
is written this way.

## 3. Values, purpose and guiding principles

**3.1 Brand principles (as recorded in the BEYU identity architecture).** **Trust. Care. Integrity.
Innovation. Impact.** Family motto: ***"Bridging Care. Building Trust."***

**3.2 What the values mean operationally.** Values are only real if they change decisions. Accordingly:

| Value | Operational commitment | How a breach is recognised |
|---|---|---|
| **Trust** | We do what we said we would do, and we say so when we have not. We keep promises about money, time and access. | An unkept commitment is raised at the next family meeting, and recorded — not excused. |
| **Care** | We consider the effect of our decisions on family members, on employees and on the communities the enterprise serves. | A decision taken without considering an obvious effect on people is a defective decision. |
| **Integrity** | We do not misrepresent the family's or the enterprise's legal position; we do not present software records, drafts or aspirations as legal facts. | Misstating a legal position to a family member is a serious breach. |
| **Innovation** | We invest in learning and in the enterprise's capabilities; we accept failure in experiments that were honestly run. | Punishing an honest failed experiment is a breach of this value. |
| **Impact** | We measure the enterprise and the Foundation by what changed for the people they serve. | Claiming impact without evidence is a breach. |

**3.3 Purpose of the family in relation to the enterprise.** (Draft — the family must own this text.)

> To build and steward enduring enterprises and institutions that serve people well — with care at the
> centre and trust as the foundation — while developing each generation's capacity to carry that
> responsibility honestly, competently and together.

**3.4 The family's responsibilities to non-family stakeholders.** The family recognises that its
enterprises employ people, serve patients and customers, and affect communities; and that the Foundation
holds funds for public benefit. **The family's convenience is never a justification for harming those
interests.** Where a family convenience and a stakeholder interest conflict, the conflict must be named
and decided openly, with reasons recorded.

## 5. Rights and entitlements: an honest statement

**5.1 What Family Members have.** Under this Constitution, Adult Family Members have: the right to
attend and speak at the Family Assembly; the right to vote on Constitutional matters if eligible
(`D13`/`D18`); the right to information about family governance (subject to `D19`); the right to be
considered for family roles under `D18`; the right to raise a concern (`D17`); and the right to see this
Constitution and the records of family decisions.

**5.2 What Family Members do NOT have (critical).** Membership of the family does **not** give any person:

(a) a right to a distribution from the Trust — that is the **Trustees'** discretion (`D05`);
(b) a share, option or interest in any company, or a right to any company's assets;
(c) a right to employment, a job, a title, a salary or a consultancy;
(d) a right to represent the family, the Trust or any company;
(e) a right to give instructions to the Trustees, a board, the Foundation or the Family Office;
(f) a right to any specific asset;
(g) a right to see another person's personal, health or financial information;
(h) a right to veto a decision made by a body competent to make it;
(i) a right to be a member of any family body;
(j) a right to keep participation in family governance if they breach `D14`/`D18`.

**5.3 The honesty duty.** No person may tell a Family Member that they are "entitled" to anything that
§5.2 excludes. **Misrepresenting a family member's legal position — particularly to a young adult, a
spouse, or a person planning their life around an expectation — is a serious breach of this
Constitution.** Where a family member asks "what will I receive?", the correct answer is to direct them to
the Trustees' disclosure policy (`D08` §6.3) and to the true position.

## 6. Family bodies and how authority flows

**6.1 The four family bodies.**

| Body | Role | Authority | Governed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Family Assembly** | The family's general meeting: adoption and amendment of this Constitution, election of the Family Council, ratification of family-level decisions | The family's own governance (no legal authority over any legal entity) | `D13` |
| **Family Council** | The family's standing governance body: prepares decisions, maintains the family's records, liaises with the Trustees and the boards, recommends candidates | **Recommendation and liaison only** | `D12` |
| **Family Office** | The shared execution and administration capability for the family's wealth and governance | Acts only within written mandates; owns nothing; decides nothing that belongs to the Trustees | `D31`–`D38` |
| **Family dispute forum** | Mediation/escalation of family disputes | Non-binding, except where a settlement is signed | `D17` |

**6.2 The authority boundary statement (mandatory reading).**

1. The Family Assembly and the Family Council have **no authority over the Trust, any company, or the
   Foundation**. They may **recommend**; they may not **instruct**.
2. The **Trustees** decide Trust matters. Their duty runs to the Beneficiaries and the Trust, not to the
   family as a body. A family vote that contradicts a Trustee's duty is **irrelevant to the law**.
3. The **boards** decide company matters, subject to company law and the articles. A family vote cannot
   bind a director's statutory duty.
4. The **Foundation's** governors decide Foundation matters, and must act for its charitable purpose.
   Family wishes are relevant only as considerations they may weigh, never as instructions (`D51`).
5. The **Family Office** acts only under written mandate. **It is not a governing body and holds no
   fiduciary power.**
6. **NOELIA_AI and any other AI system has no authority.** It may advise, analyse and draft. It may not
   decide, vote, approve, instruct, sign or represent the family. (`FIR-017`.)

**6.3 The "speak with clarity" duty.** A family representative who speaks to a Trustee, a board, the
Foundation, an employee or an outsider must state clearly **whether they speak (a) for themselves,
(b) with the Family Council's mandate, or (c) with a Family Assembly resolution** — and must not imply
authority they do not have. **A person who claims family authority they do not hold commits a breach of
this Constitution**, and the family must correct the record promptly.

## 7. Decision-making, information and transparency

**7.1 Decision categories.**

| Category | Example | Decided by | Process |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Constitutional** | Amend this Constitution; adopt the family definition; change the participation rules | Family Assembly, in person or by written resolution | `D13`; supermajority `[ ]` |
| **Governance** | Elect the Family Council; approve the family's annual plan for its own activity; appoint a mediator | Family Assembly | `D13` |
| **Preparation/recommendation** | Recommend a candidate for a role; recommend a policy position to the Trustees; prepare the family's view on a matter | Family Council | `D12` |
| **Execution (mandated)** | Act as the family's representative in a defined matter with a written mandate | The mandated person | `D18` |
| **Legal** | Trust distributions; company resolutions; filings; contracts; IP assignments; Foundation grants | **The competent legal body only** | The relevant instrument |

**7.2 Cooling-off and conflict rules.** `D14` applies to every family decision. A person with a personal
interest in a family decision must disclose it and abstain from the vote where the interest is material.

**7.3 Records.** Every family decision must be recorded in writing with: the matter, the decision, the
voters, the interests disclosed, the reasons, and the effective date. Records are kept per `D19`.
**A decision with no record did not happen for these purposes.**

**7.4 Transparency, and its limits.** The family's default is to tell Adult Family Members what was
decided and why. Limits: another person's personal/health/financial data; privileged legal advice;
information whose disclosure would create a real risk to a person's safety; and information the law or a
regulator restricts. **A decision to withhold requires a recorded reason and an annual review.**

**7.5 No secret side decisions.** A family matter may not be decided in a private meeting of some members
and presented as a family decision. **Where a subset meets, it must say so in the record** ("informal
consultation of X, Y, Z — not a Family Assembly decision") and the matter must be brought to the proper
forum. This is the single most common family-governance failure and is expressly prohibited here.

## 8. Relationship to the legal instruments

**8.1 Feed-in duty.** The Family Council must identify, for every family decision that requires a legal
act, **which instrument and which body** must implement it, and must refer it there in writing with the
supporting record.

**8.2 No back-door amendment.** The family may not achieve by resolution what the instruments prohibit.
Examples of prohibited back-door actions: resolving that a Trust distribution will be made (the Trustees
decide); resolving that a person will be a director (the members/board appoint); resolving that the
Foundation will fund a family project (the Foundation's governors and the purpose rule decide); resolving
that a company will buy something (the board decides); resolving to change a company's articles (the
members decide by special resolution under the Act).

**8.3 Consistency with the instruments.** Where this Constitution and an instrument conflict, **the
instrument prevails**, and the conflict must be reported to the Family Council and logged.

**8.4 Which instruments carry which family rules.** See the Enforceability Map in §10. The family's
representatives must, at least annually, confirm that every rule the family relies on as binding has in
fact been embedded in the correct instrument and is still in force.

## 9. The family and the enterprise

**9.1 The family's expectations of the enterprise.** The family expects the enterprise to be run
professionally, lawfully, honestly, and in the interests of the company (not of a particular family
member); to be financially disciplined; to treat employees fairly; to be transparent with the family **as
shareholders** through the proper channel; and to pursue the brand principles in §3.

**9.2 The family's restraint.** The family will not: approach employees of the enterprise to influence a
commercial decision; use the family's position to obtain a commercial advantage for a family member;
leak information; use the enterprise's resources for private purposes; or undermine a board decision in
public.

**9.3 Family members who work in the enterprise.** They are subject to `D15` (employment policy) and to
the enterprise's own policies, exactly like any other employee. **Family status is not a qualification
and does not reduce accountability.**

**9.4 Family members who wish to start a business.** Any business that could compete with, supply, or
transact with the enterprise must be disclosed to the Family Council and to the relevant board, and must
be conducted on arm's-length terms, with conflicts disclosed (`D14`). **The family supports enterprise;
it does not permit a family member to use inside information or inside relationships.**

**9.5 The Foundation.** The family accepts that the Foundation is **separately governed** and that its
assets are for its charitable purposes. Family members serving as Foundation governors must act for the
Foundation's purpose, disclose family interests, and recuse where required (`D51`). **No family
resolution may direct a Foundation grant.**

**10.1 The rule that flows from this map.** Where a family rule matters legally, **the family must
instruct counsel and have it embedded** — and until that is done, the family must not rely on it, tell
anyone it is binding, or discipline anyone for breaching a "binding" rule that is not binding.

**10.2 Annual verification.** The Family Council shall produce an annual "Enforceability Verification
Statement" listing each row of §10, its status, whether the embedding has happened, and where the
instrument can be found (`D74`). **This statement is part of the annual governance review (`D75`).**

## 11. Breach of this Constitution

**11.1 Categories.**

| Category | Example | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Procedural** | Failing to record a decision; failing to disclose a conflict | Remedy: re-take properly; recorded |
| **Substantive** | Acting outside the family's authority; misrepresenting the family's position to a Trustee or a third party; undermining a board decision in public; using family information for personal gain | Remedy: corrective action, apology, restoration, and where the person holds a family role, consideration of removal under `D18` |
| **Serious** | Misrepresenting a legal position to a family member; improperly seeking to influence a Trustee or governor; concealing a conflict that affects a legal act; retaliating against a person who raised a concern | Remedy: removal from family roles; referral under `D17`; and, where the conduct breaches a contract or a duty of an office, the consequences of that contract or office (which may include legal action) |

**11.2 What the family may NOT do in response to a breach.** It may not: withhold a Trust distribution as
a punishment (**the Trustees' discretion cannot be used as the family's sanction**); remove a person from
a company office except by the proper body's process; fine a person; restrict a person's legal rights;
or punish a person for seeking legal advice or reporting a concern in good faith.

**11.3 No consequence may override law.** Where a breach is also a breach of law or of an office's duty,
the matter must go to the body and the adviser competent to deal with it.

## 12. Adoption, amendment and review

**12.1 Adoption.** This Constitution is adopted when: (a) the Family Assembly approves it by
`[SUPERMAJORITY — TO BE DECIDED]` of Adult Family Members present in person or by written resolution;
(b) the Founder and each Adult Family Member who wishes to be recorded as a party signs an adoption page
or a separate adoption instrument; and (c) the Family Council records the adoption in the family records.

**12.2 Signature is a commitment, not a contract.** Signing this Constitution **does not** create legal
obligations for those who sign, except: (a) where a clause is expressly stated to operate as a contract
between the signatories **and** counsel has confirmed it will be enforceable; and (b) to the extent a
signatory separately signs a legal instrument giving effect to a clause. **The signature page must say
this.**

**12.3 Amendment.** Amended by Family Assembly resolution by `[SUPERMAJORITY]`, with: (a) the proposed
amendment circulated at least `[NUMBER]` days before; (b) the Family Council's reasoned recommendation;
(c) counsel's advice on any legal consequence; and (d) the record of who voted and how. **Entrenched
clauses:** §5.2 (no entitlement), §6.2 (authority boundaries), §7.5 (no secret decisions), §9.5
(Foundation independence) and §10 (the Enforceability Map) may be amended only by `[HIGHER THRESHOLD —
TO BE DECIDED]` and with counsel's confirmation that the amendment does not purport to override an
instrument.

**12.4 Review.** Annually, and immediately on: the Founder's death or incapacity; a generation change; the
addition of a family branch; a material change in the family's assets or enterprise; a serious dispute;
or a change in the law affecting any instrument.

**12.5 Relationship to the law.** If a clause is or becomes unlawful or unenforceable, the rest stands;
the clause is **not** to be interpreted to defeat a mandatory rule, a court order, or a third party's
rights.
