> **⚠ THE DEFINITION DEPENDENCY.**
> These rules determine **who may participate in family governance** — which depends entirely on **who is
> in the family**, which is **`FIR-003`: UNRESOLVED**. Therefore:
> **these rules cannot be applied until the family adopts its definition.** Any attempt to apply them
> beforehand would decide family membership by accident, person by person, in the most contested way
> possible. The correct sequence is: **adopt the definition → then apply these rules.**
> **And separately:** participation in family governance **confers no right to money, an asset, a role in
> the enterprise, or any authority over the Trust, a company or the Foundation** (`D11` §5.2).

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

**1.1 Purpose.** To define: who may attend, speak and vote in family governance; who may hold a family
role; what authority a family representative actually has; how authority is granted and withdrawn; how a
role ends; and how all of this is recorded.

**1.2 Scope.** Applies to: Family Assembly and Family Council participation; family roles (Chair,
Secretary, representative, liaison, nominee, working-group lead); and family mandates to represent the
family. It does **not** govern: trusteeships (`D03`), directorships (`D24`), Foundation governorships
(`D49`), employment (`D15`), or any other legal office — each of which has its own instrument and its own
rules.

**1.3 The separation rule (restated because it is the most frequently breached).**

> **Family eligibility ≠ legal office.** Satisfying these rules makes a person eligible **for family
> roles only**. A legal office (Trustee, director, Foundation governor, officer, signatory) requires the
> relevant instrument's own process — appointment by the competent body, the eligibility rules of that
> instrument, any regulator filing, and (for a Trust office) the deed's independence and conflict rules.
> **The family cannot appoint anyone to a legal office, and this document must never be read as authority
> to do so.**

**2.1 The policy decisions the family must make.** Categories 5 and 6 (spouses, partners, former spouses,
widows/widowers) always cause difficulty because the family definition (`FIR-003`) does not settle them.
**Decide now, in principle, and not in the middle of a family crisis.** Options: (a) spouses may attend
but not vote; (b) spouses may attend at the invitation of the Chair; (c) spouses are not admitted to the
Assembly but are invited to social and educational events; (d) a distinction between current and former
spouses. **Whatever is chosen, the family must be able to explain why, and must apply it consistently.**

**2.2 A person who is not on speaking terms with the family.** They remain entitled to the record (`D19`)
and to be heard in any matter about them (`D17`), and may be represented at mediation. **Estrangement
does not remove a person's legal rights or their entitlement to notice.** It may (by family decision,
recorded) suspend their attendance at family social events.

## 3. Eligibility to vote (`D18` in action)

**3.1 A person may vote if they are:** within the adopted family definition; of full age and legal
capacity; not subject to a **voting suspension** under §3.3; and not conflicted on the matter (see §3.4).

**3.2 Capacity.** A person who lacks capacity to make the decision does not vote. **The family may not
determine capacity** — capacity is determined as the law provides (`D03` §6.2 applies by analogy;
counsel to confirm). A person acting under a power of attorney or as a guardian may **not** exercise the
principal's vote unless the instrument validly confers that power and counsel confirms it. **Do not
assume a guardian's vote counts.**

**3.3 Voting suspension (`[TO BE DECIDED — is this available at all?]`).** A person's vote may be suspended
while: (a) they are subject to a documented serious-breach finding (`D11` §11) **and** the finding was
made after an opportunity to respond; or (b) they have an unpaid, undisputed, court-ordered obligation to
the family body or to a family member arising from a family matter. **Suspension requires** a written
decision, reasons, a time limit, and a review date; and **may be appealed** under `D17`. **Suspension
never removes** the person's right to notice, to be heard, to the record, or to any legal right, and it
must not be used to permanently exclude a person from the family. `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM that a suspension
provision is lawful and does not constitute unlawful oppression or an invalid penalty.]`

**3.4 Conflict.** A person with a material personal interest in the matter votes **not at all** and
withdraws from the discussion (`D14` §5.3). **A vote taken by a conflicted person does not count**, and
the minute must record that fact.

**3.5 Weighting.** **One adult family member, one vote** (the family's default proposal). Any weighting
(e.g. by branch, by generation, by contribution) must be expressly adopted, recorded, and applied
consistently; and **it cannot change anyone's legal rights** — a weighted family vote still cannot bind
the Trust, a company board, or the Foundation.

## 4. Family roles: what they are, and what they are not

**4.1 The roles.**

| Role | What it is | Authority | Term |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Family Council Chair** | Chairs Council and Assembly meetings | Procedural | `[TERM]` |
| **Family Council member** | Serves on the Council (`D12`) | Recommendation and liaison only | `[TERM]` |
| **Secretary** | Keeps the family's records and minutes | Administration | `[TERM]` |
| **Family representative** | Carries a **specific, written mandate** to a body or a third party for a **defined matter** | **Only what the mandate says** | Until the matter ends or the mandate is revoked |
| **Liaison (Trustees / board / Foundation)** | A channel for information between the family and a body | Receive and report; **no authority over the body** | `[TERM]` |
| **Nominee for a legal office** | A person the family **recommends** for a Trustee/director/governor role | **None until the competent body appoints them, and then only what the instrument gives** | Per the instrument |
| **Working-group lead** | Leads a family project group | Per the group's terms of reference | Project duration |

**4.2 The mandate instrument (required for any representation).** A family representative acts only under
a **written mandate** (Form F-18 below) which must state: the matter; the body/person to whom the person
may speak; the permitted positions (or the requirement to consult before agreeing anything); the expiry;
and that the mandate **confers no authority over the Trust, any company or the Foundation**.

**4.3 What a representative may never do, even with a mandate:**

1. Bind the family, the Trust, a company, the Foundation, or any person to a legal obligation.
2. Sign a contract, an instrument, a filing or a share document on behalf of a legal entity.
3. Instruct the Trustees, a director, the Foundation's governors, or the Family Office.
4. Promise money, a role, a distribution, a grant or an appointment.
5. Represent that the family has decided a matter that it has only discussed or recommended.
6. Waive a person's legal right.
7. Communicate a legal or tax position as settled without advice.

**4.4 The hat rule.** A person holding both a family role and a legal office must **state which hat they
are wearing** in every relevant communication, record it, and **not use the family role to influence the
legal office's decision, or the legal office to favour a family outcome.** Where the two conflict, the
legal office's duty prevails, and the person must disclose the conflict and (where required) recuse.

**4.5 Removal from a family role.** Grounds: loss of eligibility; resignation; failure to attend
`[NUMBER]` consecutive meetings without leave; a documented serious breach of `D14`; a failure to perform
the role's duties; a conflict that cannot be managed; or a written request by `[THRESHOLD]` of Adult
Family Members. **Process (mandatory):** written notice of the ground and the evidence; an opportunity to
respond in writing and to be heard; a decision by the Family Assembly (or, for a working-group lead, by
the Council) with reasons recorded; the person's written statement attached to the record; and a review
route under `D17`. **Removal from a family role does not remove any legal office, and vice versa.**

**4.6 "Fitness" concerns.** Where the family believes a person is not able to perform a family role (for
example because of illness, addiction, or a pattern of harmful conduct), the process is: (a) a private
conversation offering support; (b) with the person's agreement, a temporary step-back with a review date;
(c) where there is no agreement and the risk is serious, the §4.5 process with reasons recorded and
support offered. **The family may NOT declare a person "unfit to hold a legal office" and act on it** — the
legal office is governed by its own instrument (and, for a Trust, by `D03` §6, which sets out the
incapacity route and requires proper evidence and a recorded declaration).

## 5. The register of roles and mandates (`D66` interface)

**5.1 Contents.**

| Field | Note |
|---|---|
| Party reference (GlobalUserID) | The identity key; **not** a name string |
| Role | Council member / Chair / Secretary / representative / liaison / nominee / working-group lead |
| Basis of eligibility | The adopted definition category (`FIR-003` decision reference) |
| Appointment/election reference | Assembly or Council decision reference |
| Term start / end | — |
| Mandate (for representatives) | Matter, permitted positions, expiry, revocation reference |
| Conflicts declared | Reference to the `D14` declaration |
| Standing in the **legal** registers | Cross-reference to `D66`'s legal-office fields (`D03`/`D24`/`D49`), kept **distinct** from family roles |
| End of role | Date, ground, decision reference |
| Review date | Annual |

**5.2 The separation field.** The register must keep **family roles** and **legal offices** in **separate
fields**. Merging them is a defect, because it invites the belief that a family role confers a legal
office.

**5.3 No entitlement field.** The register must contain **no** field that records or implies a right to
money, an asset or a benefit. (See `FIR-009`, `FIR-010`.)

**5.4 Access.** `HIGHLY_RESTRICTED`; no public or open repository (`D70`, `D75`).

2. Decide the **age of adulthood** for governance purposes.
3. Decide the **spouse/partner/former-spouse** attendance policy (§2.1).
4. Decide whether **voting suspension** is available at all, and if so, its grounds, limits and appeal
   (`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` first).
5. Decide the **voting basis**: one member one vote, or weighted (and if weighted, how).
6. Decide the **term lengths** and whether consecutive terms are permitted.
7. Decide the **attendance requirement** that triggers removal.
8. Decide who keeps the **register of roles and mandates**, and who may read it.
9. Decide whether family representatives must report back in writing (recommended: yes).
