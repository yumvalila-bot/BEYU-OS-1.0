> **Standing warning.** This Code is a **family** code. Binding legal obligations arise only where a
> person signs a **contract** that says so (for example a director's or employee's undertaking) or holds
> an office to which duties attach by law. **No family member may be disciplined under this Code for
> breaching a duty that does not legally exist** — and equally, **holding a family role may be made
> conditional on agreeing to this Code** (that is the Code's real enforcement mechanism).

---

## 1. Purpose and how this Code works

**1.1 Purpose.** To state the conduct the family requires of anyone acting for the family or holding a
family role — and to make conflicts of interest, confidentiality and honesty **procedural**, not merely
aspirational.

**1.2 The three enforcement mechanisms (in ascending order of strength):**

| Mechanism | What it does | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Role condition** | Holding a family role (Council, representative, liaison, nominating role) is conditional on a signed undertaking to this Code. Breach → the role may be removed under `D18`. | Family roles only |
| **2. Contract** | Where the person also signs a contract (employment, consultancy, directorship, mandate), the contract can incorporate the relevant parts of this Code, with contractual consequences. | Anyone who signs |
| **3. Law** | Where the conduct is also a breach of a **legal duty** (director's duty, fiduciary duty, confidentiality obligation, data-protection duty, employment-law obligation, criminal law), the legal consequences apply **independently of this Code**. | By law, not by this Code |

**1.3 The honest limitation.** This Code cannot force anyone to be decent. What it can do is: make the
family's expectations explicit; require disclosure; require recusal; require reasons to be recorded; and
make a role terminable where the family's conditions are not met. **The family should not pretend
otherwise.**

**1.4 No AI exemption.** Nothing in this Code is satisfied by an AI system's assurance. **A person is
responsible for what they sign, certify or approve, whether or not an AI system produced the draft.**
(`FIR-017`.)

## 2. The values in conduct terms

| Value | What it requires of a person holding a family role | What it forbids |
|---|---|---|
| **Trust** | Do what you said; report when you cannot; do not overstate your authority. | Misrepresenting your authority; concealing a failure to act. |
| **Care** | Consider effects on family members, employees, patients/customers and the community; treat people with dignity. | Indifference to foreseeable harm; humiliating or excluding a person without cause. |
| **Integrity** | Tell the truth about legal, financial and factual positions; distinguish facts from drafts, assumptions and aspirations; correct an error immediately. | Presenting a draft, an assumption, a seed record, an AI output, or a software state as a legal fact. |
| **Innovation** | Support honest experimentation; learn from failure; invest in capability. | Punishing an honest failed experiment; rewarding a false success. |
| **Impact** | Judge results by what changed for the people served; use evidence. | Claiming impact without evidence; inflating results in a report to the family. |

**2.1 The "state the source" rule.** In any paper, message or meeting, a person must say whether what they
are presenting is: **a fact from a document** (say which); **an assumption**; **a draft**; **a proposal**;
**an expectation**; or **an AI output**. This single rule prevents most family-governance misinformation.

## 3. Conduct rules (apply to anyone holding a family role)

**3.1 Honesty and accuracy.** Do not mislead another person about: the family's or the enterprise's legal
position; the status of a document; the authority of a person; the state of the accounts; or the outcome
of a decision. **Correct an inaccurate statement you have made, promptly and to the same audience.**

**3.2 Authority discipline.** Act only within the authority you actually hold (§`D18`). Do not say or
imply that you speak for the Trust, a company, the Foundation or the family unless you hold that mandate.
**State which hat you are wearing.**

**3.3 Dignity and respect.** No harassment, intimidation, bullying, discrimination or retaliation — in
family meetings, in the enterprise, or online. **Retaliation against a person who raises a concern in good
faith is a serious breach.**

**3.4 Diligence.** Read the papers; attend; prepare; declare what you do not know; ask for the advice you
need. **Do not vote on a matter you have not understood, and do not sign what you have not read.**

**3.5 Confidentiality.** See §4.

**3.6 No personal advantage from a role.** Do not use a family role, family information, or the
enterprise's resources or information for personal gain. See §5.

**3.7 Financial integrity.** Do not: misappropriate funds; make or receive an unauthorised payment; falsify
a record; approve your own reimbursement; or sign a false certificate. **These are also, in most
circumstances, offences and breaches of fiduciary duty — the legal consequences are independent of this
Code.**

**3.8 Records integrity.** Do not alter, delete or back-date a record, an account, a minute or an
instrument. Corrections are made by a **recorded correction** (reversal, not overwrite). See `D08` §8.

**3.9 Pluralism and fair process.** Do not exclude a family member from information or process to gain an
advantage. Do not convene a "quiet" decision and present it as a family decision (`D11` §7.5).

**3.10 Substance and mental health.** The family recognises that illness, addiction, grief, incapacity and
financial distress affect conduct and judgement. **The correct response is support plus a proper
assessment of fitness to hold a role** — not gossip, exclusion, or a secret decision. `D18` §Fitness
applies, and `D56`/`D58` govern continuity of legal roles.

**3.11 Digital conduct.** Do not: disclose family or beneficiary information in a chat group or on a
social platform; forward papers to persons not entitled to them; use a personal device or account for
confidential documents where the family's access rules apply; or post about family disputes, the
enterprise's confidential matters, or the Foundation's beneficiaries. **Social media is not a family
channel.**

**3.12 Speaking publicly.** Only a person with a specific mandate may speak publicly for the family, the
enterprise or the Foundation. **A person speaking publicly must state their capacity.** Where a family
matter is contentious, the family's public position — if any — is set by the body competent to set it, not
by the loudest voice.

## 4. Confidentiality

**4.1 What is confidential.** (a) Family-internal matters (minutes, papers, disputes, financial support,
health and personal circumstances); (b) the Trust's affairs (assets, distributions, beneficiary data);
(c) the enterprise's confidential information (commercial, technical, financial, personnel); (d) the
Foundation's data (beneficiaries, donors); (e) legal advice; (f) third parties' information.

**4.2 The exceptions (permitted disclosures).** (a) To a professional adviser, to the extent necessary and
where the adviser owes a duty of confidence; (b) where the law or a regulator requires; (c) where
necessary in legal proceedings; (d) with the subject's informed consent; (e) within the family, subject to
§4.3.

**4.3 Limits even within the family.** A person may not disclose **another person's** health, financial or
personal information to the family without that person's consent, except to the extent the family's
decision genuinely requires it (and then it goes into a restricted annex, per `D19`).

**4.4 Duration.** Confidentiality continues after a role ends and after a person ceases to be a family
member. It is not released by the passage of time, by a family disagreement, or by the person becoming
estranged.

**4.5 Control of documents.** Confidential documents are held only in the governed repositories and stores
with access control — never in public folders, public repositories, or share links without access
control. `D19`, `D70` and `D75` apply.

**4.6 The remedy.** For a breach: removal from family roles; the contractual consequences if a contract
exists; and — where the information is protected by law (data protection, trade secret, employment duty)
— the legal remedies available to the person or body that holds the right. **The family must take advice
before pursuing a member; the object is protection, not punishment.**

## 5. Conflicts of interest

### 5.1 The three-question test

1. **Am I, or is a person connected to me, going to benefit?**
2. **Do I owe a duty to someone whose interest may differ from the family's?** (A company; a beneficiary;
   the Foundation's purpose; an employer.)
3. **Would a reasonable person think my judgement could be affected?**

If any answer is yes (or "maybe") → **declare it.**

### 5.2 The declaration duty

| Situation | What to do | Where recorded |
|---|---|---|
| Standing interests (directorships, shareholdings, offices, businesses, family relationships) | Declare on appointment and annually | Register of Declared Interests (`D14` annex / `D73`) |
| A new interest arising (a business started, a role accepted, a contract signed) | Declare within `[NUMBER]` days | Same, with an update entry |
| An interest in a **specific matter** | Declare before the discussion begins; state the nature and extent | Minute of the meeting + the decision record |
| An interest you are unsure about | **Declare it anyway and say you are unsure** | Same |

**5.2.1 Undeclared is worse than conflicted.** The family's rule: *a declared conflict is a governance
issue; a concealed conflict is a breach of trust.*

### 5.3 The management ladder

| Level | Test | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Recorded** | Interest is trivial or conceptual (a distant relationship, a small holding) | Disclose; record; participate permitted |
| **2. Restricted** | Interest could influence judgement but the person's expertise is needed | Disclose; do **not** vote on that matter; the discussion may continue with the person present **only if** the meeting resolves and records that it is appropriate; withdraw before the vote |
| **3. Recused** | A material interest, or a transaction with the person's own business, or a decision about the person's own role, remuneration or conduct | Disclose; **withdraw entirely** from discussion and vote; must not be counted in quorum |
| **4. Prohibited** | An undisclosed interest; or a transaction that gives a person an undisclosed benefit; or a self-dealing transaction not on arm's-length terms | **Must not proceed**; must be referred to the competent body and, if a legal office is involved, to counsel |

### 5.4 The standing conflict situations the family must address in advance

| Situation | The trap | Required control |
|---|---|---|
| A family member is a **director** and another family member wants a contract with the company | Directing a director is not the family's power; a related-party contract needs proper process | Disclose; the board decides on arm's-length terms; the related director recuses; record under `D72` |
| A family member **works** for the company and is also on the Council | Council access to enterprise information | Separate the roles; the Council does not receive confidential employee/HR data; the person recuses from Council discussions about their own employment |
| A family member **is a Trustee** and also a **beneficiary** | Bias to own benefit | Disclose; recuse where the decision benefits them; the deed's independence rule applies |
| A family member is a **Foundation governor** and a family business wants Foundation funds | Private benefit | **Never**; the Foundation's purpose rule and `D51` prohibit it; recusal plus an independent decision |
| A family member **supplies** goods or services to the family, the Trust, a company or the Foundation | Self-dealing | Written agreement; competitive evidence; arm's-length terms; independent approval; register entry (`D72`) |
| A family member **lends** money to the family or the enterprise | Terms and security | Written loan; independent advice on terms; disclosure; no special influence |
| A **Family Office** employee is a family member | Access and self-dealing risk | Separation of duties; independent approval of anything benefiting them; `D35` controls |
| A family member learns **inside information** about an enterprise | Trading or competitive misuse | Confidentiality; no dealing on the information; the enterprise's own insider rules apply |

### 5.5 Conflicts at the level of legal bodies

Where the conflicted decision belongs to a **legal** body (a board, the Trustees, the Foundation's
governors), the **instrument's and the law's conflict rules prevail** over this Code. This Code's ladder
is the family's own discipline; the legal body must follow its own rules (see `D04` §4 for the Trust, and
`D26`/`D27` for companies). **Where the two differ, the stricter applies, and the difference is logged.**

## 6. Use of the family name, brand and information

1. **No one may register** a company, trade mark, domain name, social account or business name using the
   BEYU name or a confusingly similar name without written authority from the body that holds or stewards
   the brand (`D47`).
2. **No one may** use the family's name to obtain credit, or to induce a person to act.
3. **No one may** describe themselves as a representative of the family, the Trust, a company or the
   Foundation without the underlying authority.
4. Family information may not be used in a prospectus, pitch, application or public statement without the
   authority of the body whose information it is.

**6.1 Why this rule exists.** A family name is a licence others rely on. Unauthorised use creates liability
for the family and for the enterprise, and can mislead the very people the enterprise serves.

## 7. Gifts, hospitality and benefits

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| A gift or hospitality of nominal value from a counterparty | Permitted; declare if it could be seen to influence a pending decision |
| Cash, or a benefit that looks like a bribe or a facilitation payment | **Prohibited.** Never acceptable. Report it. |
| A payment requested by a public official | **Prohibited.** Report to the competent body and take legal advice. |
| A gift to a person in a family role from someone seeking a benefit | Declare; the family may require the person to decline or to return it |

**7.1 Facilitation payments are not a family practice.** There is no "small payment" exception. Where a
payment is demanded, the correct response is to refuse, document, and escalate.

## 8. Reporting concerns (whistleblowing)

**8.1** A person who reasonably believes that a family member, an officer, an employee or an adviser has
acted unlawfully, dishonestly, or in a way that harms the family, the Trust, a company, the Foundation or
a stakeholder, **should report it** — to the Chair, the Chief Governance Officer function, the Protector
(for Trust matters), the Foundation's chair (for Foundation matters), or a director (for company matters),
as appropriate.

**8.2 Protection.** A good-faith reporter must not be subjected to retaliation, exclusion, loss of role,
withholding of a benefit, or social pressure. **Retaliation is a serious breach of this Code.**

**8.3 Not a channel for personal grievances.** Personal disputes between family members follow `D17`.
**Abuse of the whistleblowing route to harm another person is itself a breach.**

**8.4 Anonymity.** Where a report is made anonymously, it must still be looked into, **but no decision may
be taken against a person on the basis of an anonymous allegation alone** — the substance must be
independently established.

**8.5 Duty to report** where the law requires (for example, where a person is a director obliged to report
a breach, or where a data breach must be notified). **This Code does not replace a legal duty to report.**

## 9. Control plane, AI and record discipline

**9.1** Any information a person enters into the control plane about another person (a conflict, a dispute,
a health note) must be **necessary**, **accurate**, **access-controlled**, and entered only in the
governed register. **No gossip logs. No "watchlists" outside a governed process.**

**9.2** AI systems may be used to draft, summarise and analyse. **A person remains fully responsible for
what they sign, certify, approve or send, including AI-generated content.** An AI output may not be
presented as a person's determination, as legal advice, or as an established fact.

**9.3** Any AI system used with family or beneficiary data must comply with the data-protection framework
(`D70`) — including that a **cross-border transfer** by an AI or cloud provider may require a permit
(`Q-DP-02`). **Do not paste personal family data into an external AI service.**

## 10. Breach handling

**10.1** A reported breach is handled as follows: (a) recorded; (b) assessed by the Council (or, for a
legal-office matter, referred to the competent body); (c) where serious, referred under `D17`; (d) decided
with reasons, after an opportunity to respond; (e) recorded.

**10.2** Consequences may include: a recorded correction; an apology; a restoration; removal from a family
role (`D18`); referral to the competent legal body; and — where a contract, a duty or the law is breached
— the legal consequences of that instrument or law. **The family may not impose a fine, withhold a
person's legal rights, or use the Trust's discretion as a sanction.**

**10.3** Where the conduct may be a **crime**, the family must take legal advice; **the family must not
"handle it privately" to avoid embarrassment.** Concealing a suspected offence so that the family is not
embarrassed is itself a serious breach.

## 11. Decisions required from the user / family / governing bodies

1. **Adopt** this Code (and decide whether Council members, representatives and nominees must sign it as a
   condition of the role).
2. Decide the **declaration period** and the **annual declaration date**.
3. Decide the **reporting route** for concerns and who receives them.
4. Decide the family's position on **gifts and hospitality** thresholds.
5. Decide the family's position on **social media** for minors and next-generation members.
6. Decide whether the family will **contractually incorporate** parts of this Code into employment,
   directorship and mandate documents (recommended — this is what makes it enforceable).

6. Whether **data-protection registration** is required for the family's conflict, dispute and conduct
   records (`Q-DP-01`), and the lawful basis for processing them.
7. Whether any family consequence for a breach could constitute **oppression, defamation, restraint of
   trade, or an unlawful penalty**.
8. Whether the **duty to report suspected offences** exists in the applicable law and how the family
   should document its response.

**This Code binds nobody. It has not been adopted or signed. Its only mechanism is that family roles may
be made conditional on it, and that contracts may incorporate it.**
