> **Standing warning.** A Family Assembly decision is a **family** decision. It creates **no** legal
> obligation, **no** entitlement and **no** authority over the Trust, any company, the Foundation, or any
> asset. The Assembly may **decide family matters** and **recommend** to legal bodies. It cannot decide
> legal matters.

---

## 1. Purpose, scope and the two kinds of family meeting

**1.1 Purpose.** To define how the family meets, decides, records and reports — so that family decisions
are legitimate, transparent, reviewable and consistent with `D11`.

**1.2 The two kinds of meeting (never to be confused).**

| Type | What it is | Who attends | Can it decide? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Family Assembly** | The family's general meeting. **The only family forum that can decide Constitutional and governance matters** (`D11` §7.1). | Adult Family Members; invited observers | Yes — **family matters only** |
| **Family meeting / branch meeting / working session** | A discussion forum: branch discussions, education sessions, project groups, informal consultation | Whoever is invited | **No.** It may recommend and prepare; **it may not decide Constitutional or governance matters, and its conclusions must be labelled "not an Assembly decision"** |

**1.3 The one rule that prevents most family-governance disputes:** *if it is not recorded as an Assembly
decision, it is not an Assembly decision.* See `D11` §7.5.

---

## 2. The Assembly's competence (and its limits)

**2.1 The Assembly may decide (family matters):** adoption and amendment of the Family Constitution
(`D11` §12); adoption of family policies and codes (`D14`–`D19`); election and removal of Family Council
members (`D12` §3.4, `D18`); approval of the family's own activity plan and budget; approval of the
family's dispute process; adoption of the family definition (`FIR-003`); any other family-internal matter
not reserved to another body.

**2.2 The Assembly may recommend (to legal bodies):** candidates for the Trustees' or Protector's
consideration; candidates for company directorships (to be appointed by the competent body under the
articles/Act); candidates for Foundation governors (to be appointed by the Foundation's own process);
policy positions for the Trustees' or a board's consideration; a distribution policy view for the
Trustees' consideration.

**2.3 The Assembly may NOT decide (prohibited list):**

1. Any Trust distribution, or a person's beneficiary status.
2. The appointment or removal of a Trustee or the Protector.
3. Any company resolution, class-right variation, share transfer, issuance, valuation, dividend or
   director appointment.
4. Any Foundation grant, or the Foundation's use of its funds.
5. Any instruction to the Family Office to pay money or hold assets.
6. Anything that amends, overrides or contradicts a legal instrument.
7. Anything that reduces a person's legal rights, or imposes a fine or a punishment with legal effect.
8. Anything addressed to a third party as though it were the family's legal act.

**2.4 Referral duty.** Where a matter is on the prohibited list, the Assembly must **refer it**, in
writing, to the competent body, recording: the matter; the family's view and reasons; the body to which it
is referred; and the date. The referral is **a request, not an instruction**, and the receiving body's
decision is its own.

## 3. Who may attend, speak and vote

| Category | Attend | Speak | Vote |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adult Family Member** (definition per `FIR-003` and `D18`) | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ (subject to `D18` restrictions) |
| Family Member under the age of adulthood | ✔ where invited (and always at the education session) | ✔ at the discretion of the Chair | ✘ |
| Spouse/partner of a Family Member | ✔ where invited (`D18` — the family must decide the policy) | ✔ at the discretion of the Chair | ✘ |
| Next-generation observer | ✔ | ✔ | ✘ |
| Family Council member | ✔ | ✔ | Per their own status as a Family Member |
| Trustee, director, Foundation governor, Family Office principal, adviser | ✔ **on invitation**, for the relevant item | ✔ | ✘ |
| A person subject to a dispute under `D17` | Per §6.3 of this document | Restricted | Excluded from the affected vote |
| **Any AI system, bot or automated agent** | ✘ | ✘ | ✘ (may prepare materials only — `FIR-017`) |

**3.1 The family must decide:** the age of adulthood for governance purposes; whether spouses/partners may
attend; whether attendance is a **right** or an **invitation**; and how a person who is not on speaking
terms with the family may still receive the record.

**3.2 Attendance is not authority.** Attending the Assembly gives no authority to act for the family, the
Trust, a company or the Foundation, and confers no office.

## 4. Convening: notice, agenda and papers

**4.1 Annual Assembly.** Held at least once each year, at a date set by the Family Council with at least
`[NUMBER — e.g. 30]` days' notice.

**4.2 Special Assembly.** Convened by the Council, or on the written requisition of `[NUMBER or %]` of
Adult Family Members. A requisition must state the purpose; the Council must convene within `[NUMBER]`
days.

**4.3 Notice must contain:** date, time, venue or link; the agenda; the papers; the draft resolutions (if
any); the voting requirements; and the attendance and proxy rules.

**4.4 Papers.** Distributed at least `[NUMBER]` days before. Every paper must state its **source**, its
**status** (draft / for decision / for information / requiring advice) and, where it concerns a legal
instrument or a legal position, its **verification status** in the pack's terms. **A paper must never
present a draft, an assumption, a software record or an AI output as an established legal fact.**

**4.5 Agenda discipline.** An item may be **decided** only if it was on the agenda, or if `[%]` of the
Adult Family Members present agree to add it and the addition is recorded.

**4.6 Quorum.** `[NUMBER or %]` of Adult Family Members, present in person, by proxy or by approved
electronic means. **If quorum fails twice on the same matter, the Council must report to the family why
the matter cannot obtain quorum**, and the matter must not be decided by a smaller group (that is exactly
the failure this rule exists to prevent).

**4.7 Attendance by electronic means.** Permitted where the participant can hear and be heard
simultaneously. The record must state who attended how. `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` whether any instrument
(e.g. a shareholders' agreement or articles) requires physical presence for a specific act — **the
Assembly's own rules cannot override an instrument's requirements.**

**4.8 Proxies.** Permitted with a written proxy specifying the matter(s), the direction (if any), and its
expiry. A proxy may not be exercised on a matter the proxy-holder is conflicted on. **No blanket or
open-ended proxy.** `[The family must decide whether proxies are permitted at all.]`

## 5. Conduct of the meeting

**5.1 Chair.** The Family Council Chair chairs; in their absence, the Assembly elects a chair for that
meeting. The Chair's role is procedural: to keep order, ensure everyone is heard, ensure the record is
accurate, and ensure decisions stay within §2.

**5.2 The Chair must stop an unlawful or out-of-scope decision.** If the Assembly is about to "decide" a
matter in §2.3, **the Chair must stop it, state why, and record the referral instead.** If the Chair fails
to do so, the decision is **not an Assembly decision** and must be recorded as a "family discussion — no
decision taken", and the matter referred.

**5.3 Order of business (default).** Welcome and apologies → conflicts declared → minutes of the last
meeting → matters arising → reports (Trustees, boards, Foundation, Family Office — as given) →
next-generation report → decisions and recommendations → any other business → close and record.

**5.4 Conflicts.** Every attendee declares any interest in any item. A person with a **material** interest
in a decision must withdraw during that item and must not vote. `D14` applies. The minute records the
declaration and the withdrawal. **A conflicted vote is recorded as such and does not count.**

**5.5 Voting.** By show of hands (or a recorded electronic equivalent) unless a **secret ballot** is
requested by `[NUMBER]` members **before** the vote. The record states the result, and — if any member
requests it — how each member voted. **Default: how each member voted is recorded for Constitutional
matters and for the removal of a family officer, and may be recorded for others on request.**

| Matter | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Ordinary family matter | `[MAJORITY]` |
| Constitutional amendment (`D11` §12.3) | `[SUPERMAJORITY]` |
| Amendment of an entrenched clause (`D11` §12.3) | `[HIGHER THRESHOLD]` |
| Adoption of the family definition | `[SUPERMAJORITY]` |
| Removal of a Council member | `[SUPERMAJORITY]` |
| Recommendation to the Trustees/board/Foundation | `[MAJORITY]` |

**5.7 The fail-closed rule.** Where a threshold has not been supplied, **the higher threshold applies**,
and a matter may not be decided by a simple majority.

**5.8 Good faith and no coercion.** No member may be pressured, threatened, bribed, or subjected to a
consequence (including a threat about money, a role, a distribution or access to the family) for how they
vote or speak. **Any such conduct is a serious breach under `D11` §11 and must be raised under `D17`.**

**5.9 Minutes.** Taken by the Secretary; circulated within `[NUMBER]` days; corrections recorded; approved
at the next meeting (or by written confirmation). **Minutes must record: attendance; conflicts; the exact
wording of each decision; the votes; the reasons; referrals; actions with owners and dates; and any
matter deferred with its reason.**

**5.10 Recording standards (mandatory).** Minutes and resolutions must be written so that a reader who was
not present can tell: **what was decided; what was only recommended; what was referred; what was
deferred; and what had no decision at all.** A minute that blurs these is defective.

## 6. Disputes at the Assembly

**6.1** A procedural dispute (whether a matter is in scope; whether quorum exists; whether a vote was
properly taken) is decided by the Chair, **subject to** the right of `[NUMBER]` members to require the
matter to be referred to mediation under `D17` **before** the affected decision takes effect.

**6.2** A dispute about a person's **eligibility to attend or vote** is referred to the Family Council for
a written determination, which the Assembly may overturn by `[SUPERMAJORITY]` — but where the dispute is
about the **family definition** itself, the matter must be resolved by adopting the definition
(`FIR-003`), not case by case.

**6.3** **Conflict of interest in a dispute.** A person who is a party to a dispute may not chair the item,
may not vote on any family decision about the dispute, and must withdraw during the discussion of their own
matter. **They retain their right to be heard** through `D17`.

**6.4** **No "star chamber".** The Assembly may not decide a complaint against a family member without:
notice of the allegation; the evidence; an opportunity to respond; and a record of the decision with
reasons. `D18` §Discipline applies.

**6.5** A dispute may not be used to delay a legally required act (a filing, a filing deadline, a court
date, a regulatory response). **The competent body must act on time, and tell the family what it did.**

## 7. Decisions that require immediate implementation

Where the Assembly's decision is implemented by a **recommendation** to a legal body, the Secretary must:

1. send the recommendation to the receiving body in writing, within `[NUMBER]` days;
2. state clearly that it is a recommendation, and identify the Assembly resolution it comes from;
3. record the referral in the family records and in the decision register (`D73`) as a **referral**;
4. report back to the family at the next Assembly **what the receiving body decided** — and, where the
   receiving body declined, that it declined. **Reporting "the family decided" when the body declined is
   a misrepresentation and is prohibited.**

## 8. Emergency family meetings

**8.1** Where an event makes waiting impracticable (a death, incapacity, a safety issue, an urgent
reputational or legal exposure), the Council may convene an emergency meeting on `[SHORT NOTICE —
NUMBER]` hours' notice, and may act with a reduced quorum of `[NUMBER]` **for urgent procedural steps
only** (convening, information-sharing, referral, and the appointment of a mediator).

**8.2** An emergency meeting **may not**: amend the Constitution; remove a Council member; adopt or change
the family definition; decide any legal matter; or purport to bind anyone. Those require a properly
convened Assembly.

**8.3** Emergency actions are recorded and **ratified or corrected** at the next Assembly, on the record.
See also `D56`/`D62` (the founder-continuity and crisis-authority framework, which governs **legal**
emergency authority — a different thing from an emergency family meeting).

---

## 9. Records, privacy and confidentiality

**9.1** Records are kept per `D19` and are `RESTRICTED`. **Minutes may not contain another person's health,
financial or personal information beyond what the decision requires**; where such information is
necessary, it goes into a restricted annex, referenced but not quoted.

**9.2** A Family Member is entitled to the minutes and resolutions. **A person who is not a Family Member
is entitled only to what the Council decides, or to what a court requires.**

**9.3** **Do not publish** family minutes, papers, or decisions on any public or open channel. **No share
links, no public repositories, no unsecured cloud folders** (`D70`, `D75`).
