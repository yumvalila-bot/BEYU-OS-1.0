## 1. Purpose, philosophy and the honest limits

**1.1 Purpose.** To prepare the next generation to hold responsibility well — as family members, as
stewards of assets, as potential trustees, directors and governors, and as people. This framework covers:
education support; financial literacy; governance education; mentorship; work exposure; stewardship of
assets and the brand; and leadership development.

**1.2 Philosophy (the family's stated position).**

1. **Preparation, not entitlement.** The purpose of development is capacity, not a claim on family money.
2. **Effort is expected.** Education support is conditional on genuine engagement and honest reporting.
3. **Failure is allowed; concealment is not.** A failed course or a failed business experiment is
   survivable. A concealed failure, a false claim, or a fabricated result is not acceptable.
4. **Independence is the goal.** The measure of success is a capable adult, not a dependent one.
5. **Transparency about money.** A young adult who receives family support must know where the money comes
   from, what it costs, and what the family's actual position is — within the disclosure limits in `D08`
   §6.3 and `D19`.
6. **No pressure to join the enterprise.** A family member who chooses a different career is supported.
   **The family will not treat non-participation in the enterprise as disloyalty** — this rule prevents a
   predictable source of family conflict.
7. **No promise of a role.** Development does not create a right to any job, board seat, trusteeship, or
   governance position. Those are decided by the competent body, on merit, per `D15`, `D18` and the
   relevant instrument.

**1.3 The limits (stated honestly).** This framework cannot: compel a young person to study, work or
participate; guarantee a place or a funding outcome; replace parental responsibility; substitute for
professional educational, psychological or medical advice; or create any legal obligation.

## 2. Programme structure — five tracks

| Track | Purpose | Typical stage | Delivered by | Recorded |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 — Education support** | Support formal education and training (school fees, tertiary, professional qualifications, vocational, apprenticeships) | 5–25 | Family Office administration + Trustee decision for Trust funds | `D65` (limited) + funding record |
| **T2 — Financial literacy** | Understand money, budgets, debt, tax, investment, risk, and the difference between capital and income | 12–21 | Family Office + external educators | Attendance only |
| **T3 — Governance education** | Understand the family's structure, the Trust, the companies, the Foundation, the difference between ownership and management, and how decisions are legally made | 16–25 | Counsel, the Family Office, experienced independent directors | Attendance only |
| **T4 — Work exposure and mentorship** | Real work experience and mentorship outside the family's direct line | 16–30 | Enterprise managers + external mentors | `D15` internship records |
| **T5 — Stewardship and leadership** | Responsibility over real (small) decisions, with accountability and review | 21+ | The family's governance bodies | Governance records |

**2.1 Sequencing.** T1–T3 should precede T5. **An unprepared person should not be given stewardship over
money or people.** This is a family preference, not a legal rule; the legal rule is that a **fiduciary
office** must be held by a person eligible and competent, as the instrument requires.

**2.2 No rank, no entitlement.** There is no "seniority" in this framework. Age gives no vote, no role and
no money. Votes come from `D18`; roles come from the competent body; money comes from the funder's
decision.

## 3. T1 — Education support: the honest funding model

**3.1 Sources of funding — each with a different decision-maker, and this distinction must be explained to
the family.**

| Source | Decided by | Instrument | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trust** | **Trustees** (discretion) | `D02` Sched. 3; `D05`; RM-11 threshold; `D04` §7 | Distribution framework, caps, tax |
| **Company** (as an employee benefit or a training cost) | The company's board | The company's policy; employment contract | Must be a genuine business cost; tax treatment `[TAX TO CONFIRM]` |
| **Foundation** | The Foundation's governors | Its constitution and purpose | **Only if it fits the charitable purpose and benefits the public — never as a private education subsidy for a family member** (`D51`, `D52`) |
| **Parents/personal** | The parents or the young person | Private | Family policy may set expectations, but the family cannot bind a parent's own money |
| **Family Office (administration only)** | — | Mandate | The Family Office **administers**; it never decides who receives what |

**3.2 The Foundation's boundary (critical).** **Paying a family member's school fees from the Foundation's
charitable funds is a private benefit and is prohibited**, unless it is part of a genuine, publicly
available charitable programme on objective criteria that a family member happens to satisfy — and even
then it must be structured, documented and independently decided (counsel must confirm; `Q-G-03`).

**3.3 Application and decision process (Trust-funded support).**

| Step | Who | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Applicant (or parent, for a minor) | Written application: course, institution, cost, duration, why it fits the plan |
| 2 | Family Office | Verify the institution, the fee schedule and the enrolment document; prepare the file; obtain a cost comparison for alternatives |
| 3 | Trustees | Apply the **full chain in `D05` §4.1** — eligibility, power, purpose, limits, Reserved Matters, conflicts, affordability, tax, decision, payment, record |
| 4 | Trustees | Set conditions: evidence of enrolment; periodic progress reports; a pass standard; what happens on failure or withdrawal |
| 5 | Finance | Pay **directly to the institution** where practicable (never cash where avoidable); record in the accounting authority |
| 6 | Family Office | Track progress; escalate a failure, a withdrawal or a change of course to the Trustees |
| 7 | Trustees | Review annually: continuation, adjustment, or stop — with reasons recorded |

**3.4 Conditions (proposed).** Support may be conditioned on: enrolment evidence; minimum attendance;
a pass/grade standard; not working more than a stated number of hours; not being subject to an unresolved
dishonesty finding; and honest reporting. **Support may not be conditioned on**: a course choice that suits
the family rather than the person, where that would harm the person's prospects; a particular career; or
participation in family governance.

**3.5 What happens on failure or withdrawal.** The Trustees decide: continue with support (with reasons),
restructure (change of course), suspend, or stop. **The decision and its reasons are recorded.** The
family's stated position: failure is addressed honestly and without humiliation; **concealment is
addressed as a conduct matter.** The reason a person failed must never be shared beyond those who need to
know it (privacy).

**3.6 Repayment.** **The default position: a Trust-funded education grant is not a loan and is not
repayable** unless the funding decision documents it as a loan. Where it is a loan, it must be: written;
recorded as a Trust asset (`D67`); on terms the Trustees record as fair; and treated for tax as the advice
requires. **A "no-loan" policy is cleaner and is the family's default.**

## 4. T2–T3 — Financial and governance literacy

**4.1 Curriculum (minimum topics).**

| Module | Content | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Money, budgets, debt, savings, interest | Basic competence |
| M2 | Tax: personal, payroll, and the fact that family assets are taxed | Prevents the "money in the family is tax-free" myth |
| M3 | The family's actual structure: what the Trust is; what a company is; what the Foundation is; who decides what | Prevents the "everything belongs to everyone" myth |
| M4 | Ownership vs management vs governance | Prevents the "family can direct the board" myth |
| M5 | Reading a set of accounts and a trustee report | Allows meaningful participation |
| M6 | Risk, insurance, and the difference between assets and contingent benefits | Prevents the "insurance policy is wealth" error |
| M7 | Law and compliance basics: contracts, filings, data protection, conflicts | Prevents avoidable trouble |
| M8 | The family's own history, values and the Constitution — including the enforceability rules in `D11` §10 | Honest self-understanding |
| M9 | Digital and AI literacy — including that **AI is never an authority** (`FIR-017`) | The family runs on a governed intelligence layer and must understand it |
| M10 | Ethics and leadership under pressure — with cases from the family's own experience, anonymised | Emotional and moral preparation |

**4.2 Delivery.** At least `[NUMBER]` sessions a year, delivered by: the Family Office (M1–M2), counsel
(M3, M7), the Chief Governance Officer function (M4–M5), an independent director or adviser (M6, M10), and
external providers where appropriate. **Sessions must be practical** — reading a real (anonymised) set of
accounts, drafting a real board paper, arguing a real case.

**4.3 No examination, no exclusion.** Attendance is encouraged but not compulsory once a person is an
adult; **no one is excluded from any family right for not attending.** The family's aim is competence, not
a gatekeeping test — and a "test" would be an unfit basis for a legal role anyway.

## 5. T4 — Work exposure and mentorship

**5.1 Internal placements.** Per `D15` §2.4: a written programme; a supervisor who is not a family member;
a defined duration; lawful pay or a compliant stipend; a written assessment; **no automatic conversion to
employment.**

**5.2 External placements — strongly encouraged.** A first job **outside** the family enterprise.
Rationale: the person learns how the world treats people who are not their relatives, and the enterprise
learns whether they can succeed without the family's halo. **The family's stated preference is that a
family member's first substantive employment be outside the group** where feasible.

**5.3 Mentorship.** Each participant (from the age of `[AGE]`) is offered a mentor. **The mentor should be
independent of the family and the enterprise** where possible; where the mentor is a family member, a
second, external mentor should be provided. Mentors: meet at least `[FREQUENCY]`; keep a short record of
objectives and reflections; **may not act as a decision maker about the participant's funding or role**;
and receive no benefit from the participant's decisions.

**5.4 Confidentiality and boundaries of mentorship.** A mentor must not: disclose the participant's
personal information beyond the programme; use the relationship for business advantage; become a de facto
decision maker; or become a channel for a participant to bypass the governing bodies. **The mentor's
records are the participant's personal data** — `D70` applies.

## 6. T5 — Stewardship and leadership development

**6.1 Stewardship ladder (increasing responsibility, always with accountability).**

| Rung | What the person is trusted with | Who grants it | Review |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | A small budget for a defined project (e.g. a community project, a Foundation project) | The Funder's decision (Trust/Foundation/company, per its own rules) | Project report, delivered publicly to the family |
| S2 | A project team role, with a manager | The enterprise/Foundation | Performance review (`D15`) |
| S3 | A committee role (non-fiduciary), e.g. a family working group or a management committee | The appointing body | Terms of reference; annual review |
| S4 | A **fiduciary or governance office** (director, trustee, governor, officer) | **Only the competent body under the relevant instrument**, on merit, with the eligibility rules (`D18`, `D24`, `D03`, `D49`) | Instrument-based review; for a Trust office, `D03` applies |
| S5 | **Control authority** (chair, CEO, CFO, controlling shareholder) | The competent body, with a documented succession plan (`D58`) | Continuous; see `D58` |

**6.2 The hard rule.** **Rungs S1–S3 may be tailored to develop a person. Rung S4 and S5 may not be
tailored to suit a person** — they must be filled on merit under the instrument, because a fiduciary office
imposes duties that cannot be diluted for the officeholder's development.

**6.3 Family members in family governance.** A person may serve on the Family Council or as a family
representative once eligible under `D18`. **Family governance service is not a stepping stone to a
fiduciary office**; it is a different kind of role with different limits (`D12` §1.3).

**6.4 Shadowing and observation.** Observation of board or trustee meetings may be permitted as a
development tool **only** with the body's consent, with confidentiality undertakings, and **without** any
participation in deliberation or access to restricted matters (particularly people, litigation and
personal data). **The body's consent and the record of the arrangement are mandatory.**

## 7. Records, privacy and limits on the family's knowledge of an individual

**7.1 Who holds the record.** The Family Office administers the programme and holds a **participant
record** limited to: participation status; the funding decision and reference; enrolment evidence;
progress status; and attendance. **Health, psychological, disciplinary and family-conflict information is
NOT part of the programme record** and may not be held there.

**7.2 Access.** The participant record is `HIGHLY_RESTRICTED`: the participant (once adult), the Trustees
only as necessary for a funding decision, the Family Office administrator, and the decision maker for the
funding. **No other family member has a right of access to a participant's record — including a parent, in
respect of an adult participant's own financial and personal details** (subject to the law and to the
consent of the person).

**7.3 Minors.** For a minor: the record is minimal; the information goes to the parent/guardian as the
law requires; the funding decision is made by the Trustees where the Trust funds it; and **the family must
not discuss a child's performance, difficulties or health in a family meeting.** `D70` §Minors applies.

**7.4 Prohibited practices.**

1. Comparing participants' results publicly.
2. Using a participant's record against them in a later family decision about a role.
3. Publishing a participant's information on any family forum or group.
4. Using an AI system on participant data outside a compliant, permitted environment (`Q-DP-02`).
5. Creating a "ranking" of the next generation.

**7.5 A person's right to see their own record.** On request, a person (or a parent for a minor) may see
their own programme record and have inaccurate information corrected. **This right must be honoured** —
and, where the record includes third-party information, that part is redacted.

## 8. Funding discipline and the control plane

**8.1** Every payment under this framework runs through the accounting authority of the funding body. **No
payment may be made on the basis of a family decision, a Council resolution, a software approval or an AI
recommendation.**

**8.2** A "family education fund" **must not** be created as an off-book ledger, a spreadsheet of record,
or a software balance outside the accounting authority (`D35`, `D08` §1.3). Where the family wishes to have
a ring-fenced education fund, the correct routes are: a Trust purpose with a cap; a company's employee
education benefit; or a Foundation programme — each with its own decision-maker and its own compliance
requirements.

**8.3** Reporting: the funding body's annual report must show the aggregate spent on next-generation
development, by track, without disclosing unnecessary personal detail.
