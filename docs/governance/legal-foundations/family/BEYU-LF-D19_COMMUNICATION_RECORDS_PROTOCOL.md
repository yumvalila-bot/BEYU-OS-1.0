> **⚠ TWO WARNINGS.**
> **1. Where a family record lives is a legal question, not a convenience question.** Family records
> contain **personal data about identifiable people — including children, health information, financial
> information and conflicts.** Placing them in a public repository, an unsecured cloud folder, a personal
> device, a group chat, or an external AI service may breach the data-protection law and the family's own
> confidentiality obligations. **The pack flags the registration and cross-border questions as
> `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`, and the answers are mandatory before any such record is created.**
> **2. A record is evidence.** What the family writes about a person can later be read by that person, by
> a court, or by a regulator. **Write every record as if the person it concerns will read it — because
> they may.**

## 1. Purpose, principles and scope

**1.1 Purpose.** To define how the family communicates; where records live; who may see them; how long
they are kept; how they are disposed of; and what must never be done with them.

**1.2 Principles.**

1. **Necessity.** Collect and keep the minimum needed for the purpose.
2. **Accuracy.** A record must be true; a correction is made by adding a correction, **never** by deleting
   or rewriting history (`D08` §8).
3. **Traceability.** Every record states its source, its author and its date.
4. **Access control.** Records are held where access can be limited to the people entitled to see them.
5. **Retention discipline.** Keep what must be kept; destroy what may lawfully be destroyed, under a
   schedule — and never destroy what is under legal hold or evidences a right.
6. **Separation.** Family records, Trust records, company records and Foundation records are **separate**,
   with separate owners and separate access.
7. **Dignity.** No record about a person may be made for the purpose of embarrassing, pressuring or
   disadvantaging them.
8. **No ghosts.** No off-system "shadow" record — no private spreadsheet of who was given what, no private
   log of who said what, outside this protocol's registers.

**1.3 Scope.** Applies to: the Family Assembly and Family Council; family roles and mandates; family
policies; family-relationship matters; and the family's own records. **It does not govern** the Trust's
accounts and records (`D08` governs), the companies' statutory records (company law and the articles), or
the Foundation's records (`D55`).

## 2. Communication rules

**2.1 Channels and their permitted uses.**

| Channel | Permitted for | Prohibited for |
|---|---|---|
| **Governed repository** (`D75` §Custody) — access-controlled | All family records, minutes, policies, registers | Nothing (it is the correct home) |
| **Email to family members** | Notices, papers, decisions, communication | Personal data beyond necessity; large confidential dossiers to a whole-group list |
| **Messaging group (family chat)** | Social coordination; meeting logistics; reminders | **Decisions; minutes; distribution amounts; health or financial information; disputes; anything confidential** |
| **Video/telephone meeting** | Meetings held under `D13` | Recording **without the consent of every participant** |
| **Printed paper** | Meetings; signing; documents requiring an original | Storage outside a locked location; leaving papers behind; disposal without shredding |
| **External AI tools / cloud services** | **Only** with a compliant, permitted environment (`Q-DP-02`) | **Any family or beneficiary personal data, health data, disputes, or financial records** |
| **Public or open repositories** | Nothing family-related | **Everything** — this is prohibited absolutely |

**2.2 The channel rule.** **A decision is valid only if it is recorded in the governed repository under the
correct process.** A decision announced in a group chat, or agreed in a corridor, is **not** a decision —
it may be an instruction to prepare one.

**2.3 The list rule.** Distribution amounts, beneficiary information, health information, disputes and
personal financial information are **never** sent to a group list. They are shared only with the people
entitled to see them (`D08` §6.3, `D05` §5.3).

**2.4 The communication standard for family matters.** Every written family communication about a matter
of substance must state: **the subject; the status** (information / discussion / recommendation / decision
/ referral); **who decided or is asked to decide; the reasons; the next step; and the date.** A
communication that does not say whether it is a decision or a discussion is defective.

**2.5 Bad news travels first.** A problem, a loss, a breach, a missed filing, a legal exposure or a
dispute must be communicated **when it is known**, not when it has been "fixed" — because late disclosure
destroys trust and removes the family's options. **Concealing bad news is a serious breach of `D14`.**

**2.6 No surprise by rumour.** Where a material family matter becomes known outside the family (a filing, a
published proceeding, a regulatory action, a departure of a senior person), the relevant body must
communicate to the family **what is known, what is not known, and what happens next** — while respecting
confidentiality and privilege.

**2.7 Language and accessibility.** Family governance documents should be written in plain language, and a
governance summary should accompany technical accounts (`D08` §6.4). Where a family member's first language
differs, essential documents should be available in the language they read — `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` whether
any translation must be certified for a legal purpose (`Q-R-02`).

**3.1 The R-14 rule.** **The family does not maintain a general "family database" of personal
circumstances.** Where a specific decision requires a specific fact (e.g. a medical report supporting an
emergency distribution), the document goes into the **decision file of the body making the decision**, is
restricted to those who need it, and is **destroyed** when the purpose ends (subject to the retention
rules and any legal hold). **A family "file on a person" is prohibited.**

**3.2 The distinction between a Trust record and a family record.** The beneficiary register and the
distribution record belong to the **Trustees**, are governed by `D08`, and are **not** to be merged with
family-governance records. **Family members who are not Trustees have no right of access to Trust records
beyond `D08` §6.3.**

## 4. Custody: where records actually live

**4.1 The four requirements for any repository.**

1. **Access control** — identity-based, role-based, revocable, logged.
2. **Durability** — a backup exists; the record survives a device or account failure.
3. **Retrievability** — the record can be produced in an intelligible form, and a specific record can be
   found when a court, a regulator or an adviser asks.
4. **Legal compliance** — the location and any cross-border access comply with the data-protection law
   (`Q-DP-01`, `Q-DP-02`, `Q-DP-06`).

**4.2 Approved locations.**

| Class | Where | Who administers |
|---|---|---|
| **Originals of legal instruments** | A documented **physical vault** (plus a governed digital copy) | Company Secretary function (`D74` holds the index) |
| **Trust records** | The Trust's governed repository, access-limited to the Trustees + Protector + auditor on scope | Trustees |
| **Company records** | The company's statutory minute book/register as the law requires | Company Secretary |
| **Foundation records** | The Foundation's own repository | Foundation's secretary |
| **Family governance records** | The family's governed repository | Council Secretary |
| **Highly restricted records** (beneficiaries, health, disputes, minors) | Segregated, encrypted, limited access, distinct from general family records | The owning body only |

**4.3 Prohibited locations (absolute).** Public repositories and open-source hosting; shared links without
access control; personal email accounts used as the record store; personal devices without access control
and encryption; chat groups; external AI services; USB sticks with no register; and **any place the family
cannot control access to and cannot retrieve from.**

**4.4 The naming and reference discipline.** Every record carries: a reference; a title; a date; an owner;
its access class; its retention class; and (for anything legal) its `D74` entry and its verification
status. **A document with no reference cannot be relied on** because it cannot be found and cannot be shown
to have been reviewed.

**4.5 The "no ghosts" audit.** Once a year (`D75`), the family must look for records held **outside** the
approved locations (someone's laptop, a private spreadsheet, a WhatsApp export, a personal drive) and
bring them into the governed repository **or delete them lawfully**. **Every one found and left in place
is a real risk.**

## 5. Access: who may see what

**5.1 Access matrix (default — the family may tighten, not loosen, for personal data).**

| Record | Family Assembly | Council | Trustees | Family Office | Advisers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Family Constitution, policies | ✔ | ✔ | On request | ✔ (admin) | On engagement |
| Assembly minutes & resolutions | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ (as given) | ✔ (admin) | ✘ |
| Council minutes | Summary | ✔ | ✘ | ✔ (admin) | ✘ |
| Register of family roles | ✔ (own + role list) | ✔ | ✘ | ✔ (admin) | ✘ |
| Conflict declarations | Aggregated | ✔ | Per instrument | ✘ | ✘ |
| Beneficiary register (`D65`) | **✘** | **✘** | ✔ | On documented mandate | Per engagement |
| Distribution amounts | **✘** | **✘** | ✔ | Per mandate | ✘ |
| Next-generation participant records | ✘ | Per §`D16` §7 | Per funding need | ✔ (admin) | ✘ |
| Dispute log | Anonymised summary | ✔ | ✘ | ✘ | ✘ |
| Personal health/financial information | ✘ | ✘ | Per decision need | ✘ | ✘ |

**5.2 The least-privilege rule.** Access is granted for a **purpose**, with an **end date**, and is
**reviewed at least annually** and **immediately on any role change or departure** (`D08` §8.3).

**5.3 Revocation.** Access must be revoked on: the end of a role; the end of a mandate; a departure; a
change of adviser; a breach of confidentiality; and on request where the person no longer needs it.
**Revocation must be recorded.** (Note: revoking access is not the same as ending a legal office — `D03`
§10.2(e).)

**5.4 A person's own data.** A person is entitled to see, and to have corrected, records held about them —
subject to the law, to other people's rights, and to legitimate limits (for example, a mediation record's
confidentiality). **The family must have a written route for such a request** and must answer it honestly,
including where the answer is "the law entitles you to see this, and here it is." `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM the
statutory right of access and its limits.]`

**5.5 Minors and young adults.** Records about a minor are minimal, are shared with the parent/guardian as
the law requires, and are **not** shared with the wider family. On reaching adulthood, a person's right of
access to their own record becomes their own.

## 6. Retention, disposal, legal hold and breach

**6.1 Retention schedule.** Every record class in §3 must have a retention period **confirmed by counsel**
(pending: the pack asserts no period). **Default where unconfirmed: retain, and do not destroy.**

**6.2 Disposal.** Permitted only: under a written schedule; by a recorded decision identifying the records;
with a **destruction certificate**; and **never** for an original instrument, never for anything evidencing
a right, and never for anything under legal hold. **Shredding, not binning** for paper; secure deletion for
digital, including backups.

**6.3 Legal hold.** On any actual or reasonably anticipated dispute, investigation, audit, or court
proceeding, **all disposal is suspended**. The suspension is recorded with its date, its reason and the
authorising decision. **Deleting a record under legal hold is a serious failure; deliberate deletion is
worse and may itself be an offence** (`D08` §8.1).

**6.4 Breach response (data or confidentiality).**

| Step | Action | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contain (revoke access, recall the message, secure the repository) | Immediately |
| 2 | Record: what happened, what data, how many people, the cause | Within `[NUMBER]` hours |
| 3 | Assess the risk to the individuals affected (identity, finances, safety, dignity) | Within `[NUMBER]` hours |
| 4 | **Notify the regulator where the law requires** — `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` the obligation, the period and the content (the pack notes a statutory notification duty without asserting its terms) | Per the law — **`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`** |
| 5 | Notify the affected individuals where the law or the risk requires | As advised |
| 6 | Remediate the cause (access, storage, training, process) | Within `[NUMBER]` days |
| 7 | Report to the owning body and, in aggregate, to the family | At the next meeting |

**6.5 No concealment.** A breach must **never** be concealed to avoid embarrassment. **Concealment
converts a manageable incident into a governance failure and possibly an offence** (`D14` §10.3).

## 7. Control-plane and AI interaction

**7.1** Where the family uses the BEYU OS control plane for governance records, the control plane is a
**record system**, not the source of any right, office or authority (`D03` §10, `D12` §8, `D18` §5).

**7.2 Required properties before personal data is placed in any system.**

1. Identity-based access control with an audit trail.
2. Encryption at rest and in transit.
3. Segregation of `HIGHLY_RESTRICTED` records from general records.
4. A retention and deletion capability that actually deletes (including backups).
5. **A documented lawful basis and a documented location/transfer position** (`Q-DP-01`, `Q-DP-02`,
   `Q-DP-06`).

**7.3 AI rules.**

1. **No personal family data may be entered into an external AI service** unless counsel has confirmed
   that the transfer and processing are lawful (`Q-DP-02`).
2. AI output may **not** be recorded as a decision, a determination, or a person's statement.
3. An AI-generated summary of a meeting may be used as a **draft**, but the **minutes must be approved by
   the meeting** and must be attributable to a human author.
4. **AI may not be used** to profile, rank, or assess family members, or to draw conclusions about a
   person's conduct, capacity or character.

**7.4 Forbidden practices.** (a) A confidential record in a public or open repository; (b) a beneficiary
list in a shared drive with a link; (c) records of one person's dispute visible to another party's family
branch; (d) minutes of a closed session kept in the general file; (e) a "clean-up" deletion of adverse
records shortly after a complaint.
