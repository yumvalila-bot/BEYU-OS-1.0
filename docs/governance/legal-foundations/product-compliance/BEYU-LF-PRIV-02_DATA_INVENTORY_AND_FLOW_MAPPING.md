> **⚠ WHY THIS DOCUMENT COMES BEFORE THE PRIVACY POLICY.**
> **A privacy notice is a statement of what you do with personal data.** If it is written before the data
> inventory, it becomes **a wish list** — describing purposes, recipients, retention periods and transfers that
> nobody has verified, and promising safeguards nobody has implemented. **A false or stale privacy notice is
> itself a compliance exposure and a reputational risk, and it is the single easiest document for a regulator
> or a customer to compare against reality.**
> **So: inventory first. Then the flow map. Then the retention schedule. Then — and only then — the published
> notice (`PRIV-01`).**
> **And: `D70` already exists as a PDPA obligation checklist.** That document lists **what the law requires**.
> **This document records what the group actually does.** They must be read together, and neither proves
> compliance (`D70` §1; `CMP-01` §1).

**6.2 The system's actual semantics — the policy must not promise more than this.**
- Retention is computed from the **document's own upload timestamp** (`src/lib/data-governance/retention-service.ts`).
- **A legal hold blocks deletion regardless of expiry.**
- **Eligibility for deletion is a decision, not a deletion.**
- **Destruction requires human approval.**
**6.3 Therefore `PRIV-01` may state**: that records are kept while needed for the stated purpose and any legal
obligation; that some records are subject to **legal holds** that prevent deletion; that deletion is performed
after an approval step; and that a **legal or regulatory obligation may require longer retention**. **It may NOT
state** that data is deleted automatically at a fixed period, that a deletion request is actioned instantly, or
that nothing is retained after a period — **because the system does not do that**.
**6.4** Where data is **subject to a litigation hold or an investigation**, the hold is recorded, with the
reason and the release decision (`D71` §3; `D74`).
