# BEYU-LF-D53 — Foundation Funding, Shared Services, Cost Allocation and Inter-Entity Transactions Policy

Document ID: `BEYU-LF-D53` · Version 1.0 (draft) · Date 2026-10-15
Status: **`COMPLETE_DRAFT`** — **no funding arrangement exists, no shared cost is allocated, no inter-entity charge has been raised.**
Owner (proposed): Foundation Director with the Group CFO. Approving authority: the Foundation's governing body (independent) and the board.
Classification: `CONFIDENTIAL`. Related: `D27` (F-08, G-03, G-06), `D30` §5.4, `D35`, `D51`, `D52`, `D54`, `D69`, `D72`.
Depends on: `Q-TAX-02`, `Q-TAX-04`, `Q-G-02`, `Q-G-03`.

> **⚠ THE TWO FAILURES THIS POLICY PREVENTS.**
> **1. The Foundation as the group's cost centre** — where the group's costs are quietly pushed onto charitable
> funds. That is a private benefit and, where the numbers matter, a fraud on donors and the regulator.
> **2. The Foundation as a free service provider** — where the group consumes the Foundation's staff, systems
> or brand for nothing.
> **Both are "informal", and both are indefensible on review. Every flow in either direction must be
> documented, allocated on a stated basis, and at arm's length.**

## 1. Funding the Foundation

| # | Source | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **Group donations** | Related-party process (`D51`); no conditions that create direction or benefit; recorded and receipted; the group's own approval per `D27` G-06 |
| 1.2 | **Family donations** | Permitted; disclosed; **no control consequence**; large donations from a family member are recorded with terms |
| 1.3 | **Third-party donors and grants** | Per `D52` §5 |
| 1.4 | **Programme income** (where lawful) | Permitted only if it serves the purposes; `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` whether trading is permitted for the chosen form |
| 1.5 | **Investment income** | Per the reserves policy (`D52` §7 decision 7) |
| 1.6 | **Founder endowment** | The instrument states whether the capital is permanent and the conditions |

**1.7 The funding rule.** The Foundation must have an **independent funding base** to the extent possible. **A
Foundation funded only by the family group is structurally vulnerable** — not fatal, but it must be
acknowledged in the governance (independence is weaker when the funder is also the appointing family).

## 2. Shared services (what may be shared, on what terms)

| # | Service | Permitted? | Terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Accounting/bookkeeping | ✔ | Documented, allocated, invoiced (`D54`) |
| 2.2 | Legal/compliance support | ✔ | Per `D36`; documented |
| 2.3 | IT, hosting, software licences | ✔ | Cost allocation + a matching licence right (`D54`) |
| 2.4 | Brand and IP | ✔ by licence | `D54`; purpose-serving; no charge or a documented charge |
| 2.5 | Premises/space | ✔ | Written arrangement; market or documented cost basis |

| 2.8 | **Undocumented sharing** | ✘ | Prohibited |

## 3. Cost allocation

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 3.1 | Every shared cost has a **written allocation basis** (headcount, time recorded, transactions, square metres, revenue, or another defensible driver) and the basis is stated in the policy |
| 3.2 | The basis is **applied consistently** and reviewed annually; a change of basis is documented with reasons |
| 3.3 | **Direct costs** are charged to the body that incurred them; **only genuine shared costs** are allocated |
| 3.4 | **No cost may be allocated to the Foundation that does not benefit the Foundation** |
| 3.5 | **Centre-of-cost allocation is not a substitute for an arm's-length charge** where the service is one the Foundation could buy on the market |
| 3.6 | Allocations are **reconciled periodically** and any imbalance corrected |
| 3.7 | The **auditor's attention** is drawn to the allocation policy and the intercompany balances |
| 3.8 | The **annual report discloses** the inter-entity amounts, per `D55` and the form's requirements |

## 4. Inter-entity transactions

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 4.1 | Every transaction: a **written agreement**, an **arm's-length price**, an **invoice**, a **payment record**, and a **`D72` register entry** |
| 4.2 | The **arm's-length test**: would the parties have agreed this price if they were unrelated? The answer must be documented (comparables, cost-plus margin, a quote, a valuation) |
| 4.3 | **No netting** of intercompany balances without a documented set-off agreement |
| 4.4 | **No interest-free or undocumented loans** in either direction (prohibited for the Foundation: `D51` §5) |
| 4.5 | **Tax**: withholding, deductibility, VAT and transfer pricing are considered **before** the transaction (`D69`, `Q-TAX-04`) — **not after a query** |
| 4.6 | **Currency**: the currency, the rate and the date are recorded; the FX risk sits with the body that bears it, explicitly |
| 4.7 | **Year-end**: intercompany balances are reconciled and confirmed; the position is reported to the auditors |

## 5. What must never happen

| # | Prohibited |
|---|---|
| 1 | Paying the group's costs from charitable funds |
| 2 | Paying a family member's or a connected person's costs from charitable funds |
| 3 | Using the Foundation's funds to buy assets that the group or the family then use for free |
| 4 | A "management fee" with no documented service and no allocation basis |
| 5 | A donation "earmarked" for a benefit to the donor |
| 6 | Seconding a group employee to the Foundation while the group continues to direct their work |
| 7 | Using the Foundation's regulatory status to obtain a benefit for the group |
| 8 | Any arrangement whose purpose is not capable of being written down plainly and defended |

## 6. Decisions required from the user / family / governing bodies

1. The **funding plan** for the Foundation, and the family's intended level of support.
2. Which **shared services** will be used, and the allocation bases.
3. Whether the Foundation will **pay** for the group's services (recommended: yes, where received).
4. Whether the Foundation will **charge** the group for anything it provides (and it should, where value
   flows).
5. The **reserves policy** and the annual budget approval route.
6. Who signs for the Foundation and the two-signature rule.
