## 1. What the Foundation is, and is not

| It is (by intention) | It is **not** |
|---|---|
| A separate nonprofit whose fiduciaries owe duties to a charitable purpose | A **subsidiary** of the Trust or the Holding Company |
| Governed by its own body, with an independent quorum for related-party matters | A **Sector OS**, a product, or a brand |
| A grant-maker and programme body serving its stated public purposes | A **family bank** or a vehicle for private benefit |
| Regulated in its own right (in every candidate form) | A body that the family can **direct**, instruct or veto |
| A partner to the group — on documented, arm's-length, purpose-serving terms | An **asset-holding device** for the family |

## 2. Candidate routes (from the retrieved sources — each `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`)

### Route 1 — Non-governmental organisation under the **Non-Governmental Organisations Act (Cap. 56)**

**Verified primary-source content (from `LEGAL_SOURCES_REGISTER.md` `FDN-1`, R.E. 2023 numbering):**

| Element | What the source says |
|---|---|
| Registration is **mandatory** for an NGO | s.13 |
| Registrar and register | A Director for NGO Coordination acts as **Registrar** (ss.3–4); a **register of NGOs** is kept and each NGO is identified by a **certificate of registration** (ss.6–7) |
| Application document set | Prescribed form by founder member(s) + **copy of the constitution** + **minutes with full names and signatures of founders** + **personal particulars of office bearers** + **address and physical location of the head office** + fee + anything else the Registrar requires (s.14) |
| Timelines | Registrar refers to the Board within **one month**; Board determines within **two months** (ss.15–16) |
| **Refusal grounds** | Activities **not for public interest** or contrary to written law; false/misleading material information (s.15) — **this is the statutory purpose test `D52` must satisfy** |
| **Renewal** | Certificate subject to **renewal after ten years**, applied for **six months before expiry** (s.16(3)) `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM the current period]` |
| Registry level | District / regional / **national**, depending on the area of operation (ss.24–25) |
| **Annual duty** | **Annual audited report** submitted to the Council and the Board, **made available to the public** (s.29) |
| Governing document | The constitution and documents submitted at registration **"shall be the governing documents"** (s.30) |
| Change of constitution / name / office bearers | Must be **notified to the Registrar in writing** with a certified resolution and the certificate; the Registrar records it and may issue a new certificate (s.31) |

**Secondary (unverified):** a minimum of five founder members aged 18+, or five registered organisations;
typical processing 21–60 days; appeal to the Minister. **Do not rely on these without counsel.**

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| The purpose test is explicit and understood by the regulator | A full compliance regime: registration, renewal, public audited reporting, notification of changes |
| Clear legal personality and a defined governing body | Constitutional changes require a regulator notification |
| Public transparency supports donor trust | The document set and the constitution are scrutinised at the outset |
| The route most stakeholders recognise | Renewal and public reporting create permanent obligations (`D55`) |

### Route 2 — **Company limited by guarantee** under the Companies Act (Cap. 212)

| The source says | Consequence |
|---|---|
| A guarantee company is a recognised form (s.11/13 materials retrieved) | Corporate form with members who guarantee an amount rather than subscribe for shares |
| There is a **restriction** we read in the guarantee-company provisions (s.3(3)/s.13(6)) | **`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` whether the form is available to BEYU, and what the restriction prohibits.** This is the question that most affects the route |

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Familiar corporate governance (board, members, resolutions) | The company-law filing and accounts regime applies |
| No shareholder concept — consistent with "no private benefit" | The **restriction** may make it unavailable for the intended purpose |
| Aligns with the group's existing corporate habits | Public registry disclosure of officers |

### Route 3 — **Trustees' Incorporation Act (Cap. 318)** route for a charitable body

| The source says | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Incorporation is available for charitable/religious/educational/public purposes (`TRUST-1` ss.2–4) | A body corporate of trustees — the "registered trustees" model |
| There is a **compulsory-incorporation rule for charitable purposes** (`FDN-3`) | **`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`** — this may *require* incorporation for a body with charitable purposes |
| **s.1A mutual exclusivity** | A body incorporated under Cap. 318 cannot also be a company, society or NGO vehicle — see `TRUST-2`; the routes are **alternative, not cumulative** |
| Incorporation is by **certificate** and is noted as **revocable**; the name must include **"Registered Trustees"**; **≥2 trustees ordinarily resident** | Ongoing governance requirements |
| Trustee **liability survives incorporation** | A real exposure to understand before choosing |

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Minimal regulator-facing renewal regime relative to an NGO | **Revocability**, resident-trustee requirement, mandatory name form |
| Fits the family's comfort with the trusteeship model | **Mutual exclusivity** with other forms |
| No shareholder concept | The compulsory-incorporation rule may remove the choice |

## 4. Decision framework

| # | Question | Why it decides the route |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Is the Foundation's activity charitable in the intended sense**, and does it meet the **public-interest** test with real beneficiaries beyond the family? | The whole structure depends on this; a family-benefit body fails it |
| 2 | What form does the **compulsory-incorporation rule** require (Cap. 318) for a charitable-purpose body? | It may remove the choice |
| 3 | Is the **guarantee-company restriction** a bar? | Route 2 availability |
| 4 | What is the **tax position** of each route, for the Foundation **and** for a donor? | Re-anchors the analysis |
| 5 | What **ongoing reporting** will the family actually sustain? | A registration without the capability to report is a liability, not an asset |
| 6 | Who are the **office bearers**, and can the family accept that they owe duties to the purpose rather than to the family? | If not, the structure should not be built (`DAA-FDN-004`) |
| 7 | Is a **foreign** structure or an offshore grant-making vehicle being contemplated? | **Not researched for any foreign jurisdiction**; requires separate advice |
| 8 | What does the Foundation actually **do** (grant-making, programmes, shared services)? | Determines the governance and the cost |

## 5. The independence problem (the structural risk this pack is built around)

**5.1** A founder-controlled nonprofit whose assets, contracts and staff flow through the family's group is
**structurally suspect**. The controls that answer it are in `D50` (independence and conflicts),
`D51` (related-party protocol), `D52` (private-benefit safeguards), `D53` (funding and cost allocation) and
`D54` (IP and services templates). **They are drafted to be enforceable against the family, not by it.**

**5.2 The five tests every proposed arrangement must pass.**

| # | Test | Fails if |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Purpose** | The arrangement does not serve a stated public purpose |
| 2 | **Direction** | The family, a group company or the Trust can **instruct** the Foundation |
| 3 | **Benefit** | Any private benefit flows to a family member or a group company beyond a properly documented arm's-length exchange |
| 4 | **Cost** | The Foundation pays more, or receives less, than an arm's-length party would |
| 5 | **Evidence** | The decision, the comparison and the approver are not recorded |

**5.3** Where any test fails, **the arrangement must not proceed** — and if it has already occurred, the
Foundation's body must unwind it (`D51` §9).
