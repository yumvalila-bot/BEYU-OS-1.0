## 1. Purpose, scope and standing

**1.1 Purpose.** To define, in one place, how a person becomes a Trustee, how a Trustee's office ends,
who decides, what evidence is required, what must be filed, and how the Trust continues without
interruption.

**1.2 Scope.** Applies to: Trustees, the Protector (for the powers given here), the Family Council
(recommendation only), the Company Secretary function, and any adviser supporting a trustee change.

**1.3 Standing.** This document is a **governance schedule** intended to be incorporated by reference
into the Trust instrument. Standing alone it is a **practice document** with no legal effect. Legal
effect arises only from the instrument and applicable law. **`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`**

**1.4 Hierarchy.** If any rule here conflicts with the Trust instrument or with applicable law, the
instrument/law prevails, and the conflict is logged in `CROSS_DOCUMENT_CONSISTENCY_REPORT.md`.

**1.5 Mandatory vs proposed.** Clauses marked `[MANDATORY]` reflect a requirement of law as far as the
pack could ascertain it — every one is `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`. Clauses marked `[PROPOSED]` are private
arrangements. Clauses marked `[OPTIONAL]` are practice with no legal force.

## 2. Eligibility and disqualification

**2.1 Who may be a Trustee `[PROPOSED]`.** A Trustee must be:

(a) a natural person of full legal capacity and at least **[MINIMUM AGE — USER TO SUPPLY]** years old; or
(b) a body corporate with power to act as a trustee, including (if the route permits) a trust company
`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`;

and in each case must have confirmed in writing that they have read the Trust instrument, the
conflict-of-interest rules, and the duties schedule (`D04`), and that they accept the office.

**2.2 Disqualifications `[MANDATORY — as read from the Trustees' Incorporation Act, Cap. 318, and to be confirmed]`.**
A person is **not eligible** to apply for incorporation as, or to take part in the management or
administration of an incorporated trust as, a trustee if they:

(a) are convicted of a criminal offence or any offence involving **fraud or dishonesty**;
(b) are adjudged **bankrupt** under any written law, or enter into any agreement or scheme of a
    nature associated with insolvency;
(c) are otherwise disqualified by applicable law, a court order, or a regulator direction.

**2.3 Additional disqualifications adopted by the family `[PROPOSED]`.** A person is also not eligible
while they: (a) are the subject of an unresolved finding of dishonesty in any professional or
fiduciary capacity; (b) refuse to sign the conflict-of-interest declaration; or (c) are an employee or
director of an entity that is in unresolved adversarial dispute with the Trust, unless the Protector
waives in writing with reasons recorded.

**2.4 Continuing obligation.** A Trustee must notify the other Trustees and the Protector in writing
**within [NUMBER]** days of any event that may disqualify them.

**2.5 Fit-and-proper assessment `[PROPOSED — OPTIONAL]`.** Before appointment, the Trustees shall
record on the **Trustee, Director, Officer and Authorised-Signatory Register** (`D66`): identity
(GlobalUserID reference), the verification performed, the date, and the person who verified.

**2.6 Residency `[MANDATORY if Route 2 — COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`.** As read from Cap. 318 (`ss.15/16`),
a sole trustee must be a person **ordinarily resident in Tanzania**, and where there are two or more
trustees **not fewer than two** must be so resident. If the Trust is incorporated under that Act, this
is a continuing requirement and falling below the minimum is a ground for the regulator to act (as
read: revocation). **The Trustees must monitor and record residency compliance.**

**2.7 Number of Trustees.** Minimum **[MIN]**, maximum **[MAX]**. If Route 2 is used, the numbers
recorded in the certificate of incorporation govern. **The Trust must not be permitted to fall below
its minimum number of Trustees without immediate escalation under §7.**

## 3. Appointment

**3.1 Who appoints `[PROPOSED]`.**

| Situation | Appointing power | Consents required | Instrument |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original appointment | Settlor (in the deed) | None | The deed itself (`D02`) |
| Ordinary vacancy | The Trustees by written decision | Protector | Deed of appointment (Form 5(3) in `D02`; template in `D10`) |
| Vacancy where the Trustees are deadlocked | The Protector | — | Protector's written appointment + deed |
| Protector's own office | The Settlor, or the Trustees with the consent of all adult Beneficiaries `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` | — | Deed of appointment |
| Emergency appointment (Protector's office vacant and urgent) | The remaining Trustees, with all being agreed | Ratification by the Protector when appointed | Interim appointment deed, recorded and ratified |

**3.2 Criteria for selection `[PROPOSED]`.** The appointing body shall consider, and record: independence
(see §2.1), relevant capability, capacity to serve, absence of conflicts, and the balance of the board
(including whether at least one Trustee is independent of the family per the instrument's definition).

**3.3 Procedure `[PROPOSED]`.**

1. Identify the vacancy and the applicable appointing power.
2. Nominate a candidate; obtain a **consent to act** in writing.
3. Screen against §2 (eligibility, disqualification, residency).
4. Obtain the **Protector's** written consent (or the special procedure in §3.1).
5. Record a **trustee decision** (`D10` Form 1) stating the power relied on, the screening evidence,
   the declared interests and the decision, and the effective date.
6. Execute the **deed of appointment** (never a software act).
7. Update the registers: `D66` (officeholders), `D73` (decisions), `D74` (executed instruments),
   `D67` (if asset custody/signatory authority changes), `D71` (insurance and bonds).
8. Make every **regulator notification/return** required by the chosen route, within the statutory
   period, and record completion in `D68`. **`[MANDATORY]`** `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` the list and periods
   (as read: notification within **one month** of a change, plus periodic returns of trustees).
9. **Do not** treat the register update, the software role grant, or any notification as the source of
   the appointment. **The appointment arises from the deed.**

**3.4 Pre-commencement disclosure `[PROPOSED]`.** Before acting, each new Trustee shall sign:
(a) a conflict-of-interest declaration (template in `D14`); and (b) an acknowledgement of duties,
indemnity limits, and the AI prohibition in the Trust instrument.

**3.5 No automatic succession.** No person becomes a Trustee by being a family member, a beneficiary, a
director of a group company, an employee, or a software user. **Office is never conferred by
software, a workflow, an RBAC grant, an audit log or an AI output.**

## 4. Resignation

**4.1 Notice `[PROPOSED]`.** A Trustee may resign by **written notice** to the other Trustees, the
Protector and (if Route 2 and the law requires) the regulator, giving **[NOTICE PERIOD]** notice. The
resignation may not take effect if it would leave the Trust below its minimum number of Trustees or
below the residency minimum, until a replacement takes office.

**4.2 Continuing duties.** A resigning Trustee must, before the resignation takes effect:
(a) hand over all Trust property, records, passwords to registers (transferred through a controlled
handover), and documents in their possession; (b) account for all money and property for which they
were responsible; (c) sign a **certificate of handover** (template in `D10` Form 3) listing what was
handed over and to whom; and (d) complete or hand over any matter in progress with a written status.

**4.3 Liabilities survive.** Resignation does **not** release a Trustee from liability for acts or
omissions while in office.

**4.4 Trustee as sole signatory on any account.** If a resigning Trustee is a signatory, the Trustees
must effect the change with the bank/custodian and record it in `D67`/`D66` **before** the resignation
takes effect where practicable, and immediately after otherwise.

## 5. Removal

> **PRECEDENCE NOTE (revision 1.1).** `D02` §16.4–16.15 now contains the operative removal and replacement
> clause (grounds, who initiates and decides, notice and response, evidence, temporary safeguards, successor
> eligibility, handover, authority continuity, indemnity, deadlock, notifications). **This section and `D02`
> §16 must be read together, and if they conflict, `D02` governs.** The analysis here does not create a
> separate removal power. See `BEYU-LF-TC-01` §5 for the clause's risks and counsel questions
> (`Q-TC-07`, `Q-TC-08`).

**5.1 Grounds for removal `[PROPOSED]`** (each must be evidenced):

(a) disqualification under §2.2;
(b) breach of fiduciary duty, including any unauthorised transaction or undisclosed conflict;
(c) incapacity under §6;
(d) unauthorised absence from **[NUMBER]** consecutive trustee meetings without leave;
(e) refusal to comply with a lawful Reserved Matter process or with a court order;
(f) conduct that materially damages the Trust's interests or reputation;
(g) failure to disclose a conflict required by the instrument;
(h) failure to cooperate with an audit or an investigation;
(i) loss of the residency or other statutory qualification, where applicable.

**5.2 Who may remove, and the procedure `[PROPOSED]`.**

| Ground | Removing power | Procedure |
|---|---|---|
| Disqualification (§2.2) | Automatic on the event; verified and recorded by the remaining Trustees | Written declaration recording the disqualifying event and the evidence; regulator notification |
| Any other ground in §5.1 | The Protector on a written reasoned request by the Trustees (excluding the affected Trustee), **or** the Trustees with the Protector's consent | Notice to the affected Trustee stating the ground and the evidence; **[NOTICE PERIOD]** to respond in writing; right to be heard at a meeting; a reasoned written decision; recording in `D73`; regulator notification |

**5.3 Protections for the affected Trustee `[PROPOSED]`.**

(a) written notice of the alleged ground and the supporting evidence;
(b) a reasonable opportunity to respond in writing and, if they wish, in person;
(c) a decision recorded with reasons and the evidence relied on;
(d) no removal by the conflicted party alone;
(e) a right to have their written statement attached to the decision record;
(f) a right to refer the matter to mediation under `D17` — **which does not suspend the removal where
    the ground is disqualification, insolvency or a court order**.

**5.4 Effect of removal.** On removal, the removed Trustee ceases to hold office, must hand over as in
§4.2, and remains liable for prior acts. Removal does **not** cancel any benefit already lawfully
conferred.

**5.5 Limits on removal `[PROPOSED]` `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`.** No removal power may be exercised:
(a) to procure a breach of trust; (b) to defeat a Beneficiary's accrued entitlement; (c) to remove a
Trustee **because** they refused an unlawful direction; or (d) where its effect would be to leave the
Trust without a competent administration. **A power to remove must not be exercisable by a single
beneficiary acting alone, absent express provision confirmed by counsel.** A "no-contest" style
consequence of challenging a removal is a proposal that counsel must assess for enforceability.

**5.6 Trustee cannot remove themselves to avoid liability.** A Trustee under investigation must not
resign in lieu of a removal decision if the remaining Trustees have begun a §5 process; the process
continues and the record stands.

## 6. Incapacity, death and other automatic cessations

**6.1 Automatic cessation events.** A Trustee's office ceases automatically on:
(a) **death** (recorded on the register with the date and source of the evidence);
(b) **incapacity** as defined in §6.2;
(c) **disqualification** under §2.2;
(d) **expiry of a fixed term** if one was set, without reappointment;
(e) **dissolution** if the Trustee is a body corporate.

**6.2 Incapacity `[PROPOSED]` `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`.** For these rules, a Trustee is treated as
lacking capacity to act if **any** of the following is evidenced:

(a) a medical practitioner's written opinion that the Trustee lacks capacity to manage their own
    property and affairs, addressed to the Trustees;
(b) a court order or appointment of a guardian, manager or substitute decision-maker in respect of the
    Trustee's property;
(c) the Trustee's persistent inability, **[PERIOD]** or longer, to discharge the office and to
    participate in decisions, verified by a written resolution of the other Trustees;
(d) a determination under applicable law that has equivalent effect.

**6.3 Who decides, and what is required.**

| Step | Who | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Receive the evidence of incapacity | Any Trustee or the Protector | The document itself (medical opinion, court order, or the verified participation record) |
| Declare the office vacant | The remaining Trustees by written decision (or the Protector if none remain) | The evidence, a record of the decision, and confirmation that the affected Trustee (or their representative) was notified |
| Protect the Trust in the interim | The remaining Trustees | Emergency action under `D56`/`D62` if necessary, recorded and reported |
| Appoint a replacement | Per §3 | Same procedure as an ordinary vacancy, expedited where the Trust would otherwise fall below minimum numbers |
| Notify | Company Secretary | Regulator (if Route 2), banks/custodians, insurers, and any counterparty relying on the Trustee's signature |

**6.4 No software determination.** Incapacity is **never** inferred by a software system, a login
record, an inactivity flag, an audit-log gap or an AI output. Those may be **evidence collected**, but
the declaration requires the human process in §6.3.

**6.5 Interim protection of the Trust Fund.** Pending replacement, the remaining Trustees may take only
the acts necessary to preserve the Trust Fund and comply with law, and must record each act with
reasons and report to the Protector (or, if none, to the adult Beneficiaries) within **[NUMBER]** days.

## 7. Maintaining continuity — the "never below minimum" rule

**7.1 Standing monitoring (mandatory practice).** The Company Secretary function shall maintain a
**Trustee count and qualification dashboard** showing: number of Trustees in office; number
ordinarily resident in **[JURISDICTION]** (if applicable); number independent; term expiry dates;
outstanding regulator filings. It is reviewed at every trustee meeting.

**7.2 Escalation ladder `[PROPOSED]`.**

| Trigger | Escalate to | Time-box | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vacancy leaves the Trust at the minimum number | Protector / remaining Trustees | **[NUMBER]** days | Expedited appointment |
| Vacancy leaves the Trust **below** the minimum number | Protector + all adult Beneficiaries notified | Immediately | Emergency appointment within **[NUMBER]** days; regulator notified; consider legal advice on validity of acts |
| Residency minimum breached (Route 2) | Protector | Immediately | Appoint a resident Trustee; regulator notified; **`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` consequences of non-compliance** |
| No Trustee able to act | Protector, then the court | Immediately | Application to court for directions/appointment — **`[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]` the correct procedure** |
| Protector's office vacant and urgent | Remaining Trustees | Immediately | §3.1 emergency appointment, ratified by the next Protector |

**7.3 Acts during a vacancy deficit.** Any act taken while the Trust is below its minimum number of
Trustees may be **invalid or voidable**. Such acts must be listed in a register, reported to the
Protector and counsel, and (if the law permits) ratified by the properly constituted trustee body
once restored. **The pack does not assert that ratification is effective — `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`.**

## 8. Protector rules (see also `D02` §17)

**8.1 Appointment/removal.** The Protector is appointed and removed by **[APPOINTING POWER — USER TO
SUPPLY]** (default proposal: the Settlor during lifetime, then the Trustees with the consent of all
adult Beneficiaries). Removal requires written notice, a stated ground and a **replacement appointed
simultaneously** so that the office is never vacant.

**8.2 Independence.** The Protector must not be a Beneficiary who benefits materially from a decision
they consent to, an employee of the Trust or a group entity, or a person who has been disqualified
under §2.2. If the Protector does benefit, they must declare it and the matter must be handled as a
conflict under `D04`.

**8.3 Ceiling on the Protector's powers `[PROPOSED]` `[COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]`.** The Protector may not:
(a) direct a distribution to a named beneficiary; (b) become the Trust's investment manager;
(c) hold Trust assets; (d) amend the instrument; (e) act as a Trustee by another name; or (f) be an
artificial-intelligence system.

**8.4 Successor Protector.** A written instrument naming a successor Protector (or a mechanism for
appointing one) is **strongly recommended** and its absence is recorded as a continuity risk in
`D61`. **It must be held with the executed instruments in `D74` — not only in software.**

## 9. Records, evidence and controls

**9.1 Registers to be updated on every event in this document.**

| Event | Registers updated | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Appointment | `D66`, `D73`, `D74` (+ `D67` if signatory), `D71` (if insured/bonded) | Deed of appointment, consent to act, screening record, Protector consent |
| Resignation | `D66`, `D73`, `D74` | Written notice, handover certificate, final account |
| Removal | `D66`, `D73`, `D74`, `D71` (if a claim arises) | Notice, response, reasoned decision, regulator acknowledgement |
| Incapacity/death | `D66`, `D73`, `D74` | Medical opinion/court order/death certificate; declaration of vacancy |
| Residency change | `D66`, `D68` | Evidence of residency; regulator filing (Route 2) |
| Term expiry | `D68`, `D66` | Diary entry; reappointment or vacancy |

**9.2 Evidence standard.** A register entry must be supported by the **underlying executed document or
official record**. A register entry alone is **not** evidence of office. Screenshots of a control-plane
UI are **not** evidence of office.

**9.3 Access classification.** Trustee records: **`HIGHLY_RESTRICTED`**. Access limited to: Trustees,
Protector, Company Secretary function, and the auditor on a scoped basis. Never placed in a public or
open repository (`D75` §Custody).

**9.4 Retention.** Retain for the life of the Trust **plus [PERIOD]**, subject to legal hold. Never
destroy an original instrument because a copy exists in software.

**9.5 Separation of duties `[PROPOSED]`.** The person who records a trustee change must not be the
person who approves it. No single person may nominate, approve and record the same appointment.

**9.6 No self-dealing through process.** The Company Secretary function, the Family Office, the Family
Council, and any software system may **prepare** documents and records, but may not exercise any power
in this document.

## 10. Interaction with the BEYU control plane (read carefully)

**10.1** The BEYU OS control plane contains an `entity_appointments` model that records trustees,
protectors, directors and officers, and governance models for bodies, memberships, charters, meetings
and resolutions. **These are records.** They are useful because they make the human decision
**auditable and traceable**. They do **not** create office.

**10.2** Where a person holds office under this document:
(a) the **source of office** is the executed instrument;
(b) the **control-plane record** must reference that instrument (`document_ref`) and must not be
    presented as the appointment;
(c) a **software role grant** (e.g. a Trustee or Family Office role) is an access entitlement only and
    confers **no** fiduciary power;
(d) `trust_provisions` default to **`INERT`** and `enforceability_assumed` to **`false`** — do not
    load any provision here in a way that implies enforceability;
(e) revocation of access never removes office, and removal from office must be followed by revocation
    of access through the normal governed process.

**10.3 Forbidden practices.** (a) Creating a Trustee by inserting a database row or assigning a role;
(b) treating an audit-log entry as evidence of appointment; (c) allowing a workflow's "APPROVED" state
to be described as a trustee appointment; (d) recording an AI-generated document as a trustee
instrument.
