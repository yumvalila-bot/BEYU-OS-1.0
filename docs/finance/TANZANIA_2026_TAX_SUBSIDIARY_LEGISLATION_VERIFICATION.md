## GN 158F — forest-produce single-instalment regulations

### Statutory parent law cross-check

The official OAG MIS PDF for Finance Act No. 2 of 2026 is:
https://oagmis.oag.go.tz/portal/acts/337/download

The PDF is a 57-page official Act Supplement to the Special Gazette of the United Republic of Tanzania No. 8, Vol. 107, dated 30 June 2026. Section 25, printed pages 29–31 (PDF pages 28–30), repeals and replaces Income Tax Act section 116A. The text provides:

- A single instalment equal to 2% of gross payment.
- Payment at the earliest applicable statutory event: receipt of all or part of payment; produce about to be transported; parting with possession, use or control; or payment of forest cess under the Local Government Finance Act.
- Forest produce includes timber, logs, mirunda, poles and natural varnish including latex, resin, sap or gums.
- An instalment payer is a person other than a corporation who sells forest resources before harvest or after harvest as forest produce.
- Gross payment is the greatest of farm-gate price, purchase price or the value determined by Tanzania Forest Service Agency.
- Farm-gate price is fair market value in the relevant local government authority's open market between independent parties.

This statutory parent text has been directly reviewed from the official OAG PDF. It is not the same thing as authenticating GN 158F.

The reproduced GN 158F text states that it was made under section 155 of the Income Tax Act, is dated 30 June 2026 and commences on 1 July 2026. All of these details remain subject to official Gazette matching.

### Temporal law and no-double-count safeguards

- Finance Act No. 11 of 2025, section 58 inserted the earlier section 116A, which was scheduled to commence on 1 January 2026.
- Finance Act No. 2 of 2026, section 25 repealed and replaced that section with effect from 1 July 2026.
- The 2026 Act retains the 2% amount but changes the trigger events and definitions. The earlier and replacement provisions must be separate effective-dated versions.
- The 2026 regulations commence on 1 July 2026 according to the reproduced GN 158F. Transactions spanning the 1 July boundary require transaction-date and transitional-law review.
- Forest cess under the Local Government Finance Act is one of the statutory trigger events in section 116A(2)(d). Do not treat it as a second income-tax charge or automatically add it to gross payment. Its separate legal basis, payment evidence and interaction must be modelled only after specialist review.

### GN 158B — new-trader presumptive relief

The reproduced text describes a 12-month income-tax relief application for a newly registered individual obtaining a TIN to commence a business for the first time, where projected annual turnover falls within the statutory presumptive regime and the business remains exclusively within that regime. It also describes:
- An electronic application with statutory declaration, business description and projected turnover.
- A seven-working-day Commissioner review/decision period.
- Anti-chaining criteria, including use of a spouse, dependent, child or nominee, and a similar trade at the same location within 180 days after cessation of the previous business.
- Non-transferability, an end-of-relief turnover declaration within 30 days before expiry, revocation grounds, show-cause procedure and objection rights.

The relief is not automatic. Reconcile the official regulation against the Finance Act 2026 First Schedule, the current presumptive turnover table, definitions and any transitional conditions before any taxpayer eligibility or deadline workflow is considered.

### GN 158E — excise for non-resident service providers

Professional analyses and a reproduced copy describe an administrative regime for non-resident providers of specified services delivered through the internet or other electronic means to Mainland Tanzania residents who are not registered or required to file returns under the Excise Act for the same service. The reported covered categories include electronic communication, pay-to-view television/streaming, betting/gaming/lottery advertising, and money transfer/payment services.

The reproduced and professional sources describe online registration, customer-location indicators, monthly electronic returns, payment by the seventh day of the following month, a 90-day transition for providers already operating at commencement, permitted convertible-currency payment and an EFD exemption. These details remain subject to official text matching. As at 12 November 2026, the reported 90-day transition period appears to have elapsed; confirm the exact statutory counting rule and any subsequent relief before determining that a provider is late. The charging provisions are in Excise Act section 126(4)–(5), inserted by Finance Act 2026 section 8; do not treat the regulations as the source of the charge or infer that the regime is VAT.

Before implementation, confirm:
- The precise services and definitions in the official regulations and Act.
- The statutory test for unregistered recipients and the reliability hierarchy for customer-location evidence.
- Registration timing, including the 90-day transition and its calculation.
- Return/payment deadlines, currencies and exchange-rate date.
- EFD exemption scope, error-correction procedure, penalties, interest and deregistration.
- Overlap and non-duplication with VAT on electronic services, withholding taxes and other levies.
